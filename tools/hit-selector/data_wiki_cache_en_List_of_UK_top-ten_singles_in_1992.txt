The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |accessdate=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1992 in British music|1992]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1991|1991]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1993|1993]] but were in the top 10 in 1992. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and forty-five singles were in the top ten in 1992. Ten singles from 1991 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Could It Be Magic#Take That version|Could It Be Magic]]" by [[Take That]] was released in 1992 but did not reach its peak until 1993. "[[Addams Groove]]" by [[MC Hammer]], "[[Don't Talk Just Kiss]]" by [[Right Said Fred]], "[[Justified & Ancient]]" by [[The KLF]] featuring [[Tammy Wynette]], "Roobarb and Custard" by [[Shaft (rave band)|Shaft]] and "[[Too Blind to See It (Kym Sims song)|Too Blind to See It]]" by [[Kym Sims]] were the singles from 1991 to reach their peak in 1992. Twenty-seven artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1992. [[Celine Dion]], [[East 17]], [[Kris Kross]], [[Manic Street Preachers]] and [[Take That]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1992.

The 1991 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Bohemian Rhapsody]]"/"[[These Are the Days of Our Lives]]" by [[Queen (band)|Queen]], remained at number-one for the first three weeks of 1992. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Goodnight Girl]]" by [[Wet Wet Wet]]. Overall, twelve different singles peaked at number-one in 1992, with twelve unique artists having singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and forty-five singles charted in the top 10 in 1992, with one-hundred and thirty-nine singles reaching their peak this year.

Twenty-seven artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1992. American singer [[Michael Jackson]] and [[dance music|dance group]] [[The Shamen]] shared the record for the most top ten singles in 1992 with four hit singles each. Jackson's highest-charting single of the year was "[[Heal the World]]", which peaked at number two in November. He reached the top 10 with a further three singles in 1992: "[[In the Closet]]", which charted at number eight; "[[Who Is It (Michael Jackson song)|Who Is It]]" (number 10) and "[[Remember the Time]]" (number three). The Shamen also had four top ten entries in 1992, with their highest-charting single, "[[Ebeneezer Goode]]", spending four weeks at number-one. Solo artists [[CeCe Peniston]], [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]] and [[Freddie Mercury]], and bands [[Erasure (duo)|Erasure]], [[Take That]] and [[Guns N' Roses]] each reached the top 10 on three occasions.

[[Shakespears Sister]] were one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Stay (Shakespears Sister song)|Stay]]". [[Annie Lennox]], [[The Cure]], [[George Michael]], [[Prince (entertainer)|Prince]] and [[U2]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1992.

===Chart debuts===
Forty-four artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1992, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, three went on to record another hit single that year: [[Curtis Stigers]], [[KWS (band)|KWS]] and [[Undercover (band)|Undercover]]. [[CeCe Peniston]] and [[Take That]] both had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Annie Lennox]] had prior chart success as a member of the duo [[Eurythmics]] but 1992 marked the start of her solo career, with "[[Why (Annie Lennox song)|Why]]" becoming her first top 10 hit at number five.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[Addams Groove]]" (from ''[[The Addams Family (1991 film)|The Addams Family]]''), [[God Gave Rock and Roll to You|God Gave Rock 'N' Roll to You II]] (''[[Bill & Ted's Bogus Journey]]''), "[[My Girl (The Temptations song)|My Girl]]" (''[[My Girl (film)|My Girl]]''), "[[Everything About You (Ugly Kid Joe song)|Everything About You]]" (''[[Wayne's World (film)|Wayne's World]]''), "[[Beauty and the Beast (Disney song)|Beauty and The Beast]]" (''[[Beauty and The Beast (1991 film)|Beauty and The Beast]]''), "[[This Used To Be My Playground]]" (''[[A League of Their Own]]''), "[[The Best Things in Life Are Free (Luther Vandross and Janet Jackson song)|The Best Things In Life Are Free]]" (''[[Mo' Money]]''), "[[End of the Road (Boyz II Men song)|End of the Road]]" (''[[Boomerang (1992 film)|Boomerang]]'') and "[[I Will Always Love You]]" and "[[Someday (I'm Coming Back)]]" (''[[The Bodyguard (1992 film)|The Bodyguard]]'').

===Charity singles===
The [[Comic Relief]] single for 1992, "[[Elected (song)|(I Want to Be) Elected]]", was recorded by [[Rowan Atkinson]], in character as his alter-ego [[Mr. Bean]], alongside [[Bruce Dickinson]] from the rock band [[Iron Maiden]]. It was additionally credited to ''Smear Campaign'' which consisted of members of the group [[Skin (British band)|Skin]] (known at the time as ''Taste''). The single peaked at number nine on 11 April 1992 (week ending).

A joint project by [[Manic Street Preachers]] and [[The Fatima Mansions]] raised money for [[Scope (charity)|The Spastics Society]]. The latter covered [[Bryan Adams]]' 1991 sixteen-week chart-topper "[[(Everything I Do) I Do It for You]]", with the formerdoing a version of [[Suicide Is Painless|Suicide Is Painless (Theme from M.A.S.H.)]]". The double A-side single peaked at number seven on 26 September 1992 (week ending).

===Best-selling singles===
[[Whitney Houston]] had the best-selling single of the year with a cover of "[[I Will Always Love You#Whitney Houston version|I Will Always Love You]]", from the soundtrack of the film ''[[The Bodyguard (1992 film)|The Bodyguard]]''. The single spent fifteen weeks in the top 10 (including ten weeks at number-one), sold over 970,000 copies and was certified 2× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Industry|BPI]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/music/rockandpopfeatures/6269926/Why-we-will-always-love-Whitney-Houston.html |title=Why we will always love Whitney Houston |work=[[The Daily Telegraph|Telegraph Online]] |date=7 October 2009 |accessdate=8 June 2010 | location=London | first=Helen | last=Brown}}</ref> "[[Rhythm Is a Dancer]]" by [[Snap!]] came in second place, selling over 675,000 copies and missing out by around 295,000 sales. [[Charles & Eddie]]'s "[[Would I Lie to You? (Charles & Eddie song)|Would I Lie to You?]]", "[[Stay (Shakespears Sister song)|Stay]]" from [[Shakespears Sister]] and "[[Please Don't Go (KC and the Sunshine Band song)#KWS version|Please Don't Go]]"/"Game Boy" by [[KWS (band)|KWS]] made up the top five. Singles by [[Boyz II Men]], [[Erasure (duo)|Erasure]], [[Jimmy Nail]], [[Michael Jackson]] and [[Wet Wet Wet]] were also in the [[1992 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

"I Will Always Love You" (10) also ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 1990s in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Magic|A|a}} "Could It Be Magic" reached its peak of number three on 9 January 1993 (week ending).
*{{note label|My Girl|B|b}} "My Girl" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 43 upon its initial release in 1965. It re-entered at number 92 in 1986. It was re-released as a single in 1992, following the November 1991 release of ''[[My Girl (film)|the film of the same name]]'', which featured the song. As a result, it made the UK top ten for the first time, peaking at number two.
*{{note label|Smile|C|c|}} "I Love Your Smile" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 55 upon its initial release in November 1991.
*{{note label|Must|D|d}} "It Must Be Love" originally peaked at number 4 upon its initial release in 1981. It was re-released as a single in 1992 to coincide with the release of the greatest hits album ''[[Divine Madness (Madness album)|Divine Madness]]''.
*{{note label|Finally|E|e}} "Finally" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 29 upon its initial release in October 1991.
*{{note label|Comic Relief|F|f}} Released as the official single for [[Comic Relief]]. 
*{{note label|Smear Campaign|G|g}} "(I Want to Be) Elected" was performed by [[Rowan Atkinson]] (in character as [[Mr. Bean]]) and [[Bruce Dickinson]] of [[Iron Maiden]], with ''Smear Campaign'' made up of members of the group [[Skin (British band)|Skin]] (at that point known as ''Taste'').
*{{note label|Sample|H|h}} "Raving I'm Raving" incorporated an unauthorised sample of the song "[[Walking in Memphis]]" and was subsequently banned from the charts.
*{{note label|Heartbeat|I|i}} Nick Berry's cover of "Heartbeat" was used as the theme tune for ''[[Heartbeat (British TV series)|the television series of the same name]]''.
*{{note label|The Orb|J|j}} "Blue Room" is the longest song ever to chart in the UK with a running time of 39:57. Earlier in the year, the Official Charts Company had extended the maximum song length for chart eligibility from 25 minutes to 40 minutes.
*{{note label|Sesame's Treet|K|k}} "Sesame's Treet" is a remix of the [[Can You Tell Me How to Get to Sesame Street?|theme song]] to the television series ''[[Sesame Street]]''.
*{{note label|Trumpton|L|l}} "A Trip to Trumpton" includes samples from the 1960s ''[[Trumpton|television series of the same name]]''.
*{{note label|Remix|M|m}} The original release of "Even Better Than the Real Thing" only charted at number 12 but the remix reached the top 10.
*{{note label|Real Thing|N|n}} "Even Better Than the Real Thing" was remixed by [[Paul Oakenfold]].
*{{note label|Barcelona|O|o}} "Barcelona" originally peaked at number 8 upon its initial release in 1987.
*{{note label|Charming|P|p}} "This Charming Man" originally peaked at number 25 upon its initial release in 1983.
*{{note label|Society|Q|q}} [[Manic Street Preachers]] and [[The Fatima Mansions]] covered "Theme from M.A.S.H." and "Everything I Do (I Do It For You)" respectively. It was released as a double A-side single to raise money for [[Scope (charity)|The Spastics Society]].
*{{note label|Simple Minds|R|r}} "Love Song" originally peaked at number 47 upon its initial release in 1981. "Alive and Kicking" originally peaked at number 7 upon its initial release in 1985. The two songs were re-issued together as a double A-sided single in 1992 to promote the compilation album ''[[Glittering Prize 81/92]]''.
*{{note label|Piece of My Heart|S|s}} "(Take a Little) Piece of My Heart" was first released in 1967. It was reissued after being used in a ''[[Levi's]]'' advertising campaign in 1992.
*{{note label|Love Like That|T|t}} "Who Needs Love (Like That)" originally peaked outside the top 10 at number 55 upon its initial release in 1985.
*{{note label|Temptation|U|u}} The original version of "Temptation" peaked at number 2 upon its release in 1983.
*{{note label|Queen|V|v}} Figure includes a top 10 hit with the group Queen.
*{{note label|1991|W|w}} Figure includes single that peaked in 1991.
*{{note label|1993|X|x}} Figure includes single that peaked in 1993.
*{{note label|Iron Maiden|Y|y}} Figure includes a top 10 hit with the group Iron Maiden.
*{{note label|Elected|Z|z}} Figure includes an appearance on the Comic Relief single "(I Want to Be) Elected".
*{{note label|1992 peak|AA|aa}} Figure includes single that first charted in 1991 but peaked in 1992.
