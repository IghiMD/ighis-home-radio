The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Since 2004 the chart has been based on the sales of both [[CD single|physical singles]] and [[music download|digital downloads]], with [[airplay]] figures excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref>  This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2006 in British music charts|2006]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2005|2005]] but were in the top 10 in 2006. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and sixty-nine singles were in the top ten in 2006. Nine singles from 2005 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year. "[[When I'm Gone (Eminem song)|When I'm Gone]]" by [[Eminem]] was the only song from 2005 to reach its peak in 2006.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/4574044.stm|title=X Factor's Shayne holds No 1 spot|date=1 January 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> Forty-two artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2006. [[Amy Winehouse]], [[Fall Out Boy]], [[Lily Allen]], [[Ne-Yo]] and [[Timbaland]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 2006.

The 2005 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[That's My Goal]]" by ''[[The X Factor (UK series 2)|2005 X Factor]]'' winner [[Shayne Ward]], remained at number-one for the first three weeks of 2006.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4615282.stm|title=X Factor star stays top of charts|date=15 January 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[When the Sun Goes Down (Arctic Monkeys song)|When the Sun Goes Down]]" by [[Arctic Monkeys]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/4637506.stm|title=Arctic Monkeys top charts again|date=22 January 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> Overall, twenty-five different singles peaked at number-one in 2006, with [[McFly]] (2) having the most singles hit that position.<ref name="McFly">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/5208294.stm|title=Unstoppable McFly top music chart|date=23 July 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref><ref name ="Robbie">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6097050.stm|title=Robbie Williams tops album chart|date=29 October 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref>

==Background==

===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and sixty-nine singles charted in 2006, with one-hundred and sixty singles reaching their peak this year (including the re-entries "[[Fairytale of New York]]", "[[Three Lions (song)|Three Lions]]", "[[You Got the Love]]" and "[[You Spin Me Round]]" which charted in previous years but reached peaks on their latest chart run).

Forty-two artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2006. American singer [[Nicole Scherzinger]] of [[The Pussycat Dolls]] had the most top ten singles in 2006 with five. Four of the singles were with her band, the exception being "[[Come to Me (Diddy song)|Come to Me]]", her debut solo single and a collaboration with [[P. Diddy]]. The single peaked at number 4 on 14 October 2006, its first week of release, and spent four weeks in the top 10 in total. The best-performing single with The Pussycat Dolls was "[[Stickwitu]]" which had peaked at number-one in 2005.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4519244.stm|title=Pussycat Dolls stay at number on|date=11 December 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Beep (The Pussycat Dolls song)|Beep]]" reached a high of number 2,<ref name="Beep">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4776746.stm|title=X Factor's Chico takes top spot|date=6 March 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Buttons (The Pussycat Dolls song)|Buttons]]" landed one place lower and "[[I Don't Need a Man]]" was at number seven.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/newsbeat-32059048|title=Zayn Malik's quit - how successful will One Direction be now?|last=Rosney|first=Daniel|date=25 March 2015|work=Newsbeat|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref>

Five artists made the top 10 on four occasions in 2006, including The Pussycat Dolls as a group. British girlband [[Girls Aloud]]'s tally again included a holdover from 2005, their number 9 hit "[[See the Day]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4559230.stm|title=X Factor star in festive top spot|date=25 December 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Whole Lotta History]]" went to number six in March, "[[Something Kinda Oooh]]" was a top 3 entry in October,<ref name ="Robbie" /> while the group finished the year with their number four cover of "[[I Think We're Alone Now#Girls Aloud version|I Think We're Alone Now]]", best known as a hit for [[Tiffany Darwish|Tiffany]] in 1987.<ref name ="Leona">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6208115.stm|title=X Factor's Leona has festive No 1|date=25 December 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref>

[[Madonna (singer)|Madonna]] had another year of chart success. "[[Hung Up]]" had topped the rankings in 2005 but she also had three new entries in 2006.<ref name ="Hung Up">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4476206.stm|title=Madonna holds on to chart double|date=27 November 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Sorry (Madonna song)|Sorry]]" made number-one for a single week on 4 March 2006,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4760162.stm|title=X Factor's Chico heading to top|date=28 February 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Get Together (Madonna song)|Get Together]]" reached number seven in August and "[[Jump (Madonna song)|Jump]]" squeezed in at number 9 in November.<ref name = "Jump">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6141664.stm|title=Westlife score 14th UK number one|date=12 November 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref>

[[Fergie (singer)|Fergie]] and [[will.i.am]] of [[Black Eyed Peas]] were the final artists with four top 10 hits this year. will.i.am guested on The Pussycat Dolls' "Buttons", while the other three singles were as part of the band. "[[My Humps]]"<ref name ="Hung Up" /> and "[[Pump It]]" both reached number three, and they also got together with [[Sergio Mendes]] for a new interpretation of his song [[Mas Que Nada]], taking it to number 6 in July.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/5115114.stm|title=Furtado holds on to singles chart|date=25 June 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> Fergie's other hit single was her solo debut "[[London Bridge (Fergie song)|London Bridge]]", which peaked at number three.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/putting-pop-on-the-map-songs-inspired-by-places-in-the-uk__17754/|title=Putting pop on the map: Songs inspired by places in the UK|last=White|first=Jack|date=13 January 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=14 August 2017}}</ref>

Boyband [[McFly]] were one of a number of artists with three top-ten entries, including number-one singles "[[Star Girl (song)|Star Girl]]"<ref name = "Robbie" /> and [[Don't Stop Me Now#McFly version|"Don't Stop Me Now"/"Please, Please"]] (for [[Sport Relief]]).<ref name = "McFly" /> [[Christina Aguilera]], [[Beyoncé]], The Black Eyed Peas, [[The Feeling]], [[Nelly Furtado]], [[The Ordinary Boys]], [[P. Diddy]], [[Pink (singer)|Pink]] and [[Sugababes]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 2006.

===Chart debuts===
Sixty-two artists achieved their first top 10 single in 2006, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, ten went on to record another hit single that year: [[Beatfreakz]], [[Fall Out Boy]], [[The Fratellis]], [[Gnarls Barkley]], [[James Morrison (singer)|James Morrison]], [[The Kooks]], [[Lily Allen]], [[Ne-Yo]], [[Sunblock (band)|Sunblock]] and [[The Zutons]]. [[The Feeling]] and [[The Ordinary Boys]] both had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top 10 in the charts in 2006. The [[Sport Relief]] single was the double-A side "[[Don't Stop Me Now#McFly version|Don't Stop Me Now]]" (a cover of the [[Queen (band)|Queen]] song) and "[[Don't Stop Me Now#McFly version|Please, Please]]" by [[McFly]], peaking at number on one 29 July 2006.<ref name = "McFly" />

[[Emma Bunton]] recorded the Children in Need single for 2006, a cover of the [[Petula Clark]] song "[[Downtown (Petula Clark song)#Emma Bunton version|Downtown]]". It was the eighth solo top 10 single of her career and reached number three on 2 December 2006.<ref name="Take That">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6185994.stm|title=Take That claim number one single|date=27 November 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref>

[[U2]] and [[Green Day]] produced the single "[[The Saints Are Coming]]", in aid of relief efforts following [[Hurricane Katrina]], which caused destruction and devastation especially in [[New Orleans]] but also across the United States. It peaked at number two on its second week of release, on 18 November 2006.<ref name ="Jump" /><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-us-canada-34100538|title=Hurricane Katrina: New Orleans marks 10 years since disaster|date=30 August 2015|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=10 May 2017}}</ref>

===Best-selling singles===
[[Gnarls Barkley]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Crazy (Gnarls Barkley song)|Crazy]]". The song spent eleven weeks in the top 10 (including nine weeks at number one), sold about 820,000 copies and was certified by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref name ="Barkley" /> "[[A Moment Like This]]" by [[Leona Lewis]] came in second place, selling more than 700,000 copies and losing out by around 120,000 sales.<ref name="Leona"/> [[Shakira]] featuring [[Wyclef Jean]]'s "[[Hips Don't Lie]]",<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4788953.stm|title=Shakira holds firm at number one|date=13 August 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> "[[I Don't Feel Like Dancin'|I Don't Feel Like Dancin{{'-}}]]" from [[Scissor Sisters]]<ref name ="Scissors" /> and "[[I Wish I Was a Punk Rocker (With Flowers in My Hair)]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/5046316.stm|title=Thom curtails Crazy chart reign|date=4 June 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> by [[Sandi Thom]] made up the top five. Singles by [[Infernal (Danish band)|Infernal]],<ref name = "Barkley" /> [[Nelly Furtado]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/5115114.stm|title=Furtados holds on to singles chart|date=25 June 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> [[Take That]],<ref name = "Take That" /> [[Rihanna]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4936810.stm|title=X Factor star tops album charts|date=23 April 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> and [[Justin Timberlake]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/5354300.stm|title=Timberlake takes album top spot|date=17 September 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=10 May 2017}}</ref> were also in the [[2006 in British music charts#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/radio1/chart/top40_2006.shtml |title=The Official UK Singles Chart of the year: 2006 |work=BBC Radio 1 |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |accessdate=10 May 2017 |url-status=bot: unknown |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070329010801/http://www.bbc.co.uk/radio1/chart/top40_2006.shtml |archivedate=29 March 2007 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6225447.stm|title=Downloads send year's chart Crazy|date=2 January 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref>

The song re-entered the top 10 at number 5 on 11 February 2006 (week ending) for three weeks after it was re-released following [[Pete Burns]]' appearance on ''[[Celebrity Big Brother (British TV series)|Celebrity Big Brother]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/pete-burns-dead-or-alive-dies_uk_580e46afe4b0fce107d17a85|title=Pete Burns Dead: ‘You Spin Me Round’ Singer Dies, Aged 57|last=Welsh|first=Daniel|date=24 October 2016|work=Huffington Post|accessdate=14 August 2017}}</ref>
*{{note label|Source|D|d}} The original solo version of "You Got the Love" by Candi Staton peaked at number 95 in 1986. The remixed version by The Source peaked at number 4 for two weeks in 1991. A 1997 remix by New Voyager saw the song reach a new peak of number 3.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/search/singles/you%20got%20the%20love/|title=You Got the Love - full Official Chart History|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=14 August 2017}}</ref>
