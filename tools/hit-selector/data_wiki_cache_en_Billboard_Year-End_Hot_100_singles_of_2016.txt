[[File:Justin Bieber in 2015.jpg|thumb|190px|"[[Love Yourself]]" by [[Justin Bieber]] came in at number one, spending a total of two nonconsecutive weeks at the top position of the ''Billboard'' Hot 100 during 2016.  "[[Sorry (Justin Bieber song)|Sorry]]", which also topped the weekly chart, is ranked at number two on the year-end list.  Bieber is only the third artist to have two songs rank in the top-two of the Year-End Hot 100, after [[the Beatles]] in 1964 and [[Usher (musician)|Usher]] in 2004.<ref name="yearend">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/pro/adele-top-billboard-artist-2016-justin-bieber/|title=The Year in Charts 2016: Adele Rules as Top Artist (Again!), Justin Bieber Leads Hot 100|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]]|access-date=December 8, 2016|date=December 8, 2016|first= Keith|last= Caulfield}}</ref>]]
The [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] is a chart that ranks the best-performing [[single (music)|singles]] of the United States. Its data, published by ''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' magazine and compiled by [[Nielsen SoundScan]], is based collectively on each single's weekly [[CD single|physical]] and [[Digital distribution|digital]] sales, as well as [[Heavy rotation|airplay]] and [[streaming media|streaming]]. At the end of a year, ''Billboard'' will publish an annual list of the 100 most successful songs throughout that year on the Hot 100 chart based on the information. For 2016, the list was published on December 8, calculated with data from December 5, 2015 to November 26, 2016. The 2016 list was dominated by [[Justin Bieber]] and [[Drake (musician)|Drake]], who shared the top four spots, marking the first time two artists took up the top four spots since [[Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 2009|2009]] with [[Lady Gaga]] and [[The Black Eyed Peas]].<ref name="yearend" />

The list is also notable for being one of five ''Billboard'' Year-End lists that featured 14 songs that appeared in the previous year (in this case [[Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2015|2015]]'s) repeat onto to this list. The highest being [[Adele]]'s "[[Hello (Adele song)|Hello]]", which with only three weeks being available to count in 2015's chart year, made it on to 2015's list at number 35 and repeat higher at number 7 in 2016's. Only four other year-end lists would repeat the same feat, those being [[Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_1997|1997]], [[Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2010|2010]], [[Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2018|2018]] and [[Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2022|2022]].
  
For the first time [[Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 2006|in 10 years]], the year-end number-one single was #1 for five weeks or fewer.

His highest-ranking song on the list "[[One Dance]]" (at number 3) featuring [[Wizkid (musician)|Wizkid]] and [[Kyla (British singer)|Kyla]] became his first number-one song on the ''Billboard'' Hot 100 as a lead artist.]]

[[File:Rihanna concert in Washington DC (2).jpg|thumb|190px|[[Rihanna]] has four songs in the top 30 including "[[Work (Rihanna song)|Work]]" at number 4, "[[Needed Me]]" at number 13, "[[This Is What You Came For]]" at number 17 and "[[Too Good]]" at number 29.]]
[[File:Twentyone Pilots at Press conference of Soundbox in Bangkok.JPG|thumb|190px|[[Twenty One Pilots]] has three songs in the top 25 with "[[Stressed Out]]" at number 5, "[[Ride (Twenty One Pilots song)|Ride]]" at number 20, and "[[Heathens (Twenty One Pilots song)|Heathens]]" at number 21.]]
