[[File:Britney Spears.jpg|thumb|right|200px|[[Britney Spears]] made her arrival into the UK charts in 1999, scoring three top 10 entries, including her debut hit "[[...Baby One More Time]]", which spent two weeks at number-one and became the best-selling single of the year.]]
[[File:Eiffel 65 (cropped).jpg|thumb|right|200px|Italian group [[Eiffel 65]] achieved the second best selling single of the year with "[[Blue (Da Ba Dee)]]", which spent three weeks at number-one.]]
[[File:Cliff Richard Brussels.jpg|thumb|right|200px|[[Cliff Richard]] secured his fourteenth UK number-one single in 1999 with "[[The Millennium Prayer]]", which topped the chart for three weeks and became the year's third best selling single.]]
[[File:Lou Bega.jpg|thumb|right|200px|German singer [[Lou Bega]] had the fourth best selling single of the year with "[[Mambo No. 5#Lou Bega version|Mambo No. 5 (A Little Bit Of...)]]", which stayed at number-one for two weeks.]]
The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1999 in British music|1999]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1998|1998]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2000|2000]] but were in the top 10 in 1999. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Two hundred and three singles were in the top ten in 1999. Nine singles from 1998 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Mr. Hankey, the Christmas Poo]]" by [[List of recurring South Park characters#Mr. Hankey|Mr. Hankey]], "[[Say You'll Be Mine (Steps song)|Say You'll Be Mine]]"/"[[Better the Devil You Know#Steps version|Better the Devil You Know]]" by [[Steps (pop group)|Steps]] and "[[Two in a Million]]"/"[[You're My Number One]]" by [[S Club 7]] were all released in 1999 but did not reach their peak until 2000. "[[Chocolate Salty Balls]]" by [[Chef (South Park)|Chef]] and "[[Heartbeat (Steps song)|Heartbeat]]"/"[[Tragedy (Bee Gees song)#Steps version|Tragedy]]" by [[Steps (pop group)|Steps]] were the singles from 1998 to reach their peak in 1999. Forty-eight artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1999. [[Christina Aguilera]], [[Britney Spears]], [[Eminem]], [[Jennifer Lopez]], [[S Club 7]] and [[Westlife]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1999.

The first number-one single of the year was "[[Chocolate Salty Balls]]" by [[Chef (South Park)|Chef]], the ''[[South Park]]'' character voiced by [[Isaac Hayes]]. Overall, thirty-six different singles peaked at number-one in 1999, with [[Westlife]] (4) having the most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
Two-hundred and three singles charted in the top 10 in 1999, with one-hundred and ninety-three singles reaching their peak this year.

Forty-eight artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1999. [[Steps (pop group)|Steps]] had the most top 10 singles in 1999 with seven entries. Seven artists recorded four singles which reached the top 10 this year: [[Another Level (band)|Another Level]], [[B*Witched]], [[Melanie C]], [[Ronan Keating]], [[Vengaboys]], [[Westlife]] and [[Will Smith]].

[[Britney Spears]] was one of nine artists with three top 10 entries, including the number-one single "[[...Baby One More Time]]". [[A1 (band)|A1]], [[Boyzone]], [[Geri Halliwell]], [[Honeyz]], [[Lolly (singer)|Lolly]], [[Martine McCutcheon]], [[S Club 7]] and [[TQ (singer)|TQ]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1999.

===Chart debuts===
Seventy-three artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1999, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, twelve went on to record another hit single that year: [[Alice DeeJay]], [[ATB (DJ)|ATB]], [[Basement Jaxx]], [[Cartoons (band)|Cartoons]], [[Eminem]], [[Glamma Kid]], [[Jennifer Lopez]], [[NSYNC]], [[The Offspring]], [[Phats & Small]], [[Shanks & Bigfoot]], [[Travis (band)|Travis]], [[A1 (band)|A1]], [[Britney Spears]], [[Lolly (singer)|Lolly]], [[Martine McCutcheon]], [[S Club 7]] and [[TQ (singer)|TQ]] all had two more top 10 singles in 1999. [[Westlife]] had three other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Geri Halliwell]] left [[Spice Girls]] in 1999 and recorded her debut solo single, "[[Look at Me (Geri Halliwell song)|Look at Me]]", peaking at number 2. She also had two further entries this year - "[[Mi Chico Latino]]" and "[[Lift Me Up (Geri Halliwell song)|Lift Me Up]]" both topped the chart. Her former bandmate [[Emma Bunton]] lent her talents to [[Tin Tin Out]]'s "[[What I Am#Tin Tin Out and Emma Bunton version|What I Am]]", steering the song to number two. [[Maire Brennan]] was in the line-up of [[Clannad]] who scored their first and only top 10 single in 1981 with "[[Theme from Harry's Game|Theme from ''Harry's Game'']]". She appeared on Chicane's "Saltwater" as a featured artist in 1999.

"[[When You Say Nothing at All]]" was [[Ronan Keating]]'s first chart entry separate from [[Boyzone]], who he also had three hit singles with this year. Shanks & Bigfoot were originally known as Doolally. "Straight from the Heart" peaked at number 20 under this name in 1998 but its re-release made the top 10 in 1999. [[Jordan Knight]] of [[New Kids on the Block]] kicked off his solo career in 1999 with his sole top 10 hit, "[[Give It to You (Jordan Knight song)|Give It to You]]", making number five.

[[Oasis (band)|Oasis]] singer [[Liam Gallagher]] and [[Steve Cradock]] from [[Ocean Colour Scene]] both appeared on Buffalo Tom's "Going Underground"/"Carnation". In both cases it was their first recording independent of their bands to make the top 10.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[From the Heart (Another Level song)|From the Heart]]" and "[[When You Say Nothing at All]]" (from ''[[Notting Hill (film)|Notting Hill]]'') and "[[Wild Wild West (Will Smith song)|Wild Wild West]]" (''[[Wild Wild West]]'').

===Best-selling singles===
[[Britney Spears]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[...Baby One More Time]]". The song spent weeks in the top 10 (including weeks at number one), sold over 1.445 million copies and was certified 2× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]]. "[[Blue (Da Ba Dee)]]" by [[Eiffel 65]] came in second place, selling more than 956,000 copies and losing out by around 489,000 sales. [[Cliff Richard]]'s "[[The Millennium Prayer]]", "[[Mambo No. 5|Mambo No. 5 (A Little Bit of...)]]" from [[Lou Bega]] and "[[9PM (Till I Come)]]" by [[ATB (DJ)|ATB]] made up the top five. Singles by [[Ricky Martin]], [[Shania Twain]], [[Shanks & Bigfoot]], [[Mr. Oizo]] and [[Christina Aguilera]] were also in the [[1999 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

"...Baby One More Time" (8) also ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 1990s in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Entries by artist==
[[File:Westlife 2011 2.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Irish boy band [[Westlife]] (pictured in 2011 without original member [[Brian McFadden]]) made their UK chart debut in 1999 by scoring five consecutive number-one hits: "[[Swear It Again]]", "[[If I Let You Go]]", fool again"[[Flying Without Wings]]" and "[[I Have a Dream (song)#Westlife version|I Have a Dream]]"/"[[Seasons in the Sun]]".]]
[[File:RonanKeating.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Ronan Keating]] made the UK top 10 four times in 1999. With [[Boyzone]], he reached number-one with "[[When the Going Gets Tough, the Tough Get Going (song)#Boyzone version|When the Going Gets Tough]]" and "[[You Needed Me#Boyzone version|You Needed Me]]", while he topped the chart on his own with his first solo single, a cover of "[[When You Say Nothing at All]]", which featured on the soundtrack of the movie ''[[Notting Hill (film)|Notting Hill]]''.]]
[[File:Quentin Dupieux.jpg|thumb|right|150px|French DJ and musician [[Mr. Oizo]] secured a UK number-one hit this year with his instrumental "[[Flat Beat]]", which achieved success after featuring in an advert for clothing company [[Levi Strauss & Co.|Levi's]].]]
[[File:Baz Luhrmann Cannes 2013.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Australian film director and music producer [[Baz Luhrmann]] reached number-one in June 1999 with his [[spoken word]] song "[[Wear Sunscreen#Baz Luhrmann version|Everybody's Free (To Wear Sunscreen)]]", with uncredited narration provided by Australian actor [[Lee Perry (voice actor)|Lee Perry]]. ]]

*{{note label|Two in a Million|A|a}} "Two in a Million"/"You're My Number One" reached its peak of number two on 8 January 2000.
*{{note label|Mr. Hankey|B|b}} "Mr. Hankey, the Christmas Poo" reached its peak of number four on 1 January 2000.
*{{note label|Miami|C|c}} "Miami" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 2 January 1999 (week ending).
*{{note label|Children in Need 1998|D|d}} Released as the official single for [[Children in Need]] in 1998.
*{{note label|Prince 1999|E|e}} "1999" originally peaked outside the top 10 at number 25 upon its initial release in 1983. It was re-released in 1985, and made the top 10 for the first time, peaking at number 2.
*{{note label|Comic Relief|F|f}} Released as the official single for [[Comic Relief]].
*{{note label|No Scrubs|G|g}} "No Scrubs" re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 17 April 1999 (week ending).
*{{note label|Thank ABBA for the Music|H|h}} "Thank ABBA for the Music" was a medley of [[ABBA]] songs first performed at the [[1999 BRIT Awards]] to celebrate the release of the ''[[Mamma Mia (musical)|Mamma Mia]]'' musical. It featured the groups B*Witched, Cleopatra and Steps and solo artists Billie and Tina Cousins.
*{{note label|Say It Again|I|i}} "Say It Again" was the [[United Kingdom in the Eurovision Song Contest 1999|United Kingdom]]'s entry at the [[Eurovision Song Contest]] in [[Eurovision Song Contest 1999|1999]].
*{{note label|My Love|J|j}} "My Love Is Your Love" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 21 August 1999 (week ending).
*{{note label|Straight from the Heart|K|k}} "Straight From the Heart" originally peaked at number 20 upon its initial release in 1998.
*{{note label|Shanks & Bigfoot|L|l}} "Straight from the Heart" is credited to Doolally, the original name by which Shanks & Bigfoot were known. "Sweet Like Chocolate" entered the top 10 under their new name.
*{{note label|Back in My Life|M|m}} "Back In My Life" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 1 January 2000 (week ending).
*{{note label|Children in Need|N|n}} Released as the official single for Children in Need.
*{{note label|Barber's Adagio|O|o}} "Barber's Adagio For Strings" re-entered the top 10 at number 7 on 15 January 2000 (week ending).
*{{note label|Steal My Sunshine|P|p}} "Steal My Sunshine" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 15 January 2000 (week ending).
*{{note label|Imagine|Q|q}} "Imagine" was first released as a single in the UK in 1975 and made the top 10, peaking at number 6. Following John Lennon's death in December 1980, it re-entered the top 10 and spent four weeks at number one in January 1981.
