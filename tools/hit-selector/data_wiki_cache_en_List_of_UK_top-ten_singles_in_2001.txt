[[File:DJÖtzi Das Beste der Feste1.jpg|thumb|right|200px|Austrian musician [[DJ Ötzi]] (pictured in 2016) reached number-one in the UK in September 2001 with "[[Hey Baby (Uhh, Ahh)]]", a cover version of the [[Bruce Channel]] song "[[Hey! DJ Ötzi had a second top 10 hit later in the year with his cover of "[[Do Wah Diddy Diddy]]", which reached number nine.]]
[[File:Nobel Peace Prize Concert 2009 Westlife2.jpg|thumb|right|200px|Irish boy band [[Westlife]] (original member [[Brian McFadden]] not pictured) were responsible for this year's [[Comic Relief]] single, a reworking of [[Billy Joel]]'s "[[Uptown Girl]]" which reached number-one in March. The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of [[CD single|physical]] singles with [[airplay]] figures and [[Music download|digital downloads]] excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2001 in British music charts|2001]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2000|2000]] but were in the top 10 in 2001. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Two hundred singles were in the top ten in 2001. Ten singles from 2000 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year. Seventy-three artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2001. [[Blue (English band)|Blue]], [[Daniel Bedingfield]], [[Linkin Park]], [[Nelly Furtado]], [[Sophie Ellis-Bextor]] and [[Outkast]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 2001.

The 2000 [[Christmas number-one]], "[[Can We Fix It?]]" by [[List of Bob the Builder characters#Humans|Bob the Builder]] (voiced by [[Neil Morrissey]]<ref name = "Neil Morrissey">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/323891.stm|title=Bob cements his TV success|date=20 April 1999|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> and the theme song from the [[Bob the Builder|children's television series]]), remained at number-one for the first week of 2001.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1106099.stm|title=Bob the Builder's chart reign ends|date=8 January 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=29 July 2017}}</ref><ref name = "Now 48">{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-39628619|title=What's so great about Now That's What I Call Music 48?|last=Savage|first=Mark|date=18 April 2017|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=29 July 2017}}</ref> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Touch Me (Rui da Silva song)|Touch Me]]" by [[Rui da Silva (DJ)|Rui da Silva]]. Overall, thirty-one different singles peaked at number-one in 2001, with [[Atomic Kitten]], [[Blue (English band)|Blue]], Bob the Builder, [[Robbie Williams]], [[S Club 7]], [[Shaggy (musician)|Shaggy]] and [[Westlife]] (all 2) having the most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
Two hundred singles charted in the top 10 in 2000, with one-hundred and ninety singles reaching their peak this year (including the re-entry "[[Set You Free (N-Trance song)|Set You Free]]" which charted in previous years but reached a peak on its latest chart run).

Seventy-three artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2001. [[Jennifer Lopez]] and the three members of [[Destiny's Child]] ([[Beyoncé]], [[Kelly Rowland]] and [[Michelle Williams (singer)|Michelle Williams]]) shared the record for most top 10 hits in 1986 with five hit singles each. Both of these artists totals included a guest spot on the charity single "[[What's Going On (song)|What's Going On]]" by Artists Against AIDS Worldwide (also known as [[All Star Tribute]]).<ref name = "Aids">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/showbiz/3281609.stm|title=The show goes on in Aids battle|date=24 November 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=29 July 2017}}</ref> American girl group [[Destiny's Child]] were one of two artists along with [[Steps (band)|Steps]] to have more than three singles reach the top 10. "[[Independent Women]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1043073.stm|title=UK chart-toppers mirror US|date=27 November 2000|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> and "[[Survivor (Destiny's Child song)|Survivor]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1291334.stm|title=Destiny's Child in at number one|date=22 April 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> both reached number-one in the UK. "[[Bootylicious]]" peaked at number two<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1463422.stm|title=Kittens win chart war|date=29 July 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> and "[[Emotion (Destiny's Child song)|Emotion]]" landed one place lower at number three.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1663408.stm|title=Blue stop Westlife's chart double|date=18 November 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> The group were to go on hiatus the following year, with [[Beyoncé]] and [[Kelly Rowland]] both launching solo careers, making this one of their last years together until they reformed.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1731952.stm|title=Destiny's Child split denied|date=28 December 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref>

[[Jennifer Lopez]] was the other artist with five top ten entries, including the charity single. "[[Love Don't Cost a Thing (song)|Love Don't Cost a Thing]]" scaled the chart to reach number one, while "[[Play (Jennifer Lopez song)|Play]]" and "[[Ain't It Funny]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1487555.stm|title=So Solid Crew top of the pops|date=12 August 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> reached number three and "[[I'm Real (Jennifer Lopez song)|I'm Real]]" ranked at number four.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/jennifer-lopezs-official-biggest-songs-in-the-uk-revealed__15657/|title=Jennifer Lopez's Official biggest songs in the UK revealed|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=24 July 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=30 July 2017}}</ref>

Steps were one of four acts who had four hit singles in 2001, the best of these both double-A side singles. [[Chain Reaction (Diana Ross song)#Steps version|"Chain Reaction"/"One for Sorrow (Tony Moran US Remix)"]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1571939.stm|title=Kylie holds top chart spot|date=30 September 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> and "[[It's the Way You Make Me Feel]]" peaked at number two. Of their other singles, "[[Here and Now / You'll Be Sorry]]" reached number 4 and "[[Words Are Not Enough (song)|Words Are Not Enough]]"/"[[I Know Him So Well#Steps version|I Know Him So Well]]" took fifth position on the chart.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/steps-biggest-selling-singles-revealed__18480/|title=Steps' biggest selling singles revealed|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=14 March 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=31 July 2017}}</ref>

[[Bono]] and [[The Edge]], both from the band [[U2]], also featured on "What's Going On" and recorded three top 10 singles with their band. "[[Stuck in a Moment You Can't Get Out Of]]" had the most chart success, peaking at number two in February.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/2017/apr/19/u2-10-of-the-best|title=U2 – 10 of the best|last=Simpson|first=Dave|date=19 April 2017|work=The Guardian|accessdate=31 July 2017}}</ref> "[[Elevation (song)|Elevation]]" reached number three in July<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1451760.stm|title=Robbie holds top spot|date=23 July 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=31 July 2017}}</ref> and their final hit of the year was "[[Walk On (U2 song)|Walk On]]", making number five in December.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1675740.stm|title=S Club and Robbie top charts|date=25 November 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=31 July 2017}}</ref> Finally, [[Usher (musician)|Usher]] made the top 10 on three occasions in 2001: the American singer took "[[Pop Ya Collar]]" to number 2, "[[U Remind Me]]" charted one place lower<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1416864.stm|title=Hear'Say top charts again|date=1 July 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=31 July 2017}}</ref> and "[[U Got It Bad]]" landed at number five in October.

[[Dido (singer)|Dido]] was one of a number of artists with 3 top-ten entries, including "[[Stan (song)|Stan]]", "[[Here with Me (Dido song)|Here with Me]]" and [[Thank You (Dido song)|Thank You]]". [[Blue (English band)|Blue]], [[Mis-Teeq]], [[Samantha Mumba]], [[Robbie Williams]] and [[Stereophonics]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 2001.

===Chart debuts===
Seventy-four artists achieved their first top 10 single in 2001, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, eleven went on to record another hit single that year: [[Alicia Keys]], [[BBMak]], [[D12]], [[Dido (singer)|Dido]] [[DJ Ötzi]], [[Gorillaz]], [[Ian Van Dahl]], [[Lil' Kim]], [[M.O.P.]], [[O-Town]], [[So Solid Crew]], [[Sophie Ellis-Bextor]] and [[Wheatus]]. [[Blue (English band)|Blue]], [[Eve (rapper)|Eve]], [[Hear'Say]], [[Mis-Teeq]] and [[Nelly Furtado]] all had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top 10 in the charts in 2001. The [[Comic Relief]] single was a cover of [[Billy Joel]]'s "[[Uptown Girl#Westlife version|Uptown]]" by [[Westlife]], peaking at number one on 17 March 2001.<ref name = "Comic Relief">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/the-official-biggest-selling-comic-relief-singles-revealed__18581/|title=The Official biggest selling Comic Relief singles revealed|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=24 March 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=30 July 2017}}</ref>

[[S Club 7]] recorded the [[Children in Need]] single for 2001, "[[Have You Ever]]". It was their fourth number-one single, reaching the top spot on 1 December 2001. Their song "[[Never Had a Dream Come True (S Club 7 song)|Never Had a Dream Come True]]" was the Children in Need single the [[List of UK top 10 singles in 2000|previous year]], peaking at number-one, and it remained in the chart for the early part of the year.<ref name = "CIN">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/the-official-biggest-selling-children-in-need-singles-revealed__17116/|title=The Official biggest selling Children In Need singles revealed|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=17 November 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=30 July 2017}}</ref>

A group of artists came together under the banner Artists Against AIDS Worldwide (also known as All Star Tribute) to produce a cover of [[Marvin Gaye]]'s "[[What's Going On (song)|What's Going On]]". This included [[Christina Aguilera]], [[Backstreet Boys]], [[Britney Spears]], [[Jennifer Lopez]], [[Nelly Furtado]] and [[Wyclef Jean]]. The song reached a high of number six on 17 November 2001.<ref name = "Aids" />

===Best-selling singles===
[[Shaggy (musician)|Shaggy]] had the best-selling single of the year with "It Wasn't Me". The song spent ten weeks in the top 10 and sold 1.15 million copies and was certified 2× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1202642.stm|title=Shaggy storms UK charts|date=5 March 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=29 July 2017}}</ref> "[[Pure and Simple (song)|Pure and Simple]]" by [[Hear'Say]] came in second place, selling 1.07 million copies and losing out by around 80,000 sales.<ref name = "Now 48" /><ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/1228195.stm|title=Hear'Say hit number one|date=18 March 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> [[Kylie Minogue]]'s "[[Can't Get You Out of My Head]]",<ref name = "Head">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/flashback-to-2001-victoria-beckham-battles-kylie-minogue-for-number-1__16446/|title=Flashback to 2001: Victoria Beckham battles Kylie Minogue for Number 1|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=29 September 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=30 July 2017}}</ref> "[[Whole Again]]" from [[Atomic Kitten]]<ref name = "Kittens">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/1178208.stm|title=Kittens still purring at top spot|date=19 February 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=29 July 2017}}</ref> and "[[Hey! Baby]]" by [[DJ Ötzi]] made up the top five.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1547707.stm|title=Euro DJ ends Bob's chart reign|date=16 September 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> Singles by [[Westlife]],<ref name = "Comic Relief" /> [[S Club 7]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1332522.stm|title=REM lead Geri in albums race|date=15 May 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> Shaggy featuring Rayvon ("[[Angel (Shaggy song)|Angel]]"),<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1367493.stm|title=Shaggy's double whammy|date=3 June 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=29 July 2017}}</ref> [[Wheatus]]<ref name = "Kittens" /> and [[Afroman]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1612513.stm|title=Afroman hits High spot|date=21 October 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=30 July 2017}}</ref> were also in the [[2001 in British music charts#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1730365.stm|title=Shaggy's single is UK's best-seller|date=27 December 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=29 July 2017}}</ref>

"It Wasn't Me" (4), "Can't Get You Out of My Head" (7) and "Pure and Simple" (9) were all ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 2000s (decade) in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Entries by artist==
{{see also|List of UK Singles Chart number ones of the 2000s#2001}}
The following table shows artists who achieved two or more top 10 entries in 2001, including singles that reached their peak in 2000. The figures include both main artists and featured artists, while appearances on ensemble charity records are also counted for each artist. The total number of weeks an artist spent in the top ten in 2001 is also shown.
[[File:The opening ceremony of the FIFA World Cup 2014 10 (cropped) 2.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Jennifer Lopez]] had five singles in the top 10 in 2001, notably reaching number-one in January with "[[Love Don't Cost a Thing (song)|Love Don't Cost a Thing]]".]]
[[File:13-06-09 RiP Limp Bizkit Fred Durst 1.JPG|thumb|right|150px|[[Fred Durst]] from [[Limp Bizkit]] had three top 10 entries in 2001, two of which were with his group. The most successful of his entries was [[Rollin' (Limp Bizkit song)|Rollin']], which hit number-one in January.]]
[[File:Blue (6100521867).jpg|thumb|right|150px|English boy band [[Blue (English band)|Blue]] landed three entries in the top 10 this year, including the number-ones "[[Too Close (Blue song)|Too Close]]" and "[[If You Come Back]]".]] 
[[File:The Supermen Lovers.jpg|thumb|right|150px|French composer and electronic music producer [[The Supermen Lovers]] reached number two in the UK in September 2001 with his single "[[Starlight (The Supermen Lovers song)|Starlight]]", which featured vocals from singer [[Mani Hoffman]].]]
[[File:Gordon+Haskell.jpg|thumb|right|150px|English singer-songwriter [[Gordon Haskell]], who had been an active musician since the 1960s, became a one-hit wonder in the [[UK Singles Chart]] in December of this year with his song "[[How Wonderful You Are]]", which peaked at number two.]]
[[File:Uncle Kracker entertains troops at Camp Striker DVIDS296050.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Another one-hit wonder who made the UK charts in 2001 was American county rock singer [[Uncle Kracker]], whose single "[[Follow Me (Uncle Kracker song)|Follow Me]]" peaked at number three and lasted four weeks in the top 10.]]
[[File:Faith Hill 2006 (cropped).jpg|thumb|right|150px|US country singer [[Faith Hill]] reached number three in the UK in June of this year with her single "[[There You'll Be]]", which featured on [[Pearl Harbor (film)#Soundtrack|the soundtrack]] of the movie ''[[Pearl Harbor (film)|Pearl Harbor]]''.]]
