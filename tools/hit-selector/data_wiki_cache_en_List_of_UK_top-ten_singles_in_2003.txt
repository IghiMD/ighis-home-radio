[[File:Gaudi arena.jpg|thumb|right|200px|alt=|[[t.A.T.u.]] had two UK top 10 singles in 2003, including "[[All the Things She Said]]", which was the first song by a Russian act to top the UK charts. Spending four weeks at number-one, it went on to become the year's sixth best selling single.]]
[[File:Kelly Osbourne (8695811620).jpg|thumb|right|200px|alt=|[[Kelly Osbourne]] and her father [[Ozzy Osbourne|Ozzy]] recorded a duet version of [[Black Sabbath]]'s 1972 song "[[Changes (Black Sabbath song)#Ozzy and Kelly Osbourne version|Changes]]" and released it as a single in December of this year. It entered the UK charts at number-one, became the seventh best-selling song of the year, and remains the only UK number-one single for either artist.]]
[[File:Seanpaul01.jpg|thumb|right|200px|alt=|Jamaican singer and rapper [[Sean Paul]] had the most top ten entries in 2003 with five in total. The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]]|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|edition=18th |publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the top ten of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2003 in British music charts|2003]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2002|2002]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2004|2004]] but were in the top ten in 2003. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top ten for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Two hundred and twenty-nine singles were in the top ten in 2003, the highest unique number of top 10 singles in a calendar year of all time. Eleven singles from 2002 remained in the top ten for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Hey Ya!]]" by [[Outkast]] was released in 2003 but did not reach its peak until 2004.<ref name = "Busted">{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/cbbcnews/hi/music/newsid_3275000/3275597.stm |title=Busted beat Britney to top spot |work=[[Newsround]] |publisher=[[BBC|British Broadcasting Corporation]] |date=16 November 2003 |access-date=18 February 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/cbbcnews/hi/music/newsid_3470000/3470833.stm |title=LMC Vs U2 still top singles chart |work=[[Newsround]] |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=8 February 2004 |access-date=18 February 2011}}</ref> "[[Sk8er Boi]]" by [[Avril Lavigne]]" was the only song from 2002 to reach its peak in 2003.<ref name = "Aloud">{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2629761.stm |title=Girls Aloud hold top spot |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=5 January 2003 |access-date=17 February 2011}}</ref> Sixty artists scored multiple entries in the top ten in 2003. [[50 Cent]], [[The Darkness (band)|The Darkness]], [[Good Charlotte]], [[Sean Paul]] and [[The White Stripes]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top-ten single in 2003.

The 2002 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Sound of the Underground (song)|Sound of the Underground]]" by [[Girls Aloud]] (which was the group's winners single after being formed on ''[[Popstars: The Rivals]]''), remained at number-one for the first three weeks of 2003.<ref name = "Aloud" /><ref name = "Sound of the Underground">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2599721.stm|title=Girls Aloud top festive chart|date=23 December 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Stop Living the Lie]]" by ''[[Fame Academy (series 1)|Fame Academy]]'' winner [[David Sneddon]].<ref name = "Sneddon">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2674963.stm|title=Fame Academy's David tops chart|date=19 January 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> Overall, twenty-two different singles peaked at number-one in 2003, with [[Busted (band)|Busted]] (2) having the most singles hit that position.<ref name = "You Said No">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2980479.stm|title=Madonna tops album chart|date=27 April 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref><ref name = "Crashed the Wedding">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3275899.stm|title=Busted beat Britney to number one|date=16 November 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
Two-hundred and twenty-nine singles charted in 2003, with two-hundred and eighteen singles reaching their peak this year.

Sixty artists scored multiple entries in the top ten in 2003. Jamaican singer [[Sean Paul]] achieved five top five entries in the United Kingdom, the most of any act that year, including the number-one single "[[Breathe (Blu Cantrell song)|Breathe]]", which he recorded with [[Blu Cantrell]].<ref name="Blu Cantrell">{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3116197.stm |title=Blu beats Robbie to top spot |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|date=3 August 2003 |access-date=17 February 2011}}</ref> His second highest charting song was "[[Baby Boy (Beyoncé song)|Baby Boy]]", a duet with [[Destiny's Child]] member [[Beyoncé]] as she launched her solo career, peaking at number two.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3185840.stm|title=Beyonce fails in chart challenge|date=12 October 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> Of his other hit singles, "[[Like Glue]]" charted highest at number three,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196403.stm|title=Sir Elton tops singles chart|date=31 August 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>"[[Get Busy]]" reached a high of number four<ref name = "Get Busy">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3039099.stm|title=Girls Aloud held off top spot|date=19 May 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Gimme the Light]]" landed one place lower but made the top five.<ref name = "Gimme">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2742967.stm|date=9 February 2003|title=Tatu top singles chart again|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>

Six artists had the joint second most top ten singles in 2003 with four each. [[Busted (band)|Busted]], [[Girls Aloud]], [[Christina Aguilera]], [[Robbie Williams]], [[The Cheeky Girls]] and [[Justin Timberlake]] all had four top-ten singles in 2003. Busted claimed the number-one spot on two occasions with "[[You Said No]]"<ref name = "You Said No" /> and "[[Crashed the Wedding]]".<ref name = "Crashed the Wedding" /> A third single, "[[Year 3000]]" placed at number two<ref name = "Sneddon" /> and they achieved a fourth hit single for the year with "[[Sleeping with the Light On]]", reaching a high of number three.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3158615.stm|title=Cassidy returns to number one|date=17 August 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> Girls Aloud took debut single "[[Sound of the Underground (song)|Sound of the Underground]]" - recorded as their winning single for the television competition show [[Popstars the Rivals]] - to number one at the end of the previous year, beating rival act [[One True Voice]] to [[List of UK Singles Chart Christmas number ones|Christmas number one]].<ref name = "Sound of the Underground" /> While One True Voice disbanded after one more single,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3145325.stm|title=Reality pop band confirm split|date=13 August 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> Girls Aloud scored three more top tens in 2003 - both a cover of "[[Jump (Girls Aloud song)|Jump]]" for the ''[[Love Actually]]'' soundtrack<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3231480.stm|title=Singer Jackson tops album chart|date=23 November 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[No Good Advice]]" placed at number two,<ref name = "Get Busy" /> and "[[Life Got Cold]]" made number three.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3207926.stm|title=Girls Aloud's year at the top|date=20 October 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>

Fellow Popstars contestants The Cheeky Girls followed up December 2002's launch single "[[The Cheeky Song (Touch My Bum)]]" (a number two hit) with three more top ten singles.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2578073.stm|title=Elton takes top slot|date=15 December 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Take Your Shoes Off (song)|Take Your Shoes Off]]" went straight in at number three, as did "[[Hooray! Hooray! It's a Holi-Holiday#Cover versions|Hooray Hooray (It's a Cheeky Holiday)]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3018787.stm|title=Troubled R Kelly tops UK chart|date=11 May 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref> A Christmas single, "[[Have a Cheeky Christmas]]" scraped into the top ten at the end of the year. Christina Aguilera had one of the biggest hits of her career with "[[Beautiful (Christina Aguilera song)|Beautiful]]", which topped the chart in March 2003.<ref name="Aloud" /> "[[Fighter (Christina Aguilera song)|Fighter]]" was a number three entry,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2992314.stm|title=Radiohead's Thief steals top spot|date=15 June 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Can't Hold Us Down]]", with the added vocals of [[Lil' Kim]] placed at number 6<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3108340.stm|title=Black Eyed Peas hold UK top spot|date=14 September 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[The Voice Within]]" rounded off her year, peaking at number nine.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/search/singles/the%20voice%20within/|title=Christina Aguilera - The Voice Within|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=26 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Justin Timberlake]], who had previously been part of '[[N Sync]], had four top ten hits in his own right, including one featured appearance on [[The Black Eyed Peas]] single "[[Where Is the Love?]]". The song spent six weeks at number-one and was the best-selling single of 2003.<ref name="Black Eyed Peas">{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3205846.stm |title=Sugababes take top slot |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=19 October 2003 |access-date=17 February 2011}}</ref> His other chart hits were number-two peaking "[[Cry Me a River (Justin Timberlake song)|Cry Me a River]]"<ref name = "Gimme" /> and "[[Rock Your Body]]",<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3391979.stm|title=UK album sales 'reach new high'|date=13 January 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Work It (Nelly song)|Work It]]" with [[Nelly]] which made number seven.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2834893.stm|title=Aguilera keeps hold of top spot|date=9 March 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>

Former [[Take That]] singer [[Robbie Williams]] was the final artist with four top-ten singles to his name in 2003. "[[Feel (Robbie Williams song)|Feel]]" officially charted at the end of 2002 but remained in the chart for the first couple of weeks of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/2550643.stm|title=In brief: Eminem tops singles chart|date=8 December 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref> "[[Something Beautiful (Robbie Williams song)|Something Beautiful]]" reached number three in August,<ref name = "Blu Cantrell" /> "[[Come Undone (Robbie Williams song)|Come Undone]]" ranked one place lower<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3116197.stm|title=Madonna kept off UK top spot|date=20 April 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Sexed Up]]" placed at number ten.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3255709.stm|title=Kylie scores seventh chart-topper|date=9 November 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>

Rock band [[Evanescence]] was one of a number of artists with three top-ten entries, including the number one single "[[Bring Me to Life]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2973606.stm|title=US rockers top charts with debut|date=8 June 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>[[50 Cent]], [[Big Brovaz]], [[Delta Goodrem]], [[Good Charlotte]] and [[Westlife]] were among the other artists who had multiple top-ten entries in 2003.

===Chart debuts===
Eighty-five artists achieved their first top-ten single in 2003, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, thirteen went on to record another hit single that year: [[Appleton (music duo)|Appleton]], [[Amy Studt]], [[Black Eyed Peas]], [[The Coral]], [[The Darkness (band)|The Darkness]], [[David Sneddon]], [[Electric Six]], [[Fast Food Rockers]], [[Jaimeson]], [[Lemar]], [[Richard X]], [[t.A.T.u]] and [[Triple 8]]. Five artists achieved two more chart hits in 2003: [[50 Cent]], [[Delta Goodrem]], [[D-Side]], [[Evanescence]] and [[Good Charlotte]]. [[Sean Paul]] had four other entries in his breakthrough year.

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top ten in the charts in 2003. The [[Comic Relief]] single was a cover version of [[Norman Greenbaum]]'s "[[Spirit in the Sky#Gareth Gates (with the Kumars) version|Spirit in the Sky]]" by [[Gareth Gates]] and [[The Kumars at No. 42|The Kumars]], peaking at number one on 22 March 2003.<ref name = "Spirit">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/1891053.stm|title=Gates tops the charts|date=16 March 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref>

[[Shane Richie]] recorded the [[Children in Need]] single for 2003, a cover of [[Wham!]]'s "[[I'm Your Man (Wham! song)#Shane Richie version|I'm Your Man]]". It peaked at number two on 6 December 2003.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3299021.stm|title=Young back on top of the charts|date=30 November 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref>

===Best-selling singles===
[[Black Eyed Peas]] had the best-selling single of the year with "Where Is The Love?". The song spent eleven weeks in the top ten (including six weeks at number one), sold over 625,000 copies and was certified platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref name="Black Eyed Peas" /><ref name = "Where is the Love">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/official-chart-flashback-2003-black-eyed-peas-where-is-the-love__4623/|title=Official Chart Flashback 2003: Black Eyed Peas – Where Is The Love?|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=11 October 2013|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> "[[Spirit in the Sky#Gareth Gates (with the Kumars) version|Spirit in the Sky]]" by [[Gareth Gates]] and [[The Kumars]] came in second place, selling more than 552,000 copies and losing out by around 70,000 sales.<ref name = "Spirit" /> [[R. Kelly]]'s "[[Ignition (Remix)]]",<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/lists/100-best-songs-of-the-aughts-20110617/r-kelly-ignition-remix-20110616|title=100 Best Songs of the 2000s|date=17 July 2011|magazine=Rolling Stone|accessdate=26 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Mad World#Michael Andrews and Gary Jules version|Mad World]]" from [[Michael Andrews (musician)|Michael Andrews]] featuring [[Gary Jules]]<ref name="Gary Jules">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3335473.stm|title=Gary Jules tops Christmas charts|date=21 December 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref> and "[[Leave Right Now]]" by [[Will Young]] made up the top five.<ref name = "Changes">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3316687.stm|title=Osbournes duet is new number one|date=14 December 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref> Singles by [[t.A.T.u.]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/2718835.stm|title=Controversial duo top UK pop chart|date=2 February 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref> [[Ozzy Osbourne|Ozzy]] & [[Kelly Osbourne]],<ref name = "Changes" /> [[Blu Cantrell]] featuring [[Sean Paul]],<ref name="Blu Cantrell" /> [[Room 5]] featuring [[Oliver Cheatham]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/2914489.stm|title=Ad track holds number one|date=6 April 2003|pages=BBC News |access-date=28 November 2016}}</ref> and [[The Darkness (band)|The Darkness]]<ref name="Gary Jules" /> were also in the [[2003 in British music charts#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

==Entries by artist==
{{see also|List of UK Singles Chart number ones of the 2000s#2003}}
[[File:Evanescence 2003.jpg|thumb|right|150px|alt=|US rock band [[Evanescence]] scored three singles in the top ten this year. Their debut entry, "[[Bring Me to Life]]", remains their biggest hit, topping the chart for four weeks and spending nine weeks in the top ten altogether.]]
[[File:Fatman Scoop (cropped).jpg|thumb|right|150px|alt=|US hip hop musician [[Fatman Scoop]] reached number-one for two weeks in 2003 with his single "[[Be Faithful]]", which featured production duo The Crooklyn Clan.]]
[[File:Christina Aguilera (2006).jpg|thumb|right|150px|alt=|[[Christina Aguilera]] recorded four top ten entries this year. The highest-charting of these was "[[Beautiful (Christina Aguilera song)|Beautiful]]", which became a number-one hit in March.]]
[[File:Benassi@ElSalvador cropped.JPG|thumb|right|150px|alt=|Italian DJ [[Benny Benassi]] entered the UK top ten in July 2003 with [[Satisfaction (Benny Benassi song)|Satisfaction]], which peaked at number two.]]
[[File:Shane Richie headshot.jpg|thumb|right|150px|alt=|Actor and singer [[Shane Richie]] covered [[Wham!]]'s "[[I'm Your Man (Wham! song)|I'm Your Man]]" for this year's [[Children in Need]], taking it to number two.]]
[[File:Electric Six.jpg|thumb|right|150px|alt=|US rock band [[Electric Six]] scored two top-ten hits in the UK in 2003, most notably reaching number two in January with "[[Danger! High Voltage]]".]]
[[File:2013-08-25 Chiemsee Reggae Summer - Wayne Wonder 6050.JPG|thumb|right|150px|alt=|Jamaican singer [[Wayne Wonder]] spent six weeks in the UK top 10 this year with "[[No Letting Go (song)|No Letting Go]]", which peaked at number three.]]

The following table shows artists who achieved two or more top-ten entries in 2003, including singles that reached their peak in 2002. The figures include both main artists and featured artists, while appearances on ensemble charity records are also counted for each artist. The total number of weeks an artist spent in the top ten in 2003 is also shown.
