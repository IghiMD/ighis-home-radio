The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/|title=The Official Charts Company|publisher=[[Official Charts Company]]|accessdate=28 December 2014}}</ref> Since 2004 the chart has been based on the sales of both [[CD single|physical singles]] and [[music download|digital downloads]], with [[airplay]] figures excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]]|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]] |edition=18th }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |accessdate=21 February 2010}}</ref> Since 2014, the singles chart has been based on both sales and streaming, with the ratio altered in 2017 to 150:1 streams and only three singles by the same artist eligible for the chart.<ref name="Rules">{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/newsbeat-40405357|title=Official Singles Chart rules are changing to help new bands break through|date=26 June 2017|work=Newsbeat|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=26 June 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.musicweek.com/talent/read/official-charts-company-changes-conversion-rate-to-reflect-rise-in-streaming/066932|title=Official Charts Company changes conversion rate to reflect rise in streaming|first=Ben|last=Homewood|date=19 December 2016|work=Music Week|accessdate=20 January 2017}}</ref> From July 2018, video streams from [[YouTube Music]] and [[Spotify]] among others began to be counted for the Official Charts. This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2023 in British music|2023]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2022|2022]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2024|2024]] but were in the top 10 in 2023. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Ninety-eight singles were in the top 10 this year. Seventeen singles from 2022 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Stick Season (song)|Stick Season]]" by [[Noah Kahan]] was released in 2023 but did not reach its peak until 2024. "[[Calm Down (Rema song)|Calm Down]]" by [[Rema (musician)|Rema]], "[[Escapism (Raye song)|Escapism]]" by [[Raye]], "[[Merry Christmas (song)|Merry Christmas]]" by [[Ed Sheeran]] and [[Elton John]], "[[Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree]]" by [[Brenda Lee]], "[[It's Beginning to Look a Lot Like Christmas#Michael Bublé version|It's Beginning to Look a Lot Like Christmas]]" by [[Michael Bublé]], "Firebabe" by [[Stormzy]], "[[Fairytale of New York]]" by [[The Pogues]] featuring [[Kirsty MacColl]] and "[[Let Go (Central Cee song)|Let Go]]" by [[Central Cee]] were the singles from 2022 to reach their peak in 2023. [[Bugzy Malone]], [[Miguel (singer)|Miguel]], [[PinkPantheress]], [[Coi Leray]], [[Lizzy McAlpine]], [[Ice Spice]], [[Fred Again]], [[Sonny Fodera]] and [[Noah Kahan]] were among the many artists who achieved their first top 10 single in 2023.

"[[Last Christmas]]" by [[Wham!]], originally released in 1984, returned to the top of the chart in the first week of 2023. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Escapism (Raye song)|Escapism]]" by [[Raye]] featuring [[070 Shake]]. Overall, fifteen different songs peaked at number-one in 2023, with [[Lewis Capaldi]] (2) having the most songs hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
Ninety-eight singles charted in the top 10 in 2023, with eighty-nine singles reaching their peak this year (including the re-entries "[[Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree]]", "[[It's Beginning to Look a Lot Like Christmas#Michael Bublé version|It's Beginning to Look a Lot Like Christmas]]" and "[[Fairytale of New York]]" which charted in previous years but reached peaks on their latest chart run).

===Miley Cyrus achieves a streaming career best and second prestigious Chart Double===
On 20 January 2023 (26 January 2023, week ending), [[Miley Cyrus]] debuted at number-one in the [[UK Singles Chart]] with "[[Flowers (Miley Cyrus song)|Flowers]]", which became her third number-one, and her first since "[[Wrecking Ball (Miley Cyrus song)|Wrecking Ball]]" topped the charts in October 2013. "Flowers" boasted 92,000 chart units and exceeded 9.9 million streams, Cyrus' biggest number in its first week. It was also the UK's largest first week activity since "[[As It Was]]" by [[Harry Styles]] in April 2022.<ref>{{Cite web|date=January 20, 2023|title=Miley Cyrus gets her Flowers as she debuts at Number 1|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/miley-cyrus-gets-her-flowers-as-she-debuts-at-number-1-on-the-official-singles-chart__38326/|access-date=January 20, 2023|website=Official Charts Company}}</ref> Cyrus went on to achieve the prestigious Chart Double for the second time in her career in March 2023 when during the song's ninth week at number one, the album ''[[Endless Summer Vacation]]'' debuted at the top of the [[UK Albums Chart]], ten years after this was achieved when ''[[Bangerz (Miley Cyrus album)|Bangerz]]'' and "Wrecking Ball" topped both the albums and singles charts, respectively.<ref>{{Cite web|date=March 17, 2023|title=Miley Cyrus claims Official Chart Double as Endless Summer Vacation and Flowers hit Number 1|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/miley-cyrus-claims-official-chart-double-as-endless-summer-vacation-and-flowers-hit-number-1__38794/|access-date=March 17, 2023|website=Official Charts Company}}</ref>

===Sleeper hits gain chart success===
On 27 January 2023 (2 February 2023, week ending), [[Miguel (singer)|Miguel]] entered the top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] at number 10 with "[[Sure Thing (Miguel song)|Sure Thing]]", which gave the American singer his first UK top 10 hit. Originally released in 2010, the song saw a resurgence in popularity worldwide in late 2022, due to a viral dance trend on [[TikTok]]. It eventually peaked at number 4 on 10 February 2023 (16 February 2023, week ending).<ref>{{Cite web|date=February 9, 2023|title=Miguel on Sure Thing's revival: "Music is kept alive by humans, it's a trip"|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/miguel-on-the-viral-revival-of-sure-thing-his-first-ever-uk-top-10-single-kids-are-making-dances-to-a-song-i-made-when-i-was-a-kid-__38473/|access-date=February 23, 2023|website=Official Charts Company}}</ref>

Miguel was not the only artist to win big with a previously unheralded song. [[The Weeknd]]'s 2016 album track "[[Die for You (The Weeknd song)|Die for You]]", taken from the album ''[[Starboy (album)|Starboy]]'', took off on TikTok after [[Ariana Grande]] was added to a remix of the song. This led to the song entering the UK top 10 for the first time at number 4 on 3 March 2023 (9 March 2023, week ending), before rising to number 3 the following week, and staying in that position for two weeks.<ref>{{cite web |last=Griffiths |first=George|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/miley-cyrus-blooms-for-eighth-week-at-number-1-with-flowers-longest-running-solo-female-chart-topper-since-olivia-rodrigo-s-drivers-license-__38744/ |title=Miley Cyrus blooms for eighth week at Number 1 with Flowers – longest-running solo female chart-topper since Olivia Rodrigo's drivers license |publisher=Official Charts Company |date=10 March 2023 |access-date=11 April 2023}}</ref> The song was also a big hit in the United States, topping the [[Billboard Hot 100]] shortly after its ascension in the UK.<ref>{{cite web |last=Armstrong |first=Megan |url=https://uproxx.com/pop/the-weeknd-ariana-grande-die-for-you-no-1-hot-100-chart/ |title=Ariana Grande Heard The 'Not A Singer Anymore' Slander And Returned To No. 1 On The Hot 100 With The Weeknd |publisher=Uproxx |date=6 March 2023 |access-date=11 April 2023}}</ref>

[[Taylor Swift]] went onto achieve success with a sleeper hit later on in the year. On 7 July 2023 (13 July 2023, week ending), Swift earned her 22nd top-ten single with "[[Cruel Summer (Taylor Swift song)|Cruel Summer]]", taken from the album ''[[Lover (Taylor Swift album)|Lover]]''. The song initially peaked at number 27 in September 2019 following the album's release, but saw a resurgence in popularity as Swift embarked on [[The Eras Tour]] resulting in the song subsequently becoming a single and entering the UK top 10 for the first time at number 6 and later peaking at number 2.<ref>{{cite web |last=Griffiths |first=George |url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/inside-the-rise-and-rise-of-taylor-swifts-cruel-summer |title=Inside the rise and rise of Taylor Swift's Cruel Summer |publisher=Official Charts Company |date=27 June 2023 |access-date=7 July 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Smith|first=Carl|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/dave-central-cee-number-1-song |title= Dave & Central Lee fend off Olivia Rodrigo for fifth week at Number 1 with Sprinter|publisher=Official Charts Company |date=7 July 2023 |access-date=7 July 2023}}</ref>

===Dave & Central Cee score record-breaking number-one===
On 9 June 2023 (15 June 2023, week ending), [[Dave (rapper)|Dave]] and [[Central Cee]]'s collaboration "[[Sprinter (song)|Sprinter]]" debuted at number-one in the [[UK Singles Chart]], giving Dave his third number-one single and Central Cee his first. When "Sprinter" topped the chart, it clinched the biggest opening week of the year so far, accumulating 108,200 chart units, including a record-breaking 13.4 million streams - the biggest week of streams for a rap single ever in UK chart history, surpassing [[Stormzy]]'s "[[Vossi Bop]]", which racked up 12.7m in seven days in May 2019. It also earned the biggest first week of streams since [[Adele]]'s "[[Easy On Me]]" debuted with a record-breaking 24 million streams during its first seven days in October 2021.<ref>{{cite web |last=Griffiths |first= George|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/record-breaker-dave-central-cee-debut-at-number-1-with-sprinter-duo-land-biggest-streaming-week-for-any-rap-song-in-uk-chart-history__39527/|title=Record Breaker! Dave & Central Cee debut at Number 1 with Sprinter: Duo land biggest streaming week for any rap song in UK chart history|publisher= Official Charts Company |date=9 June 2023 |accessdate=9 June 2023}}</ref>

Dave and Central Cee set yet another record on 4 August 2023 (10 August 2023, week ending) when "Sprinter" spent a ninth week at number-one, becoming the longest-running British rap number-one single in UK chart history.<ref name=Aug4>{{cite web |last=Griffiths |first=George|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/dave-central-cee-sprinter-number-1-nine-weeks/|title=Dave & Central Cee have record-setting ninth week at Number 1 with Sprinter|publisher= Official Charts Company |date=4 August 2023 |accessdate=4 August 2023}}</ref>

===Kylie Minogue makes a triumphant return and makes chart history===
On 16 June 2023 (22 June 2023, week ending), [[Kylie Minogue]] entered the UK top 10 at number 9 with "[[Padam Padam (song)|Padam Padam]]", which became her 35th top 10 single, later peaking at number 8. The song was also her first top 10 since "[[Higher (Taio Cruz song)|Higher]]", which peaked at number 8 in February 2011. Minogue also became the fourth woman in UK Chart history to earn a top-ten single in five consecutive decades (1980s, 1990s, 2000s, 2010s and 2020s). The other artists to achieve this feat are [[Cher]], [[Diana Ross]] and [[Lulu (singer)|Lulu]] in the 1960s, 1970s, 1980s, 1990s and 2000s.<ref>{{cite web |last=Griffiths |first=George|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/kylie-minogue-exclusive-padam-padam-becomes-her-first-uk-top-10-song-in-12-years__39578/|title=Padam! [[Billie Eilish]] claimed her second number-one single with "[[What Was I Made For?]]", scoring a last-minute victory over [[Dua Lipa]]'s "[[Dance the Night]]" (2), both songs featuring on ''[[Barbie the Album]]''. Joining Eilish and Lipa in the top five were [[Olivia Rodrigo]]'s "[[Vampire (Olivia Rodrigo song)|Vampire]]" (3), [[Taylor Swift]]'s "[[Cruel Summer (Taylor Swift song)|Cruel Summer]]" (4), and [[Peggy Gou]]'s "[[(It Goes Like) Nanana]]" (5), while Rodrigo debuted at number 6 with the highest new entry of the week, "[[Bad Idea Right?]]". Additionally, the top eight that week consisted of female-led songs with [[Nicki Minaj]], [[Ice Spice]] and [[Aqua (band)|Aqua]]'s "[[Barbie World]]" at number 7 and [[Becky Hill]] and [[Chase & Status]]' "[[Disconnect (Becky Hill and Chase & Status song)|Disconnect]]" at number 8.<ref>{{cite web |last= Ainsley |first=Helen|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/solo-women-break-official-singles-chart-record/|title=The Summer of Girl Power continues as female solo artists, led by Billie Eilish, set new Official Chart record|publisher= Official Charts Company |date=18 August 2023 |accessdate=18 August 2023}}</ref>

===Kenya Grace becomes only the second British female solo act to top the UK Charts with a self-produced and self-written track===
On 13 October 2023 (19 October 2023, week ending), [[Kenya Grace]] topped the UK Singles Chart with "[[Strangers (Kenya Grace song)|Strangers]]" during its fourth week in the top 10. Grace became the first female British solo act to top the UK Singles Chart with an entirely self-written and self-produced track since [[Kate Bush]]'s "[[Running Up That Hill]]" in June 2022. She also became the first British female act to reach number one with her debut solo single since [[Ella Henderson]] reached number one with "[[Ghost (Ella Henderson song)|Ghost]]" in June 2014.<ref>{{cite web |last= Griffiths|first= George|url=https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/kenya-grace-first-number-1-strangers/|title=Kenya Grace enters the history books as Strangers climbs to Number 1 on the Official Singles Chart|publisher= Official Charts Company |date=13 October 2023 |accessdate=14 October 2023}}</ref>

===The Beatles make a record-breaking return to number-one===
On 10 November 2023 (16 November 2023, week ending), [[The Beatles]] returned to the top of the [[UK Singles Chart]] with their "final single", "[[Now and Then (Beatles song)|Now and Then]]". The song, begun as a home demo by [[John Lennon]] in 1977 and completed thanks to the use of [[Artificial intelligence|AI]], became their 18th UK number-one single, 60 years after they first topped the chart with "[[From Me to You]]" in 1963, making this the longest gap between an artist's first and last number-one in UK chart history. The Beatles also became the act with the longest gap ever between UK number-one singles, as they last reached the top with "[[The Ballad of John and Yoko]]" 54 years earlier in 1969, thus overtaking [[Kate Bush]]'s 44 years between "[[Wuthering Heights (song)|Wuthering Heights]]" (1978) and "[[Running Up That Hill]]" (2022). The single earned 78,200 chart sales, including 48,600 sales including physical sales and downloads in the first week, making it the fastest selling song of the year.<ref name=Beatles/>

==="Last Christmas" finally claims the Christmas number-one spot===
On 8 December 2023 (14 December 2023, week ending), [[Wham!]]'s "[[Last Christmas]]" returned to number-one in the [[UK Singles Chart]] for a fourth non-consecutive week, after previously topping the chart in both 2020 (for one week) and 2022 (for two non-consecutive weeks), making it the first Christmas single to reach number-one in the UK on four separate occasions.<ref>{{cite web|last= Griffiths|first= George|url= https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/wham-number-1-last-christmas-return-the-pogues-shane-macgowan-chart-record/|title= Wham! return to Number 1 for fourth time with Last Christmas: "Christmas has come early – long may it continue!"|publisher= Official Charts Company |date=8 December 2023 |accessdate=8 December 2023}}</ref> The song retained the top spot for a further three weeks, and on 22 December 2023 (28 December 2023, week ending), it finally claimed the [[List of UK Singles Chart Christmas number ones|coveted Christmas number-one spot]], 39 years after its initial release, and seven years after the death of the song's writer and Wham!'s lead singer [[George Michael]].<ref>{{cite web|last= Griffiths|first= George|url= https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/wham-last-christmas-number-1-week-five-christmas-number-1-2023/|title= Wham! spend second week at Number 1 with Last Christmas - now key contenders for Christmas Number 1|publisher= Official Charts Company |date=15 December 2023 |accessdate=15 December 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last= Griffiths|first= George|url= https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/christmas-number-1-2023-wham-last-christmas-makes-chart-history-andrew-ridgeley/|title= Christmas Number 1 2023: WHAM! make history as Last Christmas finally secures festive top spot for the first time|publisher= Official Charts Company |date=22 December 2023 |accessdate=22 December 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last= Griffiths|first= George|url= https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/whams-last-christmas-completes-straight-month-at-number-1/|title= WHAM!’s Last Christmas completes straight month at Number 1|publisher= Official Charts Company|date=29 December 2023 |accessdate=29 December 2023}}</ref>

;Notes
Despite being considered a [[Christmas]] [[Standard (music)|Standard]], "Jingle Bell Rock" entered the top-ten for the first time in January 2023 becoming Helms' first top-ten single.

[[Jimin]] earned his first UK top ten debut in April 2023 with "[[Like Crazy (song)|Like Crazy]]", and [[Jung Kook]] earned his first UK top ten debut in July 2023 with "[[Seven (Jung Kook song)|Seven]]". These also became their first solo top ten following the hiatus of [[BTS]], respectively.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from films charted in the top-ten throughout the year. These included "[[What Was I Made For?]]", "[[Dance the Night]]", "[[Barbie World]]" and "[[Speed Drive]]" (from ''[[Barbie (film)|Barbie]]'').

===Best-selling singles===
[[Miley Cyrus]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Flowers (Miley Cyrus song)|Flowers]]". The song spent ten weeks at number-one, sold 1,800,000 copies and was certified 3× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Industry|BPI]]. "[[Sprinter (song)|Sprinter]]" by [[Dave (rapper)|Dave]] and [[Central Cee]] came in second place, while [[Raye]] featuring [[070 Shake]]'s "[[Escapism (Raye song)|Escapism]]", [[Taylor Swift]]'s "[[Anti-Hero (song)|Anti-Hero]]" and [[Calvin Harris]] and [[Ellie Goulding]]'s "[[Miracle (Calvin Harris and Ellie Goulding song)|Miracle]]" made up the top five. Songs by [[Rema (musician)|Rema]], [[SZA]], [[PinkPantheress]], [[Harry Styles]] and [[Libianca Fonji|Libianca]] were also in [[2023 in British music#Top singles of the year|the top ten best-selling singles of the year]].

== Notes ==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|All|A|a}} "All I Want for Christmas Is You" re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 8 December 2022 (week ending). The song originally peaked at number 2 upon its initial release in 1994 and reached number-one for the first time ever on 17 December 2020 (week ending).
*{{note label|Last|B|b}} "Last Christmas" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 8 December 2022 (week ending). The song originally peaked at number 2 upon its initial release in 1984 and reached number-one for the first time ever on 7 January 2021 (week ending).
*{{note label|Merry Christmas|C|c}} "Merry Christmas" re-entered the top 10 at number 4 on 15 December 2022 (week ending), having originally peaked at number-one upon release in 2021.
*{{note label|Rockin'|D|d}} "Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree" re-entered the top 10 at number 6 on 15 December 2022 (week ending). Having originally peaked at number 6 upon its original release in 1962, the song reached a new peak of number 4 on 5 January 2023 (week ending).
*{{note label|Bublé|E|e}} "It's Beginning to Look a Lot Like Christmas" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 15 December 2022 (week ending). Having first peaked inside the top 10 at number 7 in 2018, the song reached a new peak of number 6 on 5 January 2023 (week ending).
*{{note label|Fairytale|F|f}} "Fairytale of New York" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 22 December 2022 (week ending), having originally peaked at number 2 upon release in 1987.
*{{note label|Bublé|G|g}} "It's Beginning to Look A lot Like Christmas" re-entered the top 10 at number 6 on 5 January 2023 (week ending).
*{{note label|Fairytale|H|h}} "Fairytale of New York" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 5 January 2023 (week ending).
*{{note label|Its|I|i}} "It's the Most Wonderful Time of the Year" first charted at number 21 in 2007, and reached the top 10 for the first time on 6 January 2022 (week ending), peaking at number 9.

*{{note label|Miss|O|o}} "Miss You" re-entered the top 10 at number 7 on 12 January 2023 (week ending).
*{{note label|Calm Down|P|p}} "Calm Down" re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 12 January 2023 (week ending).
*{{note label|Another|Q|q}} "Another Love" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 12 January 2023 (week ending), having originally peaked at number 10 upon release in 2013.

*{{note label|Boy's a Liar|S|s}} "Boy's a Liar" entered the top 10 at number 8 on 16 February 2023 (week ending), following the release of the subsequent ''[[Boy's a Liar#Boy's a Liar Pt. 2|Boy's a Liar Pt. 2]]'' remix featuring [[Ice Spice]].
