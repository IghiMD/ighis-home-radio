The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]]|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|edition=18th |publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1981 in British music|1981]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1980|1980]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1982|1982]] but were in the top 10 in 1981. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).{{citation needed|date=November 2018}}

One-hundred and forty-five singles were in the top ten in 1981. Ten singles from 1980 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Ant Rap]]" by [[Adam and the Ants]], "[[I'll Find My Way Home]]" by [[Jon and Vangelis]], "[[It Must Be Love (Labi Siffre song)|It Must Be Love]]" by [[Madness (band)|Madness]], "[[The Land of Make Believe]]" by [[Bucks Fizz]] and "[[Mirror Mirror (Dollar song)|Mirror Mirror (Mon Amour)]]" by [[Dollar (band)|Dollar]] were released in 1981 but did not reach their peak until 1982. "[[Antmusic]]" by Adam and the Ants, "[[Happy Xmas (War is Over)]]" by  John Lennon, [[Yoko Ono]], the [[Plastic Ono Band]] with the Harlem Community Center Choir and "[[Imagine (John Lennon song)|Imagine]]" by John Lennon were the singles from 1980 to reach their peak in 1981. Thirty artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1981. [[Duran Duran]], [[The Human League]], [[Kim Wilde]], [[Shakin' Stevens]] and [[Ultravox]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1981.{{citation needed|date=November 2018}}

The 1980 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[There's No One Quite Like Grandma]]" by [[St. Winifred's School Choir]], remained at number-one for the first week of 1981. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Imagine (John Lennon song)|Imagine]]" by the late former [[The Beatles|Beatles]] singer John Lennon. Overall, twenty different singles peaked at number-one in 1981, with Adam and the Ants, John Lennon and [[Shakin' Stevens]] (2) having the joint most singles hit that position.{{citation needed|date=November 2018}}

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and forty-five singles charted in the top 10 in 1981, with one-hundred and thirty-three singles reaching their peak this year.

Thirty artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1981. [[Adam and the Ants]] secured the record for most top 10 hits in 1981 with six hit singles.

[[Soft Cell]] were one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Tainted Love]]". [[Bucks Fizz]], [[Diana Ross]], [[Kim Wilde]], [[Queen (band)|Queen]] and [[Ultravox]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1981.

===Chart debuts===
Forty-eight artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1981, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, six went on to record another hit single that year: [[Bucks Fizz]], [[Chas & Dave]], [[Kim Wilde]], [[Soft Cell]], [[Stars on 45|Starsound]] and [[Ultravox]]. [[The Human League]] and [[Toyah (band)|Toyah]] both had two more entries in 1981. [[Shakin' Stevens]] had three other entries in his breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Phil Collins]] had his first solo credit independent of [[Genesis (band)|Genesis]] with "[[In the Air Tonight]]", peaking at number two. [[Headgirl]] was a collaboration between [[Motörhead]] and [[Girlschool]], the former having recorded a top 10 single previously, ''"[[The Golden Years (EP)]]"'', with the latter making their top ten debut.

[[Graham Bonnet]]'s only top 10 single as a solo artist, "[[Night Games (Graham Bonnet song)|Night Games]]", reached number 6 in 1981. He had scored hit singles as a member of [[Rainbow (rock band)|Rainbow]] and in the duo [[The Marbles (duo)|The Marbles]] with [[Trevor Gordon]]. [[Lionel Richie]]'s official chart debut on his own came this year when "[[Endless Love (song)|Endless Love]]" peaked at number 7. He had previously made an impression on the top 10 with the group [[Commodores]].

[[Godley & Creme]] had scored top 10 hits in the 1970s as members of the groups [[Hotlegs]] and [[10cc]]. They achieved their first top 10 as a duo in 1981 with "[[Under Your Thumb]]", which peaked at number three.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[Flash (Queen song)|Flash]]" (''[[Flash Gordon (film)|Flash Gordon]]''), "[[Chi Mai]]" (''[[The Professional (1981 film)|The Professional]]'') and "[[For Your Eyes Only (song)|For Your Eyes Only]]" (''[[For Your Eyes Only (film)|For Your Eyes Only]]'').

===Best-selling singles===
[[The Human League]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Don't You Want Me]]". The single spent nine weeks in the top 10 (including five weeks at number one), sold over 1.15 million copies and was certified platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]]. "[[Tainted Love]]" by [[Soft Cell]] came in second place, selling more than 1.04 million copies. [[Adam and the Ants]]' "[[Stand and Deliver]]", "[[Prince Charming (Adam and the Ants song)|Prince Charming]]" by [[Adam and the Ants]] and [[Shakin' Stevens]]' "[[This Ole House]]" made up the top five. Singles by [[Ultravox]], [[Michael Jackson]], [[Bucks Fizz]], [[Joe Dolce|Joe Dolce Music Theatre]] and [[The Tweets]] were also in the [[1981 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

[[File:The Specials crop.jpg|thumb|right|150px|alt=|[[The Specials]] achieved two top 10 singles in 1981, including their biggest hit, the protest song "[[Ghost Town (The Specials song)|Ghost Town]]", which spent three weeks at the top of the chart in July. It was also the last single recorded by the original seven members of the group before their split later that year, with Specials bandmates [[Terry Hall (singer)|Terry Hall]], [[Lynval Golding]] and [[Neville Staple]] going on to form [[Fun Boy Three]].]]
[[File:Julio Iglesias (9519693654).jpg|thumb|right|150px|alt=|[[Julio Iglesias]] made the UK top 10 for the first time in November of this year with his cover version of "[[Begin the Beguine]]", which reached number-one for one week.]]

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Bucks Fizz|A|a}} "The Land of Make Believe" reached its peak of number-one on 16 January 1982 (week ending).
*{{note label|Lennon|B|b}} "Imagine", "Happy Xmas (War is Over)" and "(Just Like) Starting Over" all re-entered the top 10 following John Lennon's [[Murder of John Lennon|murder]] on 8 December 1980. "Imagine" was first released as a single in the UK in 1975 and peaked at number 6. "Happy Xmas (War is Over)" was first released as a single in the UK in 1972 and peaked at number 4. Both singles reached new peaks of numbers 1 and 2, respectively, following Lennon's death.
*{{note label|Woman|C|c}} "Woman" was the first official Lennon single released after his death.
*{{note label|St Valentines|D|d}} Headgirl was a supergroup made up of [[Motörhead]] and [[Girlschool]].
*{{note label|Eurovision|E|e}} "Making Your Mind Up" was the [[United Kingdom in the Eurovision Song Contest 1981|United Kingdom]]'s winning entry at the [[Eurovision Song Contest]] in [[Eurovision Song Contest 1981|1981]].
*{{note label|Chi mai|F|f}} "Chi mai" was used as the theme song for the BBC television series ‘’[[The Life and Times of David Lloyd George]]’’.
*{{note label|Attention|G|g}} "Attention to Me" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 2 May 1981 (week ending).
*{{note label|Starsound|H|h}} Starsound were known as Stars on 45 in their home country, the Netherlands.
*{{note label|FA Cup|I|i}} "Ossie's Dream (Spurs Are On Their Way to Wembley)" was released by [[Tottenham Hotspur F.C.]] to celebrate reaching the [[FA Cup Final]] in [[1981 FA Cup Final|1981]].
*{{note label|Chas & Dave|J|j}} Chas & Dave's appearance on "Ossie's Dream (Spurs Are On Their Way to Wembley)" is uncredited but is counted in their [[Chas & Dave discography|discography]].
*{{note label|1981 peak|K|k}} Figure includes song that first charted in 1980 but peaked in 1981.
*{{note label|1982|L|l}} Figure includes song that peaked in 1982.
*{{note label|1980|M|m}} Figure includes song that peaked in 1980.
*{{note label|Tottenham|N|n}} Figure includes uncredited appearance on Tottenham Hotspur FA Cup Final Squad's "Ossie's Dream (Spurs Are on Their Way to Wembley)".
*{{note label|Headgirls|O|o}} Figure includes the group project "St Valentine's Day Massacre (EP)" with [[Girlschool]].
