The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]]|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]|edition=18th }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1998 in British music|1998]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1997|1997]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1999|1999]] but were in the top 10 in 1998. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and ninety-six singles were in the top ten in 1998. Ten singles from 1997 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Chocolate Salty Balls (P.S. I Love You)]]" by [[Chef (South Park)|Chef]] and "[[Heartbeat (Steps song)|Heartbeat]]"/"[[Tragedy (Bee Gees song)#Steps version|Tragedy]]" by [[Steps (pop group)|Steps]] were both released in 1998 but did not reach their peak until 1999. "[[Angels (Robbie Williams song)|Angels]]" by [[Robbie Williams]] and "[[Never Ever (All Saints song)|Never Ever]]" by [[All Saints (group)|All Saints]] were the singles from 1997 to reach their peak in 1998. Forty-nine artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1998. Steps, [[B*Witched]], [[Usher (entertainer)|Usher]], [[Destiny's Child]], [[Fatboy Slim]], [[The Corrs]], [[Shania Twain]], [[Missy Elliott]] and [[Stereophonics]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1998.

The 1997 [[Christmas number-one]], "[[Too Much (Spice Girls song)|Too Much]]" by [[Spice Girls]], remained at number-one for the first week of 1998. Charity single "[[Perfect Day (BBC promotion)|Perfect Day]]" by Various Artists returned to the top spot for an additional week. The first new number-one single of the year was "Never Ever" by All Saints. Overall, thirty-two different singles peaked at number-one in 1998, with All Saints, [[B*Witched]], [[Boyzone]] (including the "Perfect Day" charity single) and [[Mel B]] (3) having the joint most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and ninety-four singles charted in the top 10 in 1998, with one-hundred and eighty-seven singles reaching their peak this year (including the re-entries "Sit Down" and "You're the One That I Want" which charted in previous years but reached peaks on their latest chart run).

Forty-nine artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1998. Two members of [[Spice Girls]] - [[Mel B]] and [[Melanie C]] - shared the record for the most top-ten singles in 1998 with six entries. Five of these singles were with the band, but they also had individual collaborations outside of the band. Mel B reached number one in September with "[[I Want You Back (Mel B song)|I Want You Back]]", which featured American rapper [[Missy Elliott|Missy "Missdemeanor" Elliott]]. Melanie C collaborated with Canadian rock artist [[Bryan Adams]] on "[[When You're Gone (Bryan Adams song)|When You're Gone]]", the song peaking at number 3 in December. Three of Spice Girls top ten entries reached number-one: "[[Too Much (Spice Girls song)|Too Much]]" in December 1997, "[[Viva Forever]]" in July and "[[Goodbye (Spice Girls song)|Goodbye]]" in December 1998. "[[Stop (Spice Girls song)|Stop]]" peaked at number 2 in March, and they also featured on the England United collective single "[[(How Does It Feel to Be) On Top of the World]]" for the [[1998 FIFA World Cup|FIFA World Cup]], which reached number 9.

Seven acts had four singles in the top 10: [[All Saints (group)|All Saints]], [[Aqua (band)|Aqua]], [[Boyzone]], [[Five (band)|Five]], [[Madonna]], [[Robbie Williams]] and [[Steps (pop group)|Steps]].

[[Billie Piper|Billie]] was one of a number of artists with three top 10 entries, including the number-one single "[[Because We Want To]]". [[Another Level (band)|Another Level]], [[Celine Dion]], [[Natalie Imbruglia]], [[Sash!]] and [[Will Smith]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1998.

===Chart debuts===
Eighty-one artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1998, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, eleven went on to record another hit single that year: [[Brandy Norwood|Brandy]], [[Catatonia (band)|Catatonia]], [[Eagle-Eye Cherry]], [[Fatboy Slim]], [[Honeyz]], [[Monica (singer)|Monica]], [[Mýa]], [[Savage Garden]], [[Shania Twain]], [[The Tamperer featuring Maya]] and [[Wyclef Jean]]. [[Another Level (band)|Another Level]], [[B*Witched]], [[Cerys Matthews]], [[Cleopatra (group)|Cleopatra]], [[The Corrs]], [[Pras Michel]] and [[Steps (pop group)|Steps]] all had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Ian Brown]] of [[The Stone Roses]] launched a solo career in 1998, reaching number 5 in January with his debut single "My Star". [[Inaya Day]] was a featured vocalist on "[[Horny '98]]" by [[Mousse T.]] vs. Hot 'n' Juicy but is not credited on the Official Charts Company listing. She would have her first official entry in 2006 with "[[Nasty Girl (Vanity 6 song)|Nasty Girl]]", reaching number 9. [[Fat Les]] was made up of [[Blur (band)|Blur]] bassist [[Alex James (musician)|Alex James]] (who had secured ten top 10 singles up to that point, including number-ones "[[Beetlebum]]" and "[[Country House (Blur song)|Country House]]"), artist [[Damien Hirst]] making his chart debut, and [[Keith Allen (actor)|Keith Allen]], also in the charts for the first time as a singer but he had written "[[New Order (band)|New Order]]'s 1990 single "[[World in Motion]]".

[[Fatboy Slim]] was responsible for the "Renegade Master '98" remix by [[Roger McKenzie (musician)|Wildchild]] at the start of the year but he did not make his official top 10 debut until June with "The Rockafeller Skank". Under his real name Norman Cook, he was also part of the line-up of [[Freak Power (band)|Freak Power]], who debuted with 1995's reissue of "[[Turn On, Tune In, Cop Out]]". [[Jimmy Page]] was a founding member of [[Led Zeppelin]] and in 1998 he collaborated with [[Puff Daddy]] on his song "[[Come with Me (Puff Daddy song)|Come with Me]]" which sampled the Zeppelin song "[[Kashmir (song)|Kashmir]]". [[Mel B]] of [[Spice Girls]] had her first solo single alongside "[[Missy Elliott]], reaching the top of the charts with "[[I Want You Back (Mel B song)|I Want You Back]]". Bandmate [[Melanie C]] made number 3 with her feature on the [[Bryan Adams]] song "[[When You're Gone (Bryan Adams song)|When You're Gone]]".

[[East 17]] returned in 1998 after splitting up in 1997 under the name E-17, which they kept until the following year before breaking up again. Their only top 10 single under this name was the number 2 single "[[Each Time]]". [[Chef (South Park)|Chef]] is a character from the television series ''[[South Park]]'' voiced by [[Isaac Hayes]]. He had recorded two top 10 singles under his own name - "[[Theme from Shaft|Theme from ''Shaft'']]" and "Disco Connection".

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[My Heart Will Go On]]" (from ''[[Titanic (1997 film)|Titanic]]''), "Truly Madly Deeply" (''[[Music from Another Room (film)|Music from Another Room]]''), "[[Turn Back Time (Aqua song)|Turn Back Time]]" (''[[Sliding Doors]]''), "[[Ghetto Supastar (That Is What You Are)]]" (''[[Bulworth]]''), "[[Deeper Underground]]" and "[[Come with Me (Puff Daddy song)|Come with Me]]" (''[[Godzilla (1998 film)|Godzilla]]''), "[[You're the One That I Want]]" (''[[Grease (film)|Grease]]''), "[[Kiss the Girl]]" (''[[The Little Mermaid (1989 film)|The Little Mermaid]]''), "[[Lost in Space (Apollo 440 song)|Lost in Space]]" (''[[Lost in Space (film)|Lost in Space]]''), "[[I Don't Want to Miss a Thing]]" (''[[Armageddon (1998 film)|Armageddon]]''), "[[How Deep Is Your Love (Dru Hill song)|How Deep Is Your Love]]" (''[[Rush Hour (1998 film)|Rush Hour]]''), "[[Another One Bites the Dust#Wyclef Jean remix|Another One Bites the Dust]]" (''[[Small Soldiers]]''), "[[Take Me There (Blackstreet & Mýa song)|Take Me There]]" (''[[The Rugrats Movie]]'') and "[[When You Believe]]" (''[[The Prince of Egypt]]'').  "[[Antmusic]]" was recorded for the movie ''[[A Bug's Life]]'' but was not used in the finished film.

===Best-selling singles===
[[Cher]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Believe (Cher song)|Believe]]". The song spent twelve weeks in the top 10 (including seven weeks at number one), sold over 1.519 million copies and was certified by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]]. "[[My Heart Will Go On]]" by [[Celine Dion]] came in second place, selling more than 1.302 million copies and losing out by around 217,000 sales. [[Run-D.M.C.]] vs. [[Jason Nevins]]' "[[It's Like That (Run-D.M.C. song)|It's Like That]]", "[[No Matter What (Boyzone song)|No Matter What]]" from [[Boyzone]] and "[[C'est la Vie (B*Witched song)|C'est la Vie]]" by [[B*Witched]] made up the top five. Singles by [[LeAnn Rimes]], [[Chef (South Park)|Chef]], [[Spice Girls]], [[Pras Michel]] featuring [[Ol' Dirty Bastard|ODB]] & [[Mýa]] and [[Savage Garden]] were also in the [[1998 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

"Believe" (5) also ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 1990s in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Entries by artist==
{{see also|List of number-one singles from the 1990s (UK)#1998}}
[[File:Jay Kay 2017 3 (cropped).jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Jay Kay]] (pictured in 2017) and his band [[Jamiroquai]] scored their only UK number-one single in July 1998 with "[[Deeper Underground]]", taken from ''[[Godzilla: The Album|the soundtrack]]'' of the movie ''[[Godzilla (1998 film)|Godzilla]]''.]]
[[File:Usher EUA.jpg|thumb|right|150px|American R&B singer [[Usher (musician)|Usher]] (pictured in 2008) entered the UK top 10 for the first time this year with "[[You Make Me Wanna...]]", which spent one week at number-one in January.]]
[[File:AV0A5734 Mousse T (cropped).jpg|thumb|right|150px|German DJ [[Mousse T.]] (pictured in 2017) spent six weeks in the top 10 in 1998 with his hit record "[[Horny '98]]", a collaboration with pop duo [[Hot 'n' Juicy]], which peaked at number two.]]
[[File:BrandyNorwoodJuly04.jpg|thumb|right|150px|US R&B singer [[Brandy Norwood|Brandy]] (pictured in 2004) had two singles in the top 10 this year, both reaching number two. "[[The Boy Is Mine (Brandy and Monica song)|The Boy Is Mine]]" was a duet with fellow R&B artist [[Monica (singer)|Monica]] while "[[Top of the World (Brandy song)|Top of the World]]" featured contributions from rapper and hip hop artist [[Mase]].]]
[[File:Touch and Go.JPG|thumb|right|150px|Jazz pop group [[Touch and Go (band)|Touch and Go]] (pictured in 2009) became one-hit wonders this year with their single "[[Would You...?]]", which reached number three in November.]]

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Steps|A|a}} "Heartbeat"/"Tragedy" reached its peak of number-one on 9 January 1999.
*{{note label|Chef|B|b}} "Chocolate Salty Balls (P.S. I Love You)" reached its peak of number-one on 2 January 1999.
*{{note label|Candle in the Wind|C|c}} "Something About the Way You Look Tonight"/"Candle in the Wind 1997" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 6 December 1997 (week ending), at number 10 on 20 December 1997 (week ending) and at number 10 on 3 January 1998 (week ending).
*{{note label|Children in Need 1997|D|d}} Released as the official single for [[Children in Need]] in 1997.
*{{note label|Renegade|E|e}} The original version of "Renegade Master" peaked outside the top 10 at number 11 upon its release in 1995. The remix by [[Fatboy Slim]], entitled "Renegade Master '98", was released less than three years after Roger McKenzie (Wildchild)'s death.
*{{note label|Asha|F|f}} "Brimful of Asha" originally peaked outside the top 10 at number 60 upon its initial release in 1997. After a remixed version by [[Norman Cook]] became a radio and critical success, the song was re-released and reached number one in February 1998.
*{{note label|Rimes|G|g}} "How Do I Live" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 11 April 1998 (week ending) for 3 weeks and at number 9 on 30 May 1998 (week ending) for 2 weeks. 
*{{note label|Mine|H|h}} "The Boy Is Mine" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 11 July 1998 (week ending) for 2 weeks. 
*{{note label|England United|I|i}} England United was a supergroup consisting of [[Echo and the Bunnymen]], [[Space (English band)|Space]], [[Spice Girls]] and [[Ocean Colour Scene]].<ref>{{cite news |first=Liam |last=Allen |title= Official England World Cup songs |url= https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/10257896 |date= 10 June 2010 |accessdate= 25 October 2012}}</ref>
*{{note label|Grease|J|j}} "You're the One That I Want" originally peaked at number-one for 9 weeks upon its initial release in 1978. The song was re-released in 1998 to mark the 20th anniversary of ''Grease''.
*{{note label|Savage|K|k}} "To the Moon and Back" originally peaked outside the top 10 at number 55 upon its initial release in 1997.
*{{note label|E-17|L|l}} East 17 were known as E-17 from 1998 until 1999.
*{{note label|James|M|m}} The original version of "Sit Down" peaked at number 77 upon its release in 1989. A re-recorded version was released in 1991 and peaked at number 2. The 1998 remix of "Sit Down" was remixed by [[Apollo 440]].
*{{note label|Smith|N|n}} "Miami" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 2 January 1999 (week ending) for 2 weeks.
*{{note label|Charity|O|o}} Denise van Outen, Johnny Vaughan and Steps covered [[Kylie Minogue]] and [[Jason Donovan]]'s hit "Especially for You" as the official Children in Need single for 1998.<ref>{{cite news |title=Children in Need appeal passes £11m |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/218810.stm |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=21 November 1998 |access-date=25 October 2012}}</ref>
