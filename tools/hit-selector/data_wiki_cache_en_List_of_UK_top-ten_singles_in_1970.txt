The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1970 in British music|1970]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1969|1969]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1971|1971]] but were in the top 10 in 1970. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and twenty-two singles were in the top ten in 1970. Eleven singles from 1969 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Grandad (song)|Grandad]]" by [[Clive Dunn]], "[[I'll Be There (The Jackson 5 song)|I'll Be There]]" by [[The Jackson 5]] and "[[Ride a White Swan]]" by [[T. Rex (band)|T. Rex]] were all released in 1970 but did not reach their peak until 1971. "[[All I Have to Do Is Dream]]" by [[Bobbie Gentry]] and [[Glen Campbell]], "[[Melting Pot (song)|Melting Pot]]" by [[Blue Mink]], "[[Suspicious Minds#Elvis Presley recording|Suspicious Minds]]" by [[Elvis Presley]] and "[[Tracy (The Cuff Links song)|Tracy]]" by [[The Cuff Links]] were the singles from 1969 to reach their peak in 1970. Nineteen artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1970. [[Chicago (band)|Chicago]], [[Deep Purple]], [[Hot Chocolate (band)|Hot Chocolate]], [[The Jackson 5]] and [[Neil Diamond]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1970.

The 1969 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Two Little Boys]]" by [[Rolf Harris]], remained at number-one for the first four weeks of 1970. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Love Grows (Where My Rosemary Goes)]]" by [[Edison Lighthouse]]. Overall, fourteen different singles peaked at number-one in 1970, with fourteen unique artists having singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and twenty-two singles charted in the top 10 in 1970, with one-hundred and twelve singles reaching their peak this year.

Nineteen artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1970. [[Elvis Presley]] and [[The Jackson 5]] shared the record for most top 10 hits in 1970 with four hit singles each.

[[Christie (band)|Christie]] was one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Yellow River (song)|Yellow River]]". [[Andy Williams]], [[The Dave Clark Five]], [[Gerry Monroe]], [[Marmalade (band)|Marmalade]] and [[Stevie Wonder]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1970.

===Chart debuts===
Fifty artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1970, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, five went on to record another hit single that year: [[Chairmen of the Board]], [[Chicago (band)|Chicago]], [[Christie (band)|Christie]], [[Gerry Monroe]] and [[White Plains (band)|White Plains]]. [[The Jackson 5]] had three other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Yoko Ono]] and [[John Lennon]] both made their first appearances in the chart where they are given individual credit, both having appeared under the guise of [[Plastic Ono Band]], the latter as a member of [[The Beatles]]. Plastic Ono Band debuted in 1969 with "[[Give Peace a Chance]]".

[[Fair Weather (band)|Fair Weather]] was set up by [[Andy Fairweather Low]], formerly of the newly disbanded group [[Amen Corner (band)|Amen Corner]], who had four top 10 singles between 1966 and 1969, including number-one "[[(If Paradise Is) Half as Nice]]". [[Dave Edmunds]] had been part of [[Love Sculpture]]'s line-up in the late 1960s, "[[Sabre Dance]]" becoming a surprise hit. "[[I Hear You Knocking]]", his debut solo single, was 1970's [[List of UK Singles Chart Christmas number ones|Christmas number-one]].

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[Come and Get It (Badfinger song)|Come and Get It]]" (from ''[[The Magic Christian (film)|The Magic Christian]]'') and "[[Raindrops Keep Fallin' on My Head]]" (''[[Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid]]'').

===Best-selling singles===
[[Mungo Jerry]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[In the Summertime]]". The single spent eleven weeks in the top 10 (including seven weeks at number one). "[[The Wonder of You#Elvis Presley version|The Wonder of You]]" by [[Elvis Presley]] came in second place. [[Freda Payne]]'s "[[Band of Gold (Freda Payne song)|Band of Gold]]", "[[Spirit in the Sky]]" from [[Norman Greenbaum]] and "[[Bridge over Troubled Water (song)|Bridge over Troubled Water]]" by [[Simon & Garfunkel]] made up the top five. Singles by the [[1970 FIFA World Cup squads#England|England World Cup Squad 1970]], [[Free (band)|Free]], [[Lee Marvin]], [[Christie (band)|Christie]] and [[Smokey Robinson]] and [[The Miracles]] were also in the [[1970 in British music#Best-selling singles (covering 17th Jan to 19th Dec 1970)|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.
