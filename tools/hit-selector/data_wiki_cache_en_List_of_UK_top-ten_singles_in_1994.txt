The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during 1994, as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1993|1993]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1995|1995]] but were in the top 10 in 1994. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and thirty-two singles were in the top ten in 1994. Nine singles from 1993 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Another Day (Whigfield song)|Another Day]]" by [[Whigfield]], "[[Cotton Eye Joe (Rednex song)|Cotton Eye Joe]]" by [[Rednex]], "[[Love Me for a Reason#Boyzone version|Love Me for a Reason]]" by [[Boyzone]], "Them Girls, Them Girls" by [[Zig and Zag (puppets)|Zig and Zag]] and "[[Think Twice (Celine Dion song)|Think Twice]]" by [[Celine Dion]] were all released in 1994 but did not reach their peak until 1995. "[[Twist and Shout#Take That version|Twist and Shout]]" by [[Chaka Demus & Pliers]], "The Perfect Year" by [[Dina Carroll]] and "[[It's Alright (East 17 song)|It's Alright]]" by [[East 17]]  were the singles from 1993 to reach its peak in 1994. Twenty-five artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1994. [[Boyzone]], [[Oasis (band)|Oasis]], [[R. Kelly]], [[Red Hot Chili Peppers]] and [[Toni Braxton]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1994.

The 1993 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Mr Blobby (song)|Mr Blobby]]" by [[Mr Blobby]], remained at number-one for the first week of 1994. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Twist and Shout#Chaka Demus & Pliers version|Twist and Shout]]" by [[Chaka Demus & Pliers]] featuring Jack Radics and [[Sly and Robbie|Taxi Gang]]. Overall, fifteen different singles peaked at number-one in 1994, with [[Take That]] (2) having the most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and thirty-two singles charted in the top 10 in 1994, with one-hundred and twenty-one singles reaching their peak this year.

Twenty-five artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1994. American singer [[Mariah Carey]], [[East 17]] and British boy-band [[Take That]] shared the record for the most top ten singles in 1994 with four hit singles each. Carey's cover of the [[Badfinger]] song "[[Without You (Badfinger song)#Mariah Carey version|Without You]]" was her biggest hit of the year; it reached number-one in February and spent four weeks in that position, and eight weeks in the top 10 in total. Her other top ten singles were "[[All I Want for Christmas Is You (Mariah Carey song)|All I Want for Christmas Is You]]" (number 2) in December; "[[Endless Love (song)|Endless Love]]" (3) with [[Luther Vandross]] in September; and "[[Anytime You Need a Friend]]" (8) in June. Three of Take That's four top ten singles reached number-one in the UK: "[[Babe (Take That song)|Babe]]" in December 1993, "[[Everything Changes (Take That song)|Everything Changes]]" in April and "[[Sure (Take That song)|Sure]]" in October. "[[Love Ain't Here Anymore]]" was their other top 10 entry, which reached number 3 in July. [[East 17]] also had a number-one single in 1994, "[[Stay Another Day]]", which stayed there for 5 of its 8 weeks in the top ten. Their other top ten singles were "[[Around the World (East 17 song)|Around the World]]" and "[[It's Alright (East 17 song)|It's Alright]] (both number 3) and "Steam" (number 7). [[Bon Jovi]], [[Oasis (band)|Oasis]] and [[Eternal (band)|Eternal]] had three singles in the top ten in 1994.

[[D Ream|D:Ream]] were one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Things Can Only Get Better (D:Ream song)|Things Can Only Get Better]]". [[Ace of Base]], [[Celine Dion]], [[Haddaway]], [[Meat Loaf]] and [[Tori Amos]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1994.

===Chart debuts===
Sixty-four artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1994, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, seven went on to record another hit single that year: [[C. J. Lewis]], [[D Ream|D:Ream]], [[The Mad Stuntman]], [[Maxx (eurodance act)|Maxx]], [[Reel 2 Real]], [[Tori Amos]] and [[Whigfield]]. [[Oasis (band)|Oasis]] had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Sting (musician)|Sting]] recorded his first top 10 single away from [[The Police]], the collaboration with [[Bryan Adams]] and [[Rod Stewart]], "[[All for Love (song)|All for Love]]", peaking at number two. He also had a solo single in 1994, "When We Dance", which made number nine. The B-52's (best known for their 1990 hit "[[Love Shack]]") appeared on the single "(We're) The Flintstones" under the name The B. C. 52's.

Absolutely Fabulous was a pseudonym used by the highly successful [[Pet Shop Boys]] to produce a [[Absolutely Fabulous (song)|song of the same name]] in aid of [[Comic Relief]]. [[Ali Campbell|Ali]] and [[Robin Campbell (musician)|Robin Campbell]] from the group [[UB40]] guested on [[Pato Banton]]'s cover of [[Baby, Come Back (the Equals song)#The Pato Banton and UB40 version|Baby Come Back]], their first hit single away from the band.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year.  These included "[[All for Love (song)|All for Love]]" (from ''[[The Three Musketeers (1993 film)|The Three Musketeers]]''), "[[A Deeper Love#Aretha Franklin version|A Deeper Love]]" (''[[Sister Act 2: Back In The Habit]]''), "[[Streets of Philadelphia]]" (''[[Philadelphia (film)|Philadelphia]]''), "[[Mmm Mmm Mmm Mmm]]" (''[[Dumb and Dumber]]''), "[[Love Is All Around]]" (''[[Four Weddings and A Funeral]]''), "[[Baby, I Love Your Way]]" (''[[Reality Bites]]''), "[[Meet the Flintstones#The BC-52's cover|(Meet) The Flintstones]]" (''[[The Flintstones (film)|The Flintstones]]''), "[[Regulate (song)|Regulate]]" (''[[Above The Rim]]'') and "[[We Have All the Time in the World]]" (''[[Good Morning, Vietnam]]'').

===Charity singles===
The [[Comic Relief]] single this year was performed by the [[Pet Shop Boys]], who used the name ''Absolutely Fabulous'' for the single of [[Absolutely Fabulous (song)|the same name]], which included lines of dialogue from the [[Absolutely Fabulous|TV series]]. It peaked at number six on 18 June 1994 (week ending).

===Best-selling singles===
[[Wet Wet Wet]] had the biggest-selling single of 1994 with "[[Love Is All Around (The Troggs song)#Wet Wet Wet version|Love Is All Around]]", which spent twenty weeks in the top ten (including fifteen weeks at number-one), sold over 1.783 million copies and was certified 2× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]]. "[[Saturday Night (Whigfield song)|Saturday Night]]" by [[Whigfield]] came in second place. [[East 17]]'s "[[Stay Another Day]]", "[[Baby, Come Back (the Equals song)#Pato Banton featuring Ali and Robin Campbell version|Baby Come Back]]" from [[Pato Banton]] featuring [[Ali Campbell|Ali]] and [[Robin Campbell (musician)|Robin Campbell]] and "[[I Swear (John Michael Montgomery song)|I Swear]]" by [[All-4-One]] made up the top five. Singles by [[Bon Jovi]], [[Mariah Carey]], [[Let Loose]], [[Doop (band)|Doop]] and [[Ace of Base]] were also in the [[1994 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

"Love is All Around" (3) ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 1990s in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]]. It also stands as the tenth [[List of best-selling singles in the United Kingdom|biggest-selling single of all time]] in the UK.

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Whigfield|A|a}} "Another Day" reached its peak of number seven on 7 January 1995 (week ending).
*{{note label|Boyzone|B|b}} "Love Me for a Reason" reached its peak of number two on 7 January 1995 (week ending).
*{{note label|Rednex|C|c}} "Cotton Eye Joe" reached its peak of number-one on 14 January 1995 (week ending).
*{{note label|Them Girls|D|d}} "Them Girls, Them Girls" reached its peak of number five on 1 January 1995 (week ending).
*{{note label|Things|E|e}} "Things Can Only Get Better" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 24 upon its initial release in 1993.
*{{note label|Move|F|f}} "I Like to Move It" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 12 March 1993 (week ending) for 8 weeks.
*{{note label|Duets|G|g}} "Don't Go Breaking My Heart" was recorded as a duet by Elton John and RuPaul for the John album ''[[Duets (Elton John album)|Duets]]''.
*{{note label|Best Thing|H|h}} "U R the Best Thing" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 72 upon its initial release in 1992. It also entered at a new peak of number 19 in April 1993.
*{{note label|Fire|I|i}} "Light My Fire" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 45 upon its initial release in 1993. It was remixed and re-released in 1994. 
*{{note label|Man United|J|j}} "Come on You Reds" was [[1993–94 Manchester United F.C. season|released]] by [[Manchester United F.C.]] to celebrate reaching the [[FA Cup Final]] in [[1994 FA Cup Final|1994]].
*{{note label|Quo|K|k}} Status Quo collaborated on "Come on You Reds" but are not credited on the single's cover.
*{{note label|Comic Relief|L|l}} Released as the official single for [[Comic Relief]].
*{{note label|Ab Fab|M|m}} 'Absolutely Fabulous' was a pseudonym for [[Pet Shop Boys]]. The song "Absolutely Fabulous" borrowed lines from the [[Absolutely Fabulous|television series of the same name]].
*{{note label|Flintstones|N|n}} The B-52's were known as ''The B.C. 52's'' for their recording of "(Meet) The Flintstones" to match the setting of [[The Flintstones (film)|the film]] in the [[Stone Age]].
*{{note label|Crazy|O|o}} "Crazy for You" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 44 upon its initial release in 1993.
*{{note label|DJ Miko|P|p}} DJ Miko's cover of "What's Up?" by [[4 Non Blondes]] featured uncredited vocals from Louise Gard.
*{{note label|Girls|Q|q}} The original version of "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" was released in 1984 and peaked at number 2. The 1994 version, entitled "Hey Now (Girls Just Want to Have Fun)", was a reggae-tinged arrangement of Lauper's original standard, with a musical tip of the hat to Redbone's "Come and Get Your Love".
*{{note label|All the Time|R|r}} "We Have All the Time In the World" was covered by [[My Bloody Valentine (band)|My Bloody Valentine]] for charity and use in a television advertising campaign for [[Guinness]]. The original version by [[Louis Armstrong]] was subsequently re-released, peaking at number three.
*{{note label|Power Rangers|S|s}} "Power Rangers" was released during the airing of the [[Mighty Morphin Power Rangers (season 1)|first series]] of the television series ''[[Mighty Morphin Power Rangers]]'' in the UK and included several lines of dialogue from the show. 
*{{note label|Aaron Waters|T|t}} The song "Power Rangers" was performed by [[Ron Wasserman|Aaron Waters]] (who had also composed the theme tune) but the Official Charts Company credits it to ''Mighty Morphin Power Rangers''.
*{{note label|Another|U|u}} "Another Day" re-entered the top 10 at number 7 on 7 January 1995 (week ending).
*{{note label|Zig and Zag|V|v}} Zig and Zag were puppet characters performed by Mick O'Hara and Ciaran Morrison, introduced on Irish television series ''[[Dempsey's Den]]'' but who found wider fame through [[Channel 4]] series ''[[The Big Breakfast]]''.
*{{note label|1994 peak|W|w}} Figure includes single that first chart in 1993 but peaked in 1994.
*{{note label|1993|X|x}} Figure includes single that peaked in 1993.
*{{note label|1995|Y|y}} Figure includes single that peaked in 1995.
*{{note label|Move It|Z|z}} Figure includes appearances on Reel 2 Real's "I Like to Move It" and "Go On Move".
