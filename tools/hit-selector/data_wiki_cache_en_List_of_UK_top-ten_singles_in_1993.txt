The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1993 in British music|1993]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1992|1992]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1994|1994]] but were in the top 10 in 1993. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and thirty-six singles were in the top ten in 1993. Ten singles from 1992 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Twist and Shout#Take That version|Twist and Shout]]" by [[Chaka Demus & Pliers]], "The Perfect Year" by [[Dina Carroll]] and "[[It's Alright (East 17 song)|It's Alright]]" by [[East 17]] were all released in 1993 but did not reach their peak until 1994. "[[Could It Be Magic#Take That version|Could It Be Magic]]" by [[Take That]] was the only single from 1992 to reach its peak in 1993. Thirty-one artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1993. [[Ace of Base]], [[Eternal (band)|Eternal]], [[Gabrielle (singer)|Gabrielle]], [[Radiohead]] and [[Shaggy (musician)|Shaggy]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1993.

The 1992 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[I Will Always Love You]]" by [[Whitney Houston]], remained at number-one for the first six weeks of 1993. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[No Limit (2 Unlimited song)|No Limit]]" by [[2 Unlimited]]. Overall, fifteen different singles peaked at number-one in 1993, with [[Take That]] (3) having the most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and thirty-six singles charted in the top 10 in 1993, with one-hundred and twenty-four singles reaching their peak this year.

Thirty-one artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1993. Boyband [[Take That]] secured the record for the most top ten singles in 1993 with five hit singles. This included three number-one singles: "[[Pray (Take That song)|Pray]]" in July, "[[Relight My Fire#Take That version|Relight My Fire]]" in October and "[[Babe (Take That song)|Babe]]" in December. "[[Why Can't I Wake Up With You]]" and "[[Could It Be Magic?#Take That version|Could It Be Magic?]]" just missed out on number-one, peaking at numbers 2 and 3 respectively. Fellow [[Manchester]] band [[M People]] had four top ten entries, with the highest entry, "[[Moving on Up (M People song)|Moving on Up]]", reaching number 2. "[[No Limit (2 Unlimited song)|No Limit]]" was a number-one single for [[2 Unlimited]], one of three top 10 singles for the Dutch [[eurodance]] group. [[Chaka Demus & Pliers]], [[Lisa Stansfield]], [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]], [[Michael Jackson]] and [[Whitney Houston]] all had three top 10 singles in 1993, with Houston's cover of "[[I Will Always Love You#Whitney Houston version|I Will Always Love You]]" (from ''[[The Bodyguard: Original Soundtrack Album|The Bodyguard]]'' film soundtrack) spending 10 weeks at number one.

===Chart debuts===
Forty-three artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1993, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, seven went on to record another hit single that year: [[Cappella (band)|Cappella]], [[Culture Beat]], [[Gabrielle (singer)|Gabrielle]], [[Haddaway]], [[Niki Haris]], [[Shabba Ranks]] and [[Urban Cookie Collective]]. [[Chaka Demus & Pliers]] achieved two more top 10 singles in 1993. [[M People]] had three other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Slash (musician)|Slash]] from the American rock band [[Guns N' Roses]] featured on [[Michael Jackson]]'s number 2 single "[[Give In to Me]]", his first hit outside the band.

The [[Right Said Fred]]-fronted [[Comic Relief]] single "[[Stick It Out (Right Said Fred song)|Stick It Out]]" included a collection of entertainment personalities credited as ''and Friends''. Among them were chart debutants comedians [[Clive Anderson]], [[Hugh Laurie]], [[Peter Cook]] and [[Steve Coogan]], radio DJ  [[Alan Freeman]], television presenter [[Jools Holland]], actors [[Bernard Cribbins]], [[Linda Robson]] and [[Pauline Quirke]] and children's character [[Basil Brush]] (voiced by [[Ivan Owen]]).

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[I'm Every Woman]]", "[[I Have Nothing]]", "[[I Will Always Love You]]" (entered in 1992) and "[[Someday (I'm Coming Back)]]" (from ''[[The Bodyguard (soundtrack)|The Bodyguard]]''), "[[Love Song for a Vampire]]" (''[[Bram Stoker's Dracula (1992 film)#Soundtrack|Bram Stoker's Dracula]]''), "[[Can't Help Falling In Love]]" (''[[Sliver (film)|Sliver]]''), "[[Almost Unreal]]" (''[[Super Mario Bros. (film)|Super Mario Bros.]]''), "[[In All the Right Places]]" (''[[Indecent Proposal]]''), "[[Will You Be There]]" (''[[Free Willy]]''), "[[Hero (Mariah Carey song)|Hero]]" (''[[Hero (1992 film)|Hero]]'') and "[[Again (Janet Jackson song)|Again]]" (''[[Poetic Justice (film)|Poetic Justice]]'').

===Charity singles===
The [[Comic Relief]] single in 1993, "[[Stick It Out (Right Said Fred song)|Stick It Out]]", was performed by [[Right Said Fred]] with the help of a group of entertainment personalities on backing vocals. The single featured [[Alan Freeman]], [[Basil Brush]], [[Bernard Cribbins]], [[Clive Anderson]], [[Hugh Laurie]], [[Jools Holland]], [[Linda Robson]], [[Pauline Quirke]], [[Peter Cook]] and [[Steve Coogan]]. It peaked at number four on 20 March 1993 (week ending).

===Best-selling singles===
[[Meat Loaf]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[I'd Do Anything for Love (But I Won't Do That)]]", which spent fourteen weeks in the top 10 (including seven weeks at number-one), sold over 761,000 copies and was certified platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]]. "[[Can't Help Falling in Love#UB40 version|(I Can't Help) Falling in Love with You]]" by [[UB40]] came in second place. [[Ace of Base]]'s "[[All That She Wants]]", "[[No Limit (2 Unlimited song)|No Limit]]" from [[2 Unlimited]] and "[[Dreams (Gabrielle song)|Dreams]] by [[Gabrielle (singer)|Gabrielle]] made up the top five. Singles by [[Mr Blobby]], [[Shaggy (musician)|Shaggy]], [[Haddaway]], [[Culture Beat]] and [[Whitney Houston]] were also in the [[1993 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Twist and Shout|A|a}} "Twist and Shout" reached its peak of number-one on 8 January 1994 (week ending).
*{{note label|Out of Space|B|b}} "Out of Space"/"Ruff in the Jungle Bizness" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 9 January 1993 (week ending). 
*{{note label|Family|C|c}} "We Are Family" originally peaked at number 8 upon its initial release in 1979. It was also re-released in 1984 but failed to reach the top 10, peaking at number 33. It was re-released in a remix version in 1993.
*{{note label|M People|D|d}} "How Can I Love You More?" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 29 upon its initial release in 1991.
*{{note label|Comic Relief|E|e}} Released as the official single for [[Comic Relief]].
*{{note label|Right Said Fred|F|f}} "[[Stick It Out (Right Said Fred song)|Stick It Out]]" was performed by Right Said Fred, alongside a group of entertainers as ''Friends'' including [[Hugh Laurie]], [[Peter Cook]], [[Alan Freeman]], [[Jools Holland]], [[Steve Coogan]], [[Clive Anderson]], [[Pauline Quirke]], [[Linda Robson]], [[Basil Brush]] and [[Bernard Cribbins]].
*{{note label|Shabba|G|g}} "Mr Loverman" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 23 upon its initial release in 1992.
*{{note label|Bluebells|H|h}} "Young at Heart" originally peaked at number 8 upon its initial release in 1984.
*{{note label|Sweat|I|i}} "Sweat (La La La La Long)" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 43 upon its initial release in 1992.
*{{note label|Housecall|J|j}} "Housecall" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 31 upon its initial release in 1991.
*{{note label|Survive|K|k}} "I Will Survive" originally peaked at number-one upon its initial release in 1979. It was released as a remix in a similar way to "We Are Family" in 1993.
*{{note label|Freddie|L|l}} The original version of "Living on My Own" peaked outside the top ten at number 50 upon its release in 1985.
*{{note label|Relax|M|m}} "Relax" originally peaked at number-one upon its initial release in 1983. It was one of a number of Frankie Goes to Hollywood songs which were re-issued in 1993.
*{{note label|Roxette|N|n}} "It Must Have Been Love" originally peaked at number 3 upon its initial release in 1990.
*{{note label|Hero|O|o}} "Hero" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 27 November 1993 (week ending).
*{{note label|Orb|P|p}} "Little Fluffy Clouds" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 87 upon its initial release in 1990.
*{{note label|Train|Q|q}} "Long Train Runnin'" was first released in 1973 but did not chart in the UK. It was re-released in 1993 with a new remix.
*{{note label|Mr Blobby|R|r}} Mr Blobby was a character from the television variety series ''[[Noel's House Party]]'', played by Barry Killerby. The ''[[Mr Blobby (song)|self-titled novelty song]]'' became the Christmas number-one single for 1993.
*{{note label|Duets|S|s}} "I've Got You Under My Skin" was recorded as a duet by [[Frank Sinatra]] and [[Bono]] from [[U2]], for the album ''[[Duets (Frank Sinatra album)|Duets]]''. It was released as a double-A side single alongside "Stay (Faraway, So Close!)", performed by Bono and the rest of U2.
*{{note label|Frank Sinatra|T|t}} Frank Sinatra only appeared on "I Got You Under My Skin" on the double-A side, with Bono featuring on both, the other single with his bandmates.
*{{note label|Bat|U|u}} "Bat Out of Hell" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 15 upon its initial release in 1979.
*{{note label|Power|V|v}} "The Power of Love" originally peaked at number-one upon its initial release in 1984. It was one of a number of Frankie Goes to Hollywood songs which were re-issued in 1993.
*{{note label|1993 peak|W|w}} Figure includes single that first charted in 1992 but peaked in 1993.
*{{note label|1994|X|x}} Figure includes single that peaked in 1994.
*{{note label|1992|Y|y}} Figure includes single that peaked in 1992.
*{{note label|Queen|Z|z}} Figure includes a top 10 hit with the group Queen.
*{{note label|Niki Haris|AA|aa}} Figure includes appearances on Snap!'s "Exterminate!" and "Do You See the Light (Looking For)".
*{{note label|Sybil|BB|bb}} Figure includes appearance on West End's "The Love I Lost".
