[[File:Cowboy humpy 10-31-2008.JPG|thumb|right|200px|[[Engelbert Humperdinck (singer)|Engelbert Humperdinck]] made his arrival into the [[UK Singles Chart]] in 1967, scoring three top 10 singles, including the number-one hits "[[Release Me (Eddie Miller song)#Engelbert Humperdinck version|Release Me]]" and "[[The Last Waltz]]". "Release Me" became the year's best-selling single and went on to be ranked as the [[List of best-selling singles of the 1960s in the United Kingdom|eighth biggest-seller of the 1960s]].]]
[[File:The Monkees 1966.JPG|thumb|right|200px|[[The Monkees]] reached the UK chart with four top 10 singles this year, with their debut entry "[[I'm a Believer]]" spending four weeks at number-one.]]
[[File:The Tremeloes.png|thumb|right|200px|[[The Tremeloes]] had three top 10 singles in 1967, the highest-charting of which was chart-topper "[[Silence Is Golden (song)#The Tremeloes version|Silence is Golden]]".]]
The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1967 in British music|1967]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1966|1966]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1968|1968]] but were in the top 10 in 1967. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and fifteen singles were in the top ten in 1967. Eight singles from 1966 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Daydream Believer]]" by [[The Monkees]], "[[Thank U Very Much (The Scaffold song)|Thank U Very Much]]" by [[The Scaffold]] and "[[Walk Away Renée#Four Tops cover|Walk Away Renée]]" by [[Four Tops]] were all released in 1967 but did not reach their peak until 1968. Twenty-nine artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1967. [[Aretha Franklin]], [[Bee Gees]], [[Cat Stevens]], [[Engelbert Humperdinck (singer)|Engelbert Humperdinck]], [[The Monkees]] and [[Stevie Wonder]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1967.

The 1966 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Green, Green Grass of Home#Tom Jones version|Green, Green Grass of Home]]" by [[Tom Jones (singer)|Tom Jones]], remained at number one for the first two weeks of 1967. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[I'm A Believer]]" by [[The Monkees]]. Overall, fourteen different singles peaked at number-one in 1967, with [[Engelbert Humperdinck (singer)|Engelbert Humperdinck]] and [[The Beatles]] (2) having the joint most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and sixteen singles charted in the top 10 in 1967, with one-hundred and five singles reaching their peak this year. "[[This is My Song (1967 song)|This is My Song]]" was recorded by [[Petula Clark]] and [[Harry Secombe]] and both versions reached the top 10.

Twenty-nine artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1967. [[Tom Jones (singer)|Tom Jones]] secured the record for most top 10 hits in 1967 with five hit singles.

[[Nancy Sinatra]] was one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Somethin' Stupid]]" (with [[Frank Sinatra]]). [[The Beach Boys]], [[Cat Stevens]], [[Procol Harum]], [[The Rolling Stones]] and [[Val Doonican]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1967.

===Chart debuts===
Thirty-three artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1967, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, three went on to record another hit single that year: [[Bee Gees]], [[Cat Stevens]] and [[Procol Harum]]. [[Engelbert Humperdinck (singer)|Engelbert Humperdinck]], [[The Jimi Hendrix Experience]], [[The Move]] and [[Traffic (band)|Traffic]] all had two more top 10 singles in 1967. [[The Monkees]] had three other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Harry Secombe]] had previously achieved two top 10 singles as a member of [[The Goon Show|The Goons]], but his cover version of "[[This is My Song (1967 song)#Other versions|This is My Song]]" marked his first and only top 10 appearance as a solo artist. [[Dave Davies]] was the lead guitarist for the rock band [[The Kinks]]. "[[Death of a Clown]]" was his debut solo single, peaking at number three. "[[Reflections (The Supremes song)|Reflections]]" was the first single for The Supremes under their new name, Diana Ross & the Supremes. "[[San Franciscan Nights]]" was billed as [[Eric Burdon]] and [[The Animals]] - Burdon was a vocalist in the group.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[The Happening (song)|The Happening]]" (from ''[[The Happening (1967 film)|The Happening]]''), "[[Here We Go Round the Mulberry Bush]]" (''[[Here We Go Round the Mulberry Bush (film)|Here We Go Round the Mulberry Bush]]'') and "I've Been a Bad, Bad Boy" (''[[Privilege (film)|Privilege]]'').

Additionally, [[Vince Hill]] recorded a version of "[[Edelweiss (song)|Edelweiss]]" from ''[[The Sound of Music (film)|The Sound of Music]]'', with the song reaching number two in the chart.

===Best-selling singles===
Until 1970 there was no universally recognised year-end best-sellers list. However, in 2011 the Official Charts Company released a list of the best-selling single of each year in chart history from 1952 to date. According to the list, "[[Release Me (Eddie Miller song)#Engelbert Humperdinck version|Release Me]]" by [[Engelbert Humperdinck (singer)|Engelbert Humperdinck]] is officially recorded as the [[List of best-selling singles by year in the United Kingdom|biggest-selling single]] of 1967. "Release Me" (8) also ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 1960s in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Daydream Believer|A|a}} "Daydream Believer" reached its peak of number five on 10 January 1968 (week ending).
*{{note label|Scaffold|B|b}} "Thank U Very Much" reached its peak of number four on 3 January 1968 (week ending).
*{{note label|My Mind's Eye|C|c}} "My Mind's Eye" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 5 January 1967 (week ending).
*{{note label|The Supremes|D|d}} The Supremes' name was altered to [[Diana Ross]] & The Supremes in 1967.
*{{note label|Brokenhearted|E|e}} "What Becomes of the Brokenhearted" re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 4 January 1967 (week ending).
*{{note label|Puppet on a String|F|f}} "Puppet on a String" was the [[United Kingdom in the Eurovision Song Contest 1967|United Kingdom]]'s winning entry at the [[Eurovision Song Contest]] in [[Eurovision Song Contest 1967|1967]].
*{{note label|The Monkees|G|g}} "Alternate Title" was originally known as "Randy Scouse Git" but The Monkees' record label ([[RCA Corporation|RCA]]) asked for it to be changed. "Randy Scouse Git" was a reference from the sitcom ''[[Till Death Us Do Part]]''.
*{{note label|Beatles|H|h}} "All You Need Is Love" was used as the UK's contribution to the first live global television programme, ''[[Our World (TV special)|Our World]]''. For the show it was performed over a pre-recorded backing track.
*{{note label|Johnny Mann|I|i}} "Up, Up and Away" re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 9 August 1967 (week ending) for 3 weeks.

*{{note label|All My Love|K|k}} "All My Love" re-entered the top 10 at number 7 on 19 December 1967 (week ending) for 2 weeks.
