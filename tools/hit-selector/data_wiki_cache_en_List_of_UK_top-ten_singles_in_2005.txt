The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Since 2004 the chart has been based on the sales of both [[CD single|physical singles]] and [[music download|digital downloads]], with [[airplay]] figures excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]]|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|edition=18th |publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2005 in British music charts|2005]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2004|2004]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2006|2006]] but were in the top 10 in 2005. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Two hundred and twenty-eight singles were in the top ten in 2005. Ten singles from 2004 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[When I'm Gone (Eminem song)|When I'm Gone]]" by [[Eminem]] was released in 2005 but did not reach its peak until 2006.<ref name = "Gone">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4592976.stm|title=Shayne tops chart for third week|date=8 January 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=4 June 2017}}</ref> Fifty-two artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2005.  [[Akon]], [[Arctic Monkeys]], [[James Blunt]], [[Rihanna]], [[Kaiser Chiefs]] and [[The Pussycat Dolls]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 2005.

To mark [[Elvis Presley]]'s 70th birthday, all eighteen of his UK number-one singles were re-released. The first single, "[[All Shook Up]]", came with a collectors' box that made it ineligible to chart again; each of the other seventeen reissues hit the UK top 5.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1050223/new-presley-reissue-campaign-aimed-at-uk|title=New Presley Reissue Campaign Aimed at U.K.|date=3 August 2007|magazine=Billboard|accessdate=22 July 2019}}</ref> Two of the three re-released singles which topped the chart - "[[One Night (Elvis Presley song)|One Night]]"/"[[I Got Stung]]" and "[[It's Now or Never (song)|It's Now or Never]]" - were also the first and second singles in chart history to spend their only week in the top 10 at number-one before dropping out.

The 2004 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]" by [[Band Aid 20]], remained at number-one for the first week of 2005.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4126843.stm|title=Band Aid 20 holds on to top slot|date=26 December 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=4 June 2017}}</ref> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Against All Odds (Take a Look at Me Now)#Steve Brookstein version|Against All Odds]]" by ''[[The X Factor (UK series 1)|2004 X Factor]]'' winner [[Steve Brookstein]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4141883.stm|title=X Factor star ends Band Aid reign|date=2 January 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=4 June 2017}}</ref> Overall, twenty-eight different singles peaked at number-one in 2005, with [[Elvis Presley]] (3) having the most singles hit that position.<ref name = "Jailhouse Rock">{{cite news |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/4159735.stm |title=Elvis regains top chart position |work=BBC News |access-date=4 June 2017|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=9 January 2005}}</ref><ref name = "One Night">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/4179669.stm|title=Elvis claims 1,000th number one|date=16 January 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=4 June 2017}}</ref><ref name = "Never">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4223451.stm|title=Elvis returns to No 1 in charts|date=31 January 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=4 June 2017}}</ref>

==Background==
===Elvis Presley sets chart records===
[[Elvis Presley]] set several chart records in 2005. With a selection of his back-catalogue (all his UK number-one singles) being reissued to mark what would have been the late singer's 70th birthday, his seventeen entries were the most by any artist in the same year. It remains a record as of 2024, with [[Ed Sheeran]]'s [[List of UK top 10 singles in 2017|2017]] haul of eleven top 10s in a calendar year the closest anyone has come since (Sheeran had seventeen singles in the top 20 that year).

On 16 January 2005 (22 January 2005, week ending), the re-release of Presley's 1959 chart-topper "[[One Night (Elvis Presley song)|One Night]]"/"[[I Got Stung]]" became the 1000th number-one in the [[UK Singles Chart]]. "One Night"/"I Got Stung" also became one of the first singles to drop out of the top 10 entirely the week after topping the chart. The feat was repeated by another Presley classic, “[[It's Now or Never (song)|It's Now or Never]]”, with its only week in the top ten spent on top of the chart. “[[Baby's Coming Back]]”/[[Baby's Coming Back/Transylvania|Transylvania]]” by [[McFly]] (2007) was the next single to join Presley on the list.

===Multiple entries===
Two hundred and twenty-eight singles charted in 2005, with two-hundred and seventeen singles reaching their peak this year (including the re-entry "[[Fairytale of New York]]" and a series of [[Elvis Presley]] re-releases which charted in previous years but reached a peak on their latest chart run). [[Cabin Crew (band)|Cabin Crew]] and [[Sunset Strippers]] both reached the charts with a remixed version of "[[Waiting for a Star to Fall]]", under the title "Star to Fall" and "Falling Stars" respectively.

Fifty-two artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2005. [[Elvis Presley]] had the most top ten singles in 2005 with seventeen in total. [[50 Cent]] and [[Girls Aloud]] both recorded five top ten hit singles this year. The former spent eleven weeks in the top 10 with his five entries, which included a collaboration on [[Game (rapper)|The Game]]'s number four single "[[Hate It or Love It]]". Girls Aloud's "[[I'll Stand by You#Girls Aloud version|I'll Stand by You]]" originally charted at number-one at the end of 2004 in support of [[Children in Need]] and spent a couple more weeks near the top of the chart in January 2005.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4030705.stm|title=Girls Aloud seize number one slot|date=21 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Wake Me Up (Girls Aloud song)|Wake Me Up]]" was their next hit when it reached number four in March.<ref name = "Wake" /> "[[Long Hot Summer (Girls Aloud song)|Long Hot Summer]]" took seventh spot in September,<ref name = "Oasis">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4193272.stm|title=Oasis single hits number one spot|date=28 August 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Biology (song)|Biology]]"<ref name = "Hung Up">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4454818.stm|title=Madonna in chart double success|date=20 November 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> was another number four entry and they finished the year with "[[See the Day]]" peaking at number ten.<ref name = "That's My Goal">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4559230.stm|title=X Factor star in festive top spot|date=25 December 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Chris Martin]] and [[Bono]]'s four top ten hits include their vocals on [[Band Aid 20]] at the end of 2004.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4012975.stm|title=Band Aid 20: The group photo|date=15 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> Martin had three top tens with his band [[Coldplay]] in 2005 - "[[Speed of Sound (song)|Speed of Sound]]" (2),<ref name = "Crazy">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4591685.stm|title=Crazy Frog hops to chart success|date=29 May 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Fix You]]" (4)<ref name = "Don't Cha">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/4235812.stm|title=Pussycats triumph over Gorillaz|date=11 September 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Talk (Coldplay song)|Talk]]" (10).<ref name = "That's My Goal" /> Similarly, Bono's other successful singles were as part of [[U2]], the best performing being the number-one single "[[Sometimes You Can't Make It On Your Own]]" in February.<ref name = "Sometimes" /> "[[City of Blinding Lights]]" (2) just missed the top spot in June<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4086328.stm|title=Coldplay storm into album chart|date=12 June 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[All Because of You (U2 song)|All Because of You]]" in October peaked at number four.<ref name = "Friday Hill">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4347784.stm|title=Sugababes secure UK chart double|date=16 October 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref>

American rapper [[Eminem]] also had four top ten hits in the UK across 2005. "[[Like Toy Soldiers]]" became the sixth number-one single of his career in February,<ref name = "Lonesome" /> after "[[The Real Slim Shady]]", "[[Stan (song)|Stan]]" (both 2000), "[[Without Me (Eminem song)|Without Me]]", "[[Lose Yourself]]" (both 2002) and "[[Just Lose It]]" (2004). "[[Ass Like That]]",<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4733419.stm|title=Singer Blunt keeps grip on charts|date=31 July 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Mockingbird (Eminem song)|Mockingbird]]"<ref name = "Lonely">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4527755.stm|title=Akon topples Christie chart reign|date=8 May 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[When I'm Gone (Eminem song)|When I'm Gone]]"<ref name = "Gone" /> all reached number four. Charity single for [[Comic Relief]], [[All About You/You've Got a Friend|"All About You"/"You've Got a Friend"]],<ref name = "Charm" /> and "[[I'll Be OK]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4171790.stm|title=McFly knock Blunt off top spot|date=21 August 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> both topped the chart, while "[[I Wanna Hold You]]" made number three.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/4369856.stm|title=Arctic Monkeys' debut tops charts|date=23 October 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> They ended the year with number 9 hit [[Ultraviolet/The Ballad of Paul K|"Ultraviolet"/"The Ballad of Paul K"]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/4540514.stm|title=JCB single beats Westlife to top|date=18 December 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref>

Girl group [[Sugababes]] was one of a number of artists with three top-ten entries, including number one "[[Push the Button (Sugababes song)|Push the Button]]".<ref name = "Push">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/4302728.stm|title=Sugababes get fourth number one|date=2 October 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> [[Akon]], [[Ciara]], [[Kaiser Chiefs]], [[Oasis (band)|Oasis]], [[Robbie Williams]] and [[The White Stripes]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 2005.

===Chart debuts===
Seventy artists achieved their first top 10 single in 2005, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, four went on to record another hit single that year: [[Bloc Party]], [[Crazy Frog]], [[Goldfrapp]] and [[The Pussycat Dolls]]. [[Akon]], [[Ciara]], [[The Game (rapper)|The Game]] and [[Kaiser Chiefs]] all had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top 10 in the charts in 2005. The [[Comic Relief (charity)|Comic Relief]] single was the double-A side [[All About You/You've Got a Friend|"All About You"/"You've Got a Friend"]] by [[McFly]], peaking at number-one on 19 March 2005.<ref name = "Charm" />

A second single was released in aid of Comic Relief, a re-release of [[Tony Christie]]'s "[[(Is This the Way to) Amarillo#Tony Christie featuring Peter Kay version|(Is This the Way to) Amarillo]]", featuring comedian [[Peter Kay]], who shot a music video featuring various celebrities and was credited on the release. It topped the charts on 26 March 2005 and spent eleven weeks in the top 10, including seven at number-one.<ref name = "Amarillo" />

[[Liberty X]] recorded the [[Children in Need]] single for 2005, "[[A Night to Remember (Shalamar song)|A Night to Remember]]". It was the eighth top 10 single of their career and reached number six on 26 November 2005.<ref name="Hung Up"/>

[[Band Aid 20]]'s "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]", first released in 2004, also spent several weeks in the top 10 at the beginning of the year, having peaked at number-one on 11 December 2004.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4126843.stm|title=Band Aid 20 holds on to top slot|date=26 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref>

===Best-selling singles===
[[Tony Christie]] featuring [[Peter Kay]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[(Is This the Way to) Amarillo#Tony Christie featuring Peter Kay version|(Is This the Way to) Amarillo]]". The song spent eleven weeks in the top 10 (including seven weeks at number one), sold over 1.1 million copies and was certified 2× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]] (July 2013).<ref name = "Amarillo" /> "[[That's My Goal]]" by [[Shayne Ward]] came in second place, selling more than 874,000 copies and losing out by around 226,000 sales.<ref name = "That's My Goal" /> [[Crazy Frog]]'s "[[Axel F#Crazy Frog version|Axel F]]",<ref name = "Crazy" /> "[[You're Beautiful]]" from [[James Blunt]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/4692177.stm|title=Blunt climbs to number one|date=18 July 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Don't Cha]]" by [[The Pussycat Dolls]] featuring [[Busta Rhymes]] made up the top five.<ref name = "Don't Cha" /> Singles by [[McFly]],<ref name = "Charm" /> [[Akon]]<ref name = "Lonely" /> [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]],<ref name = "Hung Up" /> [[Westlife]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4390986.stm|title=Westlife score 13th chart topper|date=30 October 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref> and [[Sugababes]]<ref name = "Push" /> were also in the [[2005 in British music charts#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/4575226.stm|title=Amarillo tops 2005 single sales|date=2 January 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=19 June 2017}}</ref>

"(Is This the Way to) Amarillo" (3) and "That's My Goal" (8) were both ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 2000s (decade) in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Entries by artist==
{{see also|List of UK Singles Chart number ones of the 2000s#2005}}
[[File:Eminem at DJ hero party with d12.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Eminem]] was a four-time entrant to the UK top 10 in 2005. The highest charting of these entries was "[[Like Toy Soldiers]]", which was a number-one hit in February.]]
[[File:McFly group.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[McFly]] recorded this year's [[Comic Relief]] single, "[[All About You/You've Got a Friend|All About You"/"You've Got a Friend]]". The song stayed at number one for a single week in March. The group had a total of four top 10 hits this year, with "[[I'll Be OK]]" also topping the chart.]]
[[File:Tim McGraw October 24 2015.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Country music singer [[Tim McGraw]] provided guest vocals on [[Nelly]]'s 2005 number-one hit "[[Over and Over (Nelly song)|Over and Over]]". As of 2021, it remains McGraw's only top 40 single in the UK.]]
[[File:Charlotte Church cropped.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Welsh singer [[Charlotte Church]] appeared in the top 10 with two singles this year. The most successful of these was "[[Crazy Chick]]", which reached number two in July.]]
[[File:James Bourne Pigs Can Fly Newmarket 2016.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Former [[Busted (band)|Busted]] singer [[James Bourne]] hit the top 10 in November 2005 with his new band [[Son of Dork]], their debut single "[[Ticket Outta Loserville]]" reaching number three. He also featured on the Band Aid 20 single.]]
[[File:Jessica Simpson Joining Forces with the Rockies April 2011 (cropped).jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Jessica Simpson]] recorded a cover of "[[These Boots Are Made for Walking]]" for the ''[[The Dukes of Hazzard (film)|Dukes of Hazzard]]'' film soundtrack. It reached number four in the UK in September of this year.]]

The figures include both main artists and featured artists, while appearances on ensemble charity records are also counted for each artist. The total number of weeks an artist spent in the top ten in 2005 is also shown.
