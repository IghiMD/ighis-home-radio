The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Since 2004 the chart has been based on the sales of both [[CD single|physical singles]] and [[music download|digital downloads]], with [[airplay]] figures excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref>  This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2008 in British music charts|2008]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2007|2007]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2009|2009]] but were in the top 10 in 2008. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and twenty-two singles were in the top ten in 2008. Nine singles from 2007 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Broken Strings (song)|Broken Strings]]" by [[James Morrison (singer)|James Morrison]] and [[Nelly Furtado]] was released in 2008 but did not reach its peak until 2009.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7823068.stm|title=Number one single for Lady GaGa|date=11 January 2009|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Crank That (Soulja Boy)|Crank That]]" by [[Soulja Boy]] was the only song from 2007 to reach its peak in 2008.<ref name = "Soulja Boy">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7173993.stm|title=Radiohead CD tops UK album chart|date=6 January 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> Twenty-nine artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2008. [[Adele]], [[Flo Rida]], [[Katy Perry]], [[Kings of Leon]], [[The Script]] and [[The Saturdays]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 2008.

The 2007 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[When You Believe#Leon Jackson version|When You Believe]]" by ''[[The X Factor (UK series 4)|2007 X Factor]]'' winner [[Leon Jackson]], remained at number-one for the first two weeks of 2008.<ref name = "Soulja Boy" /> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Now You're Gone (Basshunter song)|Now You're Gone]]" by [[Basshunter]] featuring [[Mental Theo|DJ Mental Theo's Bazzheadz]].<ref name = "Basshunter">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7186208.stm|title=MacDonald tops the UK album chart|date=13 January 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> Overall, twenty different singles peaked at number-one in 2008, with [[Alexandra Burke]] (2) having the most singles hit that position (including her credit on "[[Hero (Mariah Carey song)#The X Factor Finalists 2008 version|Hero]]" with [[The X Factor (UK series 5)|The X Factor Finalists 2008]]).<ref name = "Hero">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7705172.stm|title=X Factor single tops UK charts|date=2 November 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref><ref name = "Hallelujah">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7794709.stm|title=Christmas double for Hallelujah|date=22 December 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref>

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and twenty-two singles charted in the top 10 in 2008, with one-hundred and twelve singles reaching their peak this year. Two versions of the [[Leonard Cohen]] song "[[Hallelujah (Leonard Cohen song)|Hallelujah]]" - [[Alexandra Burke]]'s ''[[The X Factor|X Factor]]'' winners single and [[Jeff Buckley]]'s 1994 recording which was re-released in 2007 - reached the top 10.

Twenty-eight artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2008. [[Cheryl Cole]], [[Leona Lewis]] and [[Rihanna]] shared the record for most top 10 hits in 2008 with four hit singles each.

===Chart debuts===
Fifty-three artists achieved their first top 10 single in 2008, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, eight went on to record another hit single that year: [[Alexandra Burke]], [[Basshunter]], [[Duffy (singer)|Duffy]], [[Peter Kay|Geraldine McQueen]], [[Katy Perry]], [[Kings of Leon]], [[The Saturdays]] and [[The Ting Tings]].

[[Chris Martin]] had his first official credit outside [[Coldplay]] (aside from the [[Band Aid 20]] charity single in 2004) singing vocals on [[Kanye West]]'s "[[Homecoming (Kanye West song)|Homecoming]]" which peaked at number 9.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.xxlmag.com/news/2016/08/lil-yachty-chris-martin-coldpaly/|work=XXL Mag|title=Lil Yachty wants to work with Chris Martin and Coldplay|last=Simmons|first=Ted|date=9 August 2016|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref> [[Alex Turner (musician)|Alex Turner]] from [[The Last Shadow Puppets]] had previous chart success as part of the [[Arctic Monkeys]] but "[[The Age of the Understatement (song)|The Age of the Understatement]]" was his first chart entry with his new group.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/features/story-of-the-song-the-age-of-the-understatement-the-last-shadow-puppets-2008-1799631.html|title=Story of the song: The age of the understatement, The Last Shadow Puppets (2008)|last=Webb|first=Robert|date=8 October 2009|work=The Independent|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Frankie Sandford]] and [[Rochelle Humes]] of [[The Saturdays]] were formerly members of [[S Club Juniors]] (later known as S Club 8) who had multiple top 10 entries in 2002 and 2003.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.irishmirror.ie/showbiz/celebrity-news/s-club-juniors-stacey-honeymoon-10609357|title=S Club Junior's Stacey McClean defiantly enjoys a honeymoon cocktail after dismissing claims of drunken chaos at wedding|last=Martin|first=Lara|date=12 June 2017|work=Irish Mirror|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref> [[Peter Kay]], who played the character Geraldine McQueen, had previous chart credits on "[[Is This the Way to Amarillo]] in 2005 and "[[I'm Gonna Be (500 Miles)|(I'm Gonna Be) 500 Miles]]" in 2007, but "[[The Winner's Song]]" was his first entry as McQueen.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/newsbeat/hi/entertainment/newsid_7667000/7667153.stm|title=Geraldine aims for the top spot|date=13 October 2008|work=Newsbeat|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Estelle (musician)|Estelle]] had her first chart hit in 2008 on "[[American Boy]]" with [[Kanye West]] but she was a vocalist on the number-one [[Band Aid 20]] single "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]" in 2004.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.digitalspy.com/music/feature/a801986/whatever-happened-to-estelle/|title=Whatever happened to Estelle?|last=Corner|first=Lewis|date=20 July 2016|work=Digital Spy|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref> [[Alexandra Burke]] sang on "[[Hero (Mariah Carey song)#The X Factor 2008 Finalists version|Hero]]" with her fellow ''[[The X Factor (UK)|X Factor]]'' [[The X Factor (UK series 5)|series five]] contestants prior to her debut solo hit "[[Hallelujah (Leonard Cohen song)#Alexandra Burke|Hallelujah]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/celebritynews/4013996/X-Factor-winner-Alexandra-Burkes-Hallelujah-is-biggest-selling-single-of-2008.html|title=X Factor winner Alexandra Burke's 'Hallelujah' is biggest-selling single of 2008|date=29 December 2008|work=The Telegraph|publisher=Telegraph Media Group|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref> [[JLS]] and [[Diana Vickers]] would both go on to record number-one singles in later years.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/reality-stars-second-place-olly-murs-susan-boyle_uk_5873a7e4e4b0a1ff7042d7cd|title=14 Stars Who Made The Most Out Of Finishing In Second Place|last=Welsh|first=Daniel|date=9 January 2017|work=Huffington Post|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.digitalspy.com/music/feature/a813507/whatever-happened-to-diana-vickers/|title=Whatever happened to Diana Vickers?|last=Kheraj|first=Alim|date=8 November 2016|work=Digital Spy|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Alesha Dixon]] recorded her first solo top 10 in 2008, "[[The Boy Does Nothing]]" which peaked at number five. Her previous chart entries were as part of girl group [[Mis-Teeq]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.radiotimes.com/news/2017-05-20/everything-you-need-to-know-about-britains-got-talent-judge-alesha-dixon|title=Everything you need to know about Britain's Got Talent judge Alesha Dixon|last=Griffiths|first=Eleanor Bley|work=Radio Times|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref> After chart success with [[The White Stripes]] and [[The Raconteurs]], [[Jack White]] peaked at number 9 with his solo single "[[Another Way to Die]]" with [[Alicia Keys]], recorded for the ''[[Quantum of Solace]]'' soundtrack.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/celebritynews/2473735/James-Bond-Quantum-of-Solace-theme-tune-sung-by-Alicia-Keys-and-Jack-White.html|title=James Bond Quantum of Solace theme tune sung by Alicia Keys and Jack White|last=Singh|first=Anita|date=30 July 2008|work=The Telegraph|publisher=Telegraph Media Group|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref>

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top 10 in the charts in 2008. The Sport Relief single was "[[Footprints in the Sand (Leona Lewis song)|Footprints in the Sand]]" by [[Leona Lewis]], which was released as a double-A side single with "[[Better in Time]]". It peaked at number two on 22 March 2008 after a performance on the telethon.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/newsbeat/hi/entertainment/newsid_7286000/7286965.stm|title=Leona Lewis does Sport Relief in South Africa|last=Jamieson|first=Natalie|date=10 March 2008|work=Newsbeat|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref>

The [[List of The X Factor finalists (UK series 5)|finalists]] from the [[The X Factor (UK series 5)|fifth series]] of ''[[The X Factor (UK)|The X Factor]]'' (which included winner [[Alexandra Burke]], [[JLS]] and [[Diana Vickers]]) got together for a single, "[[Hero (Mariah Carey song)|Hero]]", which had been a hit for [[Mariah Carey]]. It reached number one on 8 November 2008, with profits going to benefit [[Help for Heroes]] and [[The Royal British Legion]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nme.com/news/music/girls-aloud-49-1324805|title=X Factor finalists are number one|date=2 November 2008|work=NME|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref>

[[McFly]]'s official [[Children in Need]] single for 2008, "[[Do Ya/Stay with Me]]", only reached number 18 in the chart.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/the-official-biggest-selling-children-in-need-singles-revealed__17116/|title=The Official biggest selling Children In Need singles revealed|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=17 November 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref> However [[Terry Wogan|Sir Terry Wogan]] and [[Aled Jones]] ranked at number 3 with a cover version of "[[Peace on Earth/Little Drummer Boy]]", originally by [[David Bowie]] and [[Bing Crosby]], for the same cause on 20 December 2008.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/celebritynews/3521237/Sir-Terry-Wogan-and-Aled-Jones-wade-into-Christmas-chart-battle.html|title=Sir Terry Wogan and Aled Jones wade into Christmas chart battle|date=25 November 2008|work=The Telegraph|publisher=Telegraph Media Group|accessdate=27 June 2017}}</ref>

===Best-selling singles===
[[Alexandra Burke]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Hallelujah (Leonard Cohen song)|Hallelujah]]". The song spent five weeks in the top 10 (including three weeks at number one), sold over 887,000 copies and was certified platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref name = "Hallelujah" /> "[[Hero (Mariah Carey song)#The X Factor UK 2008 finalists version|Hero]]" by [[List of The X Factor finalists (UK series 5)|The X Factor Finalists 2008]] came in second place, selling more than 751,000 copies and losing out by around 136,000 sales.<ref name = "Hero" /> [[Duffy (singer)|Duffy]]'s "[[Mercy (Duffy song)|Mercy]]",<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7261763.stm|title=Duffy's Mercy tops UK chart again|date=24 February 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> "[[I Kissed a Girl]]" from [[Katy Perry]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7603150.stm|title=Perry's Girl stays at number one|date=7 September 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Rockstar (Nickelback song)|Rockstar]]" by [[Nickelback]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7494848.stm|title=Nickelback sign Live Nation deal|date=8 July 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> made up the top five. Singles by [[Estelle (musician)|Estelle]] featuring [[Kanye West]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7310592.stm|title=Singer Estelle tops singles chart|date=23 March 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> [[Kings of Leon]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/mobile/entertainment/7640893.stm|title=Kings of Leon reign over charts|date=28 September 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> [[Basshunter]],<ref name = "Basshunter" /> [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]] & [[Justin Timberlake]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/7394927.stm|title=German techno band topple Madonna|date=11 May 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> and [[Sam Sparro]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7383141.stm|title=Madonna celebrates chart double|date=4 May 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> were also in the [[2008 in British music charts#Best-selling singles of 2008|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7802941.stm|title=Duffy and Burke top 2008 charts|date=29 December 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref>

"Hallelujah" (5) was also ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 2000s (decade) in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].
