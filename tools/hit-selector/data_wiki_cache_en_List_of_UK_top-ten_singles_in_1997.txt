[[File:Elton John 2011 Shankbone 2.JPG|thumb|right|200px|[[Elton John]] (pictured in 2011) achieved the best-selling single of 1997 with "[[Something About the Way You Look Tonight]]"/"[[Candle in the Wind 1997]]", a tribute to [[Diana, Princess of Wales]]. The song entered the charts at number-one in September, where it remained for five weeks, ultimately spending 13 non-consecutive weeks in the top 10. It remains [[List of best-selling singles in the United Kingdom|the best-selling single in UK chart history]] with over 4.77 million copies sold.]]
[[File:Aqua Gron Concert 2008.jpg|thumb|right|200px|Danish-Norwegian group [[Aqua (band)|Aqua]] (pictured in 2008) scored the second best selling single of the year with their signature song "[[Barbie Girl]]", which topped the chart for four weeks and spent 12 weeks in the top 10.]]
[[File:Diddy.jpg|thumb|right|200px|In 1997, [[Puff Daddy]] (pictured in 2007) recorded a tribute song to his recently deceased friend, [[The Notorious B.I.G.]], called "[[I'll Be Missing You]]". Sampling [[The Police]]'s 1983 hit "[[Every Breath You Take]]", the track features vocals from Biggie's widow, singer [[Faith Evans]], and R&B group [[112 (band)|112]]. The song spent 12 weeks in the UK top 10, with six of those weeks at number-one, becoming the third best selling single of the year.]]
The [[UK singles chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK singles chart]] during [[1997 in British music|1997]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1996|1996]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1998|1998]] but were in the top 10 in 1997. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Two hundred and twenty-eight singles were in the top ten in 1997. Ten singles from 1996 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year. "[[Angels (Robbie Williams song)|Angels]]" by [[Robbie Williams]] and "[[Never Ever (All Saints song)|Never Ever]]" by [[All Saints (band)|All Saints]] were both released in 1997 but did not reach their peak until 1998.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/robbie-williams-official-top-40-biggest-selling-solo-singles-revealed__3763/|title=Robbie Williams’ Official Top 40 Biggest Selling Solo Singles revealed|date=13 February 2014|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref><ref name = "All Saints">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/everything-you-need-to-know-about-all-saints__13875/|title=Everything you need to know about All Saints|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=4 April 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> Sixty-four artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1997. [[All Saints (group)|All Saints]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.digitalspy.com/music/feature/a779716/all-saints-greatest-hits-all-10-of-their-singles-ranked-from-worst-to-best/|title=All Saints' greatest hits: All 10 of their singles ranked from worst to best|last=Corner|first=Lewis|date=13 January 2016|work=Digital Spy|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> [[Aqua (band)|Aqua]],<ref name="Aqua">{{cite web|url=http://www.digitalspy.com/music/news/a807775/aqua-back-together-barbie-girl-anniversary/|title=Aqua are getting back together for the 20th anniversary of 'Barbie Girl'|last=Anderton|first=Joe|date=13 September 2016|work=Digital Spy|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> [[Daft Punk]],<ref name = "Daft Punk">{{cite web|url=https://www.nme.com/blogs/nme-blogs/albums-turning-20-in-2017-1942223|title=27 Albums Turning 20 in 2017|last=Smith|first=Thomas|date=12 January 2017|work=NME.com|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> [[Natalie Imbruglia]]<ref name = "Torn">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/natalie-imbruglia-interview-i-knew-id-be-singing-torn-forever__11160/|title=Natalie Imbruglia interview: "I knew I'd be singing Torn forever"|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=5 November 2015|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> and [[No Doubt]]<ref name = "No Doubt">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/eminem-and-no-doubt-score-uks-latest-million-selling-singles__11655/|title=Eminem and No Doubt score UK's latest million-selling singles|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=14 November 2015|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1997.

[[Elton John]] recorded a [[Candle in the Wind 1997|reworking]] of his 1973 single "[[Candle in the Wind]] as a tribute to [[Diana, Princess of Wales]], who [[Death of Diana, Princess of Wales|died]] in 1997.<ref name = "Candle">{{cite news |title=Diana fund given £20m boost |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/38582.stm |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=10 December 1997 |access-date=4 June 2012}}</ref> Released as a double-A side with "[[Something About the Way You Look Tonight]]" and spending five weeks at number-one, it sold almost 4.8 million copies and remains (as of 2018) the [[List of best-selling singles (UK)|best-selling single of all-time]].<ref>{{cite news |title=Elton's candle burns in Canada |url=http://newscdn.bbc.net.uk/1/hi/entertainment/471353.stm |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=11 October 1999 |accessdate=4 June 2012}}</ref> The other major event to impact the charts in 1997 was the passing of [[The Notorious B.I.G]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://theblast.com/diddy-faith-evans-biggie-smalls-anniversary-bad-boy/|title=Diddy & Faith Evans Pay Tribute to Notorious B.I.G. on 21st Anniversary of Death|last=Trock|first=Gary|date=9 March 2018|work=The Blast|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref> A tribute song by [[Puff Daddy]], the rapper's wife [[Faith Evans]] and R&B group [[112 (band)|112]], "[[I'll Be Missing You]]" which sampled [[The Police]]'s "[[Every Breath You Take]]", ranked as the third best-selling single of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.billboard.com/photos/6538627/most-touching-tribute-songs-see-you-again-furious-7-wiz-khalifa-charlie-puth-more|title=The 16 Most Touching Tribute Songs|last=Payne|first=Chris|date=17 April 2015|work=Billboard|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref>

The 1996 [[Christmas number-one]], "[[2 Become 1]]" by [[Spice Girls]], remained at number-one for the first two weeks of 1997.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/classic-christmas-number-1s-spice-girls-2-become-1__17230/|title=Classic Christmas Number 1s: Spice Girls' 2 Become 1|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=14 December 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Professional Widow (It's Got to Be Big)]]" by [[Tori Amos]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/second-chance-singles-the-songs-that-underperformed-first-time-round__15964/|title=Second chance singles: The songs that underperformed first time round|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=5 August 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> Overall, twenty-four different singles peaked at number-one in 1997, with [[Spice Girls]] (4) having the most singles hit that position.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-36714177|title=How the Spice effect still packs punch|last=McKie|first=John|date=7 July 2016|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref>

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
Two-hundred and twenty-eight singles charted in the top 10 in 1997, with two-hundred and sixteen singles reaching their peak this year.

Sixty-four artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1997. [[U2]] frontman [[Bono]] and the five members of [[Boyzone]] shared the record for the most top ten singles in 1997 with five hit singles each. Four of Bono's entries were with the band, the most successful of which was the number-one hit "[[Discothèque (song)|Discothèque]]" in February.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/gwen-stefanis-official-top-20-biggest-selling-singles-revealed__14183/|title=Gwen Stefani's Official Top 20 biggest selling singles revealed|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=22 March 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> "[[Staring at the Sun (U2 song)|Staring at the Sun]]" peaked at number 3 in April,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://observer.com/2017/06/u2-best-songs/|title=The 5 Best U2 Songs of All Time|last=Deino|first=Daryl|date=30 June 2017|work=The Observer|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> "[[Please (U2 song)|Please]]" made number 7 in September<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/flashback-u2-perform-please-at-the-1997-mtv-video-music-awards-243860/|title=Flashback: U2 Perform ‘Please’ At The 1997 MTV Video Music Awards|last=Greene|first=Andy|date=6 August 2013|work=Rolling Stone|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> and "[[Last Night on Earth (U2 song)|Last Night on Earth]]" reached number 10 in July.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/u2-pop-album-anniversary-review-7710077/|title=The 'Pop' Enigma: Revisiting U2's Most Misunderstood Album 20 Years Later|last=Olivier|first=Bobby|date=3 March 2017|publisher=Billboard|access-date=22 July 2018}}</ref> His other appearance was on the [[BBC]] charity single for [[Children in Need]], "[[Perfect Day (BBC promotion)|Perfect Day]]", alongside artists such as [[Boyzone]], [[David Bowie]], [[Elton John]] and the original composer of the song [[Lou Reed]]. The song entered the chart in November and spent 3 weeks at number-one, and 9 weeks in the top 10.<ref name = "Perfect Day">{{cite web|url=http://www.dailyedge.ie/perfect-day-bbc-3798595-Jan2018/|title=An important examination of the BBC's star-studded version of Perfect Day, 20 years later|date=16 January 2018|work=DailyEdge.ie|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> Along with [[U2]], eight other acts had four singles in the top 10: [[911 (UK band)|911]], [[Backstreet Boys]], [[Boyzone]], [[Brett Anderson]] (from the [[britpop]] band [[Suede (band)|Suede]]), [[George Michael]], [[Spice Girls]] and [[Texas (band)|Texas]]

[[No Doubt]] were one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Don't Speak]]".<ref name = "No Doubt" /> [[All Saints (band)|All Saints]],<ref name = "All Saints" /> [[Daft Punk]],<ref name = "Daft Punk" /> [[Jon Bon Jovi]],<ref name = "Jon Bon Jovi">{{cite web|url=http://ultimateclassicrock.com/gdpr/consent/?redirect=/jon-bon-jovi-destination-anywhere/|title=WHEN JON BON JOVI MIXED MUSIC AND ACTING WITH ‘DESTINATION ANYWHERE’|date=1 July 2017|work=Ultimate Classic Rock|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> [[Oasis (band)|Oasis]]<ref name = "Summer">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/the-official-top-40-biggest-selling-singles-of-summer-1997__19591/|title=The Official Top 40 biggest selling singles of summer 1997|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=6 July 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> and [[Toni Braxton]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1997.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/toni-braxton-songs-best-hits-list-7989973/|title=Toni Braxton’s 25 Best Songs: Critic's Picks|last=Smith|first=Da'Shan|date=7 October 2017|work=Billboard|access-date=23 July 2018}}</ref>

===Chart debuts===
Eighty-five artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1997, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, fourteen went on to record another hit single that year: [[All Saints (band)|All Saints]], [[The Course]], [[Daft Punk]], [[DJ Quicksilver]], [[Eels (band)|Eels]], [[Hanson (band)|Hanson]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2018/apr/24/hanson-how-we-made-mmmbop-interview|title=Hanson: how we made MMMBop|last=Simpson|first=Dave|date=24 April 2018|work=Guardian|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> [[Kavana (singer)|Kavana]], [[Mansun]], [[No Doubt]], [[No Mercy (pop band)|No Mercy]], [[The Notorious B.I.G.]], [[Orbital (band)|Orbital]], [[The Seahorses]] and [[Shola Ama]]. [[Sash!]] and [[The Verve]] both had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

''Makaveli'' was a pseudonym used by the late [[Tupac Shakur]], who had charted at number six under the name 2Pac in 1996 with "[[California Love]]", alongside [[Dr. Dre]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://edition.cnn.com/2016/09/13/entertainment/tupac-shakur-death-anniversary/index.html|title=Tupac Shakur: 20 years after his death|last=Respers|first=Lisa|date=13 September 2016|work=CNN|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/tupac-shakurs-top-10-biggest-singles-on-the-official-chart__20888/|title=Tupac Shakur's Top 10 biggest singles on the Official Chart|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=21 October 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> [[The Seahorses]] was formed by former [[The Stone Roses]] guitarist [[John Squire]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.wired.com/2014/04/britpop-cheat-sheet/|title=12 TUNES YOU NEED TO KNOW TO SOUND LIKE A BRITPOP EXPERT|last=McMillan|first=Graeme|date=24 April 2014|work=Wired|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> His old group had debuted in 1989 with "[[Fools Gold/What the World is Waiting For|Fools Gold"/"What the World is Waiting For]]", a number 8 hit.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/music/8434870/The-Stone-Roses-a-potted-history.html|title=The Stone Roses: a potted history|last=Lachno|first=James|date=7 April 2011|work=Telegraph|publisher=Telegraph Media Group|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref>

"[[Midnight in Chelsea]]" was [[Jon Bon Jovi]]'s first single to make an impact without his [[Bon Jovi|bandmates]], peaking at number four. He had a second hit single this year, "[[Queen of New Orleans]]", which reached number 10.<ref name = "Jon Bon Jovi" /> [[Will Smith]] first made the chart in the duo [[DJ Jazzy Jeff and the Fresh Prince]] using his character name from ''[[The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/guide-dj-jazzy-jeff-fresh-princes-five-studio-161741271.html|title=A Guide to DJ Jazzy Jeff & The Fresh Prince's Five Studio Albums (And Overall Legacy)|date=30 April 2017|work=Billboard|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> The single "[[Men in Black (song)|Men in Black]]" was the first time he charted under his own name.<ref name = "Men in Black">{{cite web|url=https://www.etonline.com/features/220725_on_1997_set_of_will_smiths_men_black_music_video|title='Men in Black' Turns 20! How Will Smith's Theme Song Marked His Return to Rap|last=Bergren|first=Joe|date=30 June 2017|work=Entertainment Tonight|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref>

The "[[Perfect Day (BBC promotion)|Perfect Day]]" charity single brought together musicians and performers from across entertainment, both established and new to the UK charts but performing as this line-up for the first time. The solo artists with previous chart credits were [[David Bowie]], [[Elton John]], [[Gabrielle (singer)|Gabrielle]], [[Joan Armatrading]], [[Laurie Anderson]], [[Lou Reed]], [[Suzanne Vega]], [[Tammy Wynette]], and [[Tom Jones (singer)|Tom Jones]]. Several artists had made the charts with their groups before, including [[Bono]] ([[U2]]), [[Brett Anderson]] ([[Suede (band)|Suede]]), [[Heather Small]] ([[M People]]), [[Huey Morgan]] ([[Fun Lovin' Criminals]]), [[Ian Broudie]] ([[The Lightning Seeds]]), [[Shane MacGowan]] ([[The Pogues]]), as well as [[Keith Duffy]], [[Michael Graham (singer)|Michael Graham]], [[Ronan Keating]], [[Shane Lynch]] and [[Stephen Gately]] (all [[Boyzone]]). Additionally, conductor [[Andrew Davis (conductor)|Andrew Davis]], the [[BBC Symphony Orchestra]], string group [[Brodsky Quartet]], soprano saxophone player [[Courtney Pine]] and tenor horn player [[Sheona White]] all played on the track.<ref name = "Perfect Day" />

===Best-selling singles===
[[Elton John]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Something About the Way You Look Tonight]]"/"[[Candle in the Wind|Candle in the Wind 1997]]". The song spent thirteen weeks in the top 10 (including five weeks at number one), sold over 4.77 million copies and was certified 8× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref name = "Candle" /> "[[Barbie Girl]]" by [[Aqua (band)|Aqua]] came in second place, selling more than 1.5 million copies and losing out by around 3.27 million sales.<ref name = "Aqua" /> [[Puff Daddy]] & [[Faith Evans]] featuring [[112 (band)|112]]'s "[[I'll Be Missing You]]",<ref name = "I'll Be Missing You">{{cite web|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/1997-best-musical-year-1990s-6273806/|title=Why 1997 Was the Best Musical Year of the '90s|last=Payne|first=Chris|date=9 October 2014|work=Billboard|access-date=22 July 2018}}</ref> "[[Perfect Day (BBC promotion)|Perfect Day]]" from ''Various Artists''<ref name = "Perfect Day" /> and "[[Teletubbies say "Eh-oh!" ]]" by [[Teletubbies]] made up the top five.<ref name = "Teletubbies">{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/tvandradio/6019164/Teletubbies-to-reunite.html|title=Teletubbies to reunite|date=13 August 2009|work=Telegraph|publisher=Telegraph Media Group|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> Singles by [[Will Smith]],<ref name = "Men in Black" /> [[No Doubt]],<ref name = "No Doubt" /> [[Natalie Imbruglia]],<ref name = "Torn" /> [[Spice Girls]] and [[Chumbawamba]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://metro.co.uk/2015/05/28/19-one-hit-wonders-from-the-90s-that-will-make-you-feel-nostalgic-5219174/|title=19 one-hit wonders from the 90s that will make you feel nostalgic|last=Lee|first=Ann|date=28 May 2015|work=Metro|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref> were also in the [[1997 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/official-charts-flashback-1997-spice-girls-spice-up-your-life__20793/|title=Official Charts Flashback 1997: Spice Girls - Spice Up Your Life|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=19 October 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref>

"Something About the Way You Look Tonight"/"Candle in the Wind 1997" (1) was ranked as the [[List of best-selling singles of the 1990s in the United Kingdom|best-selling single of the decade]], while "Barbie Girl" (4), "Perfect Day" (6) and "I'll Be Missing You" (9) were all ranked in the top 10 best-selling singles of the 1990s. "Something About the Way You Look Tonight"/"Candle in the Wind 1997" also stands as the [[List of best-selling singles in the United Kingdom|biggest-selling single of all time]] in the UK (as of July 2018).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/elton-johns-candle-in-the-wind-1997-20-years-on-the-tribute-to-princess-diana-that-is-the-uks-biggest-selling-single-of-all-time__19805/|title=Elton John's Candle in the Wind 1997 20 years on - the tribute to Princess Diana that is the UK's biggest selling single of all time|date=25 August 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=22 July 2018}}</ref>

==Entries by artist==
[[File:WillSmithSept09.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Will Smith]] (pictured in 2009) spent four weeks at number-one with the song "[[Men in Black (song)|Men in Black]]", which featured on [[Men in Black: The Album|the soundtrack]] of ''[[Men in Black (1997 film)|the film of the same name]]''.]]
[[File:NO DOUBT 327b Brian McCauley.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[No Doubt]], fronted by [[Gwen Stefani]] (pictured third from right), had two top 10 hits in the UK this year, including "[[Don't Speak]]", which spent three weeks at number-one and lasted nine weeks in the top 10, becoming the seventh best selling single of the year.]]
[[File:Bono U2 at Prague 2000 IMF.jpeg|thumb|right|150px|[[Bono]] (pictured in 2000) had five top 10 singles in 1997, the joint most with the members of fellow countrymen [[Boyzone]]. This included four with his band [[U2]], including the number-one hit "[[Discothèque (song)|Discothèque]]" and one on the charity ensemble "[[Perfect Day (BBC promotion)|Perfect Day]]", which topped the chart in November.]]
[[File:Chumbawamba.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Lancashire band [[Chumbawamba]] had one of the year's biggest selling singles, "[[Tubthumping]]", which spent three weeks at number two and had an 11-week stay in the top 10.]]
[[File:Gala Rizzatto.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Italian artist [[Gala (singer)|Gala]] spent eight weeks in the UK top 10 this year with her hit single "[[Freed from Desire]]", which had its highest position at number two.]]
[[File:Katrinaleskanich.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Katrina and the Waves]] (lead singer [[Katrina Leskanich]] pictured in 1986) won the [[1997 Eurovision Song Contest]] with "[[Love Shine a Light]]", which reached number three in the UK.]]

*{{note label|Never Ever|A|a}} "Never Ever" reached its peak of number-one on 17 January 1998 (week ending).<ref name = "All Saints" />
*{{note label|Tori Amos|B|b}} "Professional Widow (It's Got to Be Big)" originally peaked at number 20 upon its initial release in 1996. It was remixed by [[Armand van Helden]] and re-released in 1997, reaching number-one in January.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/music/musicblog/2016/nov/23/tori-amos-10-of-the-best|title=Tori Amos - 10 of the best|last=Richardson Andrews|first=Charlotte|date=23 November 2016|work=Guardian|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref>
*{{note label|Remember|C|c}} "Remember Me" re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 15 February 1997 (week ending).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/20-classic-hits-turning-20-years-old-in-2017__18621/|title=20 classic hits turning 20 years old in 2017|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=1 April 2017|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref>
*{{note label|You Got the Love|D|d}} The original solo version of "You Got the Love" by Candi Staton peaked at number 95 in 1986. The 1991 remix by The Source peaked at number 4.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/features/story-of-the-song-youve-got-the-love-florence-the-machine-2008-2075153.html|title=Story of the Song: You've Got the Love, Florence + the Machine (2008)|last=Webb|first=Robert|date=10 September 2010|work=Independent|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref>
*{{note label|Show Me Love|E|e}} The original version of "Show Me Love" peaked at number 6 upon its release in 1993.
*{{note label|DJ Quicksilver|F|f}} "Bellissima" charted as a double A-side single with another song, "I Have a Dream", across Europe in 1996 but only "Bellissima" reached the UK chart.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://mixmag.net/feature/the-15-best-mid-90s-trance-tracks|title=THE 15 BEST MID-90S TRANCE TRACKS|last=Ball|first=James|date=22 June 2018|work=Mixmag|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref>
*{{note label|Tupac|G|g}} ''Makaveli'' was a stage name used by American rapper [[Tupac Shakur]] (sometimes known alternatively as ''2Pac'').<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.joe.co.uk/entertainment/long-lost-2pac-album-liner-notes-sees-the-rapper-diss-jay-z-dr-dre-biggie-more-171201|title=Long-lost 2Pac album liner notes sees rapper diss JAY-Z, Dr. Dre, Biggie & more|last=Lavin|first=Will|date=6 April 2018|work=JOE.co.uk|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref>
*{{note label|Lovefool|H|h}} "Lovefool" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 21 upon its initial release in September 1996.<ref name = "Lovefool" />
*{{note label|Hypnotize|I|i}} "Hypnotize" was the last single released by [[The Notorious B.I.G.]] before his death.
*{{note label|Love Shine a Light|J|j}} "Love Shine a Light" was the [[United Kingdom in the Eurovision Song Contest 1997|United Kingdom]]'s winning entry at the [[Eurovision Song Contest]] in [[Eurovision Song Contest 1997|1997]].
*{{note label|Rembrandts|K|k}} "I'll Be There for You" was the theme song from the television series ''[[Friends]]''. It originally peaked at number 3 upon its initial release in 1995.
*{{note label|Six Underground|L|l}} "6 Underground" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 15 upon its initial release in October 1996.
*{{note label|I'll Be Missing You|M|m}} "I'll Be Missing You" was recorded as a tribute to The Notorious B.I.G. after he was shot dead. It featured his wife [[Faith Evans]] alongside [[Puff Daddy]] and R&B group [[112 (group)|112]] and included a sample of "[[Every Breath You Take]]" by [[The Police]].<ref name = "I'll Be Missing You" /><ref name = "Summer" />
*{{note label|Bitter Sweet Symphony|N|n}} "Bitter Sweet Symphony" uses a sample of "[[The Last Time (The Rolling Stones song)|The Last Time]]"  by [[The Rolling Stones]]. It was the subject of a legal challenge regarding plagiarism, and as a result [[Mick Jagger]] and [[Keith Richards]] had subsequent songwriting credits added.
*{{note label|Just a Girl|O|o}} "Just a Girl" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 38 upon its initial release October 1996.
*{{note label|Meredith Brooks|P|p}} "Bitch" was sometimes known by its censored title "Nothing in Between".
*{{note label|California Dreamin|Q|q}} "California Dreamin' originally peaked outside the top ten at number 23 upon its initial release in 1966.
*{{note label|Mo Money Mo Problems|R|r}} "Mo Money Mo Problems" was the first single released since The Notorious B.I.G.'s tragic death, and charted posthumously at number six.
*{{note label|Never Gonna Let You Go|S|s}} "Never Gonna Let You Go" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 13 September 1997 (week ending), and at number 9 on 27 September 1997 (week ending).
*{{note label|Princess Diana|T|t}} "Candle in the Wind 1997" was released as a tribute to [[Diana, Princess of Wales]] who [[Death of Diana, Princess of Wales|died]] in 1997.<ref name = "Candle" /> 
*{{note label|Candle in the Wind|U|u}} "Something About the Way You Look Tonight"/"Candle in the Wind 1997" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 6 December 1997 (week ending), at number 10 on 20 December 1997 (week ending) and at number 10 on 3 January 1998 (week ending).
*{{note label|Secret Service|V|v}} "On Her Majesty's Secret Service" was remixed by [[Propellerheads]] for the ''[[Shaken and Stirred: The David Arnold James Bond Project]]'', an album of cover versions of [[James Bond]] theme songs.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.gq-magazine.co.uk/article/on-her-majestys-secret-service-james-bond|title=The GQ Guide to James Bond: On Her Majesty's Secret Service|last=Williams|first=David|date=21 July 2015|work=GQ|accessdate=29 July 2018}}</ref>
*{{note label|You Sexy Thing|W|w}} "You Sexy Thing" originally peaked at number 2 upon its initial release in 1975. A [[Ben Liebrand]] remix of the song was released in 1987 and peaked at number 10. In 1997, the original version of "You Sexy Thing" was re-released after it featured in the successful comedy film ''[[The Full Monty]]''. This re-release peaked at number 6.
*{{note label|Children in Need|X|x}} Released as the official single for [[Children in Need]].
*{{note label|Various Artists|Y|y}} "Perfect Day" was credited to Various artists which included [[Lou Reed]], [[David Bowie]], [[Tom Jones (singer)|Tom Jones]] and [[Boyzone]]. 
*{{note label|Teletubbies|Z|z}} "Teletubbies Say "Eh-oh"" was a remix of the theme tune from the ''[[Teletubbies]]'' children's television series. It spent two weeks at number-one in December but narrowly lost out to "[[Too Much (Spice Girls song)|Too Much]]" by [[Spice Girls]] for the Christmas number-one.<ref name = "Teletubbies" />
