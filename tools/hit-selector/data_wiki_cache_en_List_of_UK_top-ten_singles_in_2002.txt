[[File:Junkie-xl.jpg|thumb|right|200px|Dutch DJ [[Tom Holkenborg|JXL]] remixed [[Elvis Presley]]'s 1968 song [[A Little Less Conversation#Junkie XL / JXL remix|A Little Less Conversation]] for [[Nike, Inc.|Nike]]'s commercial for the [[2002 FIFA World Cup]]. Released as a single, the remix spent four weeks at number-one in the UK and became the fifth best-selling single of 2002. It later featured on the compilation album ''[[ELV1S: 30 No. 1 Hits]]''. The song was re-released in 2005 to mark Presley's 70th birthday and made the UK top 10 again, this time reaching number three.]]

The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of [[CD single|physical]] singles with [[airplay]] figures and [[Music download|digital downloads]] excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]]|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]|edition=18th}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |accessdate=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2002 in British music charts|2002]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2001|2001]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2003|2003]] but were in the top 10 in 2002. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Two hundred and sixteen singles were in the top ten in 2002. Ten singles from 2001 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Sk8er Boi]]" by [[Avril Lavigne]] was released in 2002 but did not reach its peak until 2003.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/2629761.stm |title=Girls Aloud hold top spot |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=5 January 2003 |accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> Fifty-one artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2002. [[Blazin' Squad]], [[Girls Aloud]], [[Busted (band)|Busted]], [[Holly Valance]], [[Pharrell Williams]] and [[Shakira]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 2002.

The 2001 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Somethin' Stupid]]" by [[Robbie Williams]] and [[Nicole Kidman]], remained at number-one for the first week of 2002.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/1730293.stm|title=Williams wins again|date=30 December 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref> [[Daniel Bedingfield]]'s "[[Gotta Get Thru This (song)|Gotta Get thru This]]" returned to number-one in January after previously occupying the top spot for two weeks in December 2001.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1746017.stm|title=Bedingfield back at number one|date=6 January 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[More than a Woman (Aaliyah song)|More Than a Woman]]" by the late [[Aaliyah]], who was killed in a plane crash in August 2001.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/world/americas/1510034.stm|title=R&B's Aaliyah dies in plane crash|date=26 August 2001|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/1758494.stm|title=Posthumous number one for Aaliyah|date=13 January 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref> Overall, thirty-two different singles peaked at number-one in 2002, with [[Gareth Gates]] and [[Will Young]] (both 3) having the most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
Two hundred and sixteen singles charted in the top 10 in 2002, with two-hundred and five singles reaching their peak this year.

Fifty artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2002. Four artists had the joint most top ten singles in 2002 with four. American singer [[Ashanti (singer)|Ashanti]]'s "[[Foolish (Ashanti song)|Foolish]]" was her only solo single of her top ten entries, peaking at number four.<ref name ="Ashanti">{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2128219.stm|title=Gates ends Elvis revival|date=14 July 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> Of her collaborations, "[[What's Luv?]]" with [[Fat Joe]], and "[[Down 4 U]]" alongside [[Irv Gotti]], [[Murder Inc. Records|The Inc.]], [[Ja Rule]], [[Charlie Baltimore]] and [[Vita (rapper)|Vita]] both reached number four. A fourth hit, "[[Always on Time]]" also with Ja Rule reached number six.

[[Pop Idol]] winner [[Will Young]] and runner-up [[Gareth Gates]] both had four top tens in 2002. Young had two number-one hits on his own, including "[[Anything is Possible (Will Young song)|Anything is Possible]]"/"[[Evergreen (Westlife song)#Will Young version|Evergreen]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1848656.stm|title=Young scores record number one|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref> and "[[Light My Fire (Will Young song)|Light My Fire]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2022191.stm|title=Will Young scores second number one|date=2 June 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> His [[Children in Need]] single "[[Don't Let Me Down (Will Young song)|Don't Let Me Down]]"/"[[You and I (Will Young song)|You and I]]" just missed out on the top spot, peaking at number two.<ref name ="Children">{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2509183.stm|title=Young misses out on top spot|date=24 November 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref> Young and Gates got together to record a cover of [[The Beatles]] song "[[The Long and Winding Road#Will Young and Gareth Gates version|The Long and Winding Road]]" which landed at the top spot. 
<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2287352.stm|title=Pop Idol duet hits top|date=29 September 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref>

Young's competition rival Gates had three top 10 singles in his own right. Debut single "[[Unchained Melody#Gareth Gates version|Unchained Melody]]"<ref name="Unchained Melody">{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1891053.stm|title=Pop Idol's Gareth is number one|date=24 March 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> and follow-up "[[Anyone of Us (Stupid Mistake)]]"<ref name ="Ashanti"/> were chart toppers, while a third song, album title track "[[What My Heart Wants to Say]]" reached number five. Gates also recorded a version of [[Elvis Presley]]'s "[[Suspicious Minds]]", which featured on "The Long and Winding Road" single as a double-A side.<ref name="Elton">{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2578073.stm|title=Elton takes top slot|date=15 December 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref>

The final four-time entrants to the top ten this year were [[S Club Juniors]], a spinoff group from the successful [[S Club 7]] (renamed as S Club in March 2002). Beginning with "[[One Step Closer (S Club Juniors song)|One Step Closer]]", which got to number two,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1956664.stm|title=Sugababes claim number one|date=28 April 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> they achieved further chart fame as "[[Automatic High]]" and "[[New Direction (song)|New Direction]]" equalled that placing.<ref name="Juniors">{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2325061.stm|title=Young conquers album charts|date=13 October 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> Christmas double single "[[Puppy Love/Sleigh Ride]]" rounded off their year, peaking in sixth position.<ref name="Elton"/>

Rapper [[Eminem]] was one of a number of artists with three top-ten entries, including number ones "[[Without Me (Eminem song)|Without Me]]"<ref name ="Without Me">{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2009529.stm|title=Top spot for Eminem|date=26 May 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> and "[[Lose Yourself]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/2550643.stm|title=In Brief: Eminem tops singles chart|date=8 December 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=8 August 2017}}</ref> [[Atomic Kitten]], [[Daniel Bedingfield]], [[Oasis (band)|Oasis]], [[Ronan Keating]], [[Sugababes]] and [[Westlife]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 2002.

===Chart debuts===
Ninety artists achieved their first top 10 single in 2002, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, twelve went on to record another hit single that year: [[Avril Lavigne]], [[Blazin' Squad]], [[Darius Campbell|Darius]], [[Flip & Fill]], [[Holly Valance]], [[Lasgo]], [[Milk Inc.]], [[Moony]], [[Ms. Dynamite]], [[Nickelback]], [[Scooter (band)|Scooter]] and [[Shakira]]. Three artists achieved two more chart hits in 2002: [[Chad Kroeger]], [[Christina Milian]] and [[H & Claire]] (as a duo). [[Ashanti (singer)|Ashanti]], [[Gareth Gates]], [[S Club 8|S Club Juniors]] and [[Will Young]] all had three other entries in their breakthrough year.

[[Darren Hayes]] was one of the participants on the Artists Against AIDS Worldwide (known as All Star Tribute in the UK) charity single "[[What's Going On (Marvin Gaye song)#Artists Against AIDS Worldwide cover|What's Going On]]" which reached number six in 2001, but "[[Insatiable (Darren Hayes song)|Insatiable]]" was his first official credited top 10 single.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/showbiz/3281609.stm|title=The show goes on in Aids battle|date=24 November 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref> [[H & Claire]] performed together as a duo for the first time in 2002, reaching the top 10 on three occasions during the year. They had both been members of the 1990s-formed pop group [[Steps (band)|Steps]]. Bandmate [[Faye Tozer]] had her only single outside [[Steps (band)|Steps]] in 2002, featuring on [[Russell Watson]]'s "Someone Like You", which reached number ten.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bbc.co.uk/pressoffice/pressreleases/stories/2002/05_may/13/totp_steps.shtml|title=Former Steps stars do battle on this week's Top of the Pops|date=13 May 2002|work=BBC Press Office|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref>

[[Abz Love|Abs]] had eleven top 10 singles with [[Five (band)|Five]] between 1997 and 2001, but "[[What You Got (Abs song)|What You Got]]" marked his solo debut.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/reviews/qzdp/|title=Abs: Abstract Theory review|last=O'Callaghan|first=Bren|work=BBC Music|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref> [[Nicole Appleton|Nicole]] and [[Natalie Appleton]] had multiple chart hits with [[All Saints (group)|All Saints]] but appeared in the chart for the first time in 2002 as a [[Appleton (music duo)|duo]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/reviews/2225649.stm|title=Saintly sisters go it alone|last=Osborn|first=Michael|date=2 September 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref>

"Bunsen Burner" marked his return to the chart and his only solo top 10 hit. [[Ja Rule]] participated on the charity single "[[What's Going On (song)|What's Going On]]" in 2001 but the collaboration with [[Ashanti (singer)|Ashanti]], "[[Always on Time]]", was her first official lead credit.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/artist/888/ja-rule/|title=Ja Rule - Official Chart History|work=Official Chart|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=3 July 2017}}</ref> [[Justin Timberlake]] launched his solo career in 2002 with "[[Like I Love You]]", leaving [[NSync]] after their last single together, "[[Girlfriend (NSYNC song)|Girlfriend]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2366375.stm|title=Timberlake fails to top chart|date=27 October 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref>

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top 10 in the charts in 2002. The [[Sport Relief]] single was a new version of [[Elton John]]'s "[[Your Song]]", featuring Italian tenor [[Alessandro Safina]] alongside John, peaking at number four on 27 July 2002.<ref name = "Sport Relief">{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport1/hi/special_events/sport_relief/1961425.stm|title='Your Song' released|date=15 July 2002|work=BBC Sport|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/2144817.stm|title=Gates stays on top|date=22 July 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=23 April 2017}}</ref>

[[Will Young]] recorded the [[Children in Need]] single for 2002, the double-A side "[[Don't Let Me Down (Will Young song)|Don't Let Me Down]]"/"[[You and I (Will Young song)|You and I]]". It was his fourth successive top 2 single and reached number two on 30 November 2002.<ref name="Children"/>

===Best-selling singles===
Will Young had the best-selling single of the year with "Anything is Possible"/"Evergreen", his first recording after beating Gareth Gates to the Pop Idol title. The single spent six weeks in the top 10 (including three weeks at number one), sold almost 1.8 million copies and was certified 3× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/music/music-news/6917900/Will-Young-has-biggest-selling-single-of-the-decade.html|title=Will Young has biggest selling single of the decade|date=1 January 2009|work=The Telegraph|publisher=Telegraph Media Group|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> Gates' "Unchained Melody" came in second place, selling over 1.3 million copies and losing out by around 400,000 sales.<ref name ="Unchained Melody" /> [[Enrique Iglesias]]'s "[[Hero (Enrique Iglesias song)|Hero]]",<ref name="Kelly"/> "[[Dilemma (Nelly song)|Dilemma]]" from [[Nelly]] & [[Kelly Rowland]],<ref name="Kelly"/> and "[[A Little Less Conversation]]" by [[Elvis Presley|Elvis]] vs. [[Tom Holkenborg|JXL]]<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/2048610.stm|title=Elvis makes chart history|date=16 June 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> made up the top five. Singles by Gareth Gates ("[[Anyone of Us (Stupid Mistake)]]"<ref name ="Ashanti" />), [[Las Ketchup]],<ref name ="Juniors"/> [[Shakira]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1865598.stm|title=Young stays top of the pops|date=10 March 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> [[Liberty X]]<ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/1997309.stm|title=Popstars 'rejects' hit top spot|date=19 May 2002|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=28 November 2016}}</ref> and [[Eminem]]<ref name = "Without Me" /> were also in the [[2002 in British music charts#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

"Anything is Possible"/"Evergreen" was ranked as the [[List of best-selling singles of the 2000s (decade) in the United Kingdom|best-selling single of the decade]], while "Unchained Melody" (2) also ranked in the top 10 best-selling singles of the 2000s.

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Sk8er Boi|A|a}} "Sk8er Boi" reached its peak of number eight on 11 January 2003 (week ending).
*{{note label|Children in Need 2001|B|b}} Released as the official single for Children in Need in 2001.<ref name = "CIN">{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/the-official-biggest-selling-children-in-need-singles-revealed__17116/|title=The Official biggest selling Children In Need singles revealed|last=Copsey|first=Rob|date=17 November 2016|work=Official Charts|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=30 July 2017}}</ref>
