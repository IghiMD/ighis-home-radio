{{1990s in music (UK)}}
[[File:Robson & Jerome Duo.jpg|thumb|right|200px|[[Robson & Jerome]], who rose to prominence via the television series ''[[Soldier Soldier]]'', achieved the best-selling single of 1995 with "[[Unchained Melody#Robson & Jerome version|Unchained Melody]]"/"[[(There'll Be Bluebirds Over) The White Cliffs of Dover|White Cliffs of Dover]]", which spent seven weeks at number-one in May and June and has since sold 1,800,000 copies in the UK alone. They scored a second number-one hit in November with "[[I Believe (Frankie Laine song)#Selected list of recorded versions|I Believe]]"/"[[Up on the Roof (song)#Other recordings|Up on the Roof]]", which became the year's third best seller.]]
[[File:Coolio.jpg|thumb|right|200px|[[Coolio]] (pictured in 2002) had the second best selling single of this year with his signature song "[[Gangsta's Paradise]]", featuring guest vocals from [[L.V. (singer)|L.V.]], which spent two weeks at number-one and lasted 12 weeks in the top 10 altogether.]]
[[File:Mick Hucknall Bremen 2003.jpeg|200px|thumb|right|alt=|[[Mick Hucknall]] (pictured in 2003) and his band [[Simply Red]] scored their first and only UK number-one single in September of this year with "[[Fairground (song)|Fairground]]", which topped the chart for four weeks.]]
[[File:Oasis Liam and Noel.jpg|200px|thumb|right|alt=|[[Oasis (band)|Oasis]] achieved four top 10 singles in 1995. These included "[[Some Might Say]]", which became the band's first number-one single, and "[[Wonderwall]]", which spent 11 weeks in the top 10 and is now Oasis' biggest-selling single in the UK.]]
The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1995 in British music|1995]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1994|1994]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1996|1996]] but were in the top 10 in 1995. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and fifty-three singles were in the top ten in 1995. Nine singles from 1994 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[I Am Blessed]]" by [[Eternal (band)|Eternal]] was released in 1995 but did not reach its peak until 1996. "[[Another Day (Whigfield song)|Another Day]]" by [[Whigfield]], "[[Cotton Eye Joe (Rednex song)|Cotton Eye Joe]]" by [[Rednex]], "[[Love Me for a Reason#Boyzone version|Love Me for a Reason]]" by [[Boyzone]], "Them Girls, Them Girls" by [[Zig and Zag (puppets)|Zig and Zag]] and "[[Think Twice (Celine Dion song)|Think Twice]]" by [[Celine Dion]] were the singles from 1994 to reach their peak in 1995. Sixty-one artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1995. [[Coolio]], [[Foo Fighters]], [[Mary J. Blige]], [[N-Trance]] and [[Supergrass]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1995.

The 1994 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Stay Another Day]]" by [[East 17]], remained at number-one for the first week of 1995. The first new number-one single of the year was "[[Cotton Eye Joe (Rednex song)|Cotton Eye Joe]]" by [[Rednex]]. Overall, seventeen different singles peaked at number-one in 1995, with [[Michael Jackson]], [[The Outhere Brothers]], [[Robson & Jerome]] and [[Take That]] (2) having the joint most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and fifty-three singles charted in the top 10 in 1995, with one-hundred and forty-eight singles reaching their peak this year.

Sixty-one artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1995. The five members of Irish boyband [[Boyzone]] ([[Keith Duffy]], [[Michael Graham (singer)|Michael Graham]], [[Ronan Keating]], [[Shane Lynch]] and [[Stephen Gately]]) shared the record for the most top ten singles in 1995 with five hit singles each. "[[Father and Son (song)#Boyzone version|Father and Son]]" was Boyzone's biggest hit of the year: it peaked at number 2 in December and spent eight weeks in the top 10. "[[Love Me for a Reason]]" also reached number 2 in January, "[[So Good (Boyzone song)|So Good]]" made number 3 in August, and "[[Key to My Life]]" charted at the same position in April. The group also gave up their time for the Childliners charity record "[[The Gift of Christmas (song)|The Gift of Christmas]]", peaking at number nine.

[[Blur (band)|Blur]] were one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Country House (Blur song)|Country House]]". [[Bobby Brown]], [[Eternal (band)|Eternal]], [[M People]], [[Shaggy (musician)|Shaggy]] and [[Take That]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1995.

===Chart debuts===
Sixty artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1995, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, eight went on to record another hit single that year: [[Black Grape]], [[Björk]], [[N-Trance]], [[Nightcrawlers (band)|Nightcrawlers]], [[Paul Oakenfold]], [[Robson & Jerome]], [[Scatman John]] and [[Supergrass]]. [[Clock (dance act)|Clock]], [[MN8]], [[The Outhere Brothers]] and [[Pulp (band)|Pulp]] all had two other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
Italian brothers Gianni and Paolo Visnadi made their chart debuts with two separate acts, charting first as members of [[Alex Party]] and later in the year with [[Livin' Joy]]. [[UB40]]'s [[Ali Campbell]] charted for the first time as a solo artist in 1995, "That Look in Your Eye" peaking at number five in May. John Reid was credited as a featured artist for the [[Nightcrawlers (band)|Nightcrawlers]] single "[[Surrender Your Love]]" but he was actually part of the band.

The duo [[McAlmont & Butler]] consisted of singer [[David McAlmont]] and guitarist [[Bernard Butler]], the latter who was in [[Suede (band)|Suede]] until his departure the previous year, The group had their first hit with the number 7 single "[[Animal Nitrate]]" in 1993. Former [[Happy Mondays]] members [[Shaun Ryder]] and [[Bez (dancer)|Mark "Bez" Berry]] became part of the line-up of newly created [[Black Grape]]. [[Edwyn Collins]] first found fame with [[Orange Juice (band)|Orange Juice]] in the mid-1980s but went it alone on number 4 hit "[[A Girl Like You (Edwyn Collins song)|A Girl Like You]]".

[[Vic Reeves]] of the comedy partnership [[Vic & Bob|Reeves & Mortimer]] had previous chart entries to his name with 1991's "[[Born Free (Matt Monro song)|Born Free]]" and "[[Dizzy (Tommy Roe song)|Dizzy]]". [[Bob Mortimer]] was making his debut in the top 10. [[Suggs (singer)|Suggs]] launched a solo career while still an active member of [[Madness (band)|Madness]], scoring a top 10 single with double-A side "[[I'm Only Sleeping]]"/"Off On Holiday".

[[Ricardo da Force]] had his first official entry as a featured artist on [[N-Trance]]'s cover of "[[Stayin' Alive#N-Trance version|Stayin' Alive]]" but he had performed vocals on [[The KLF]] chart topper "[[3 a.m. Eternal]]", as well as their other singles "[[Last Train to Trancentral]]" and "[[Justified & Ancient]]", and [[Adamski]]'s "[[The Space Jungle]]". [[Prince (musician)|Prince]] adopted the new moniker ''Artist Formerly Known as Prince'' from 1995 onwards, charting with "[[Gold (Prince song)|Gold]]" which landed at number ten.

The Childliners charity single "[[The Gift of Christmas (song)|The Gift of Christmas]]" saw a collective of new and veteran chart acts uniting to raise money for [[Childline]]. Members of [[Boyzone]], [[China Black]], [[East 17]], [[Let Loose]] and [[Ultimate Kaos]] all had previous top 10 credits to their name prior to 1995, as did solo artists [[C. J. Lewis]], [[Dannii Minogue]], [[Michelle Gayle]] and West End. [[Deuce (band)|Deuce]], [[MN8]] and [[Nightcrawlers (band)|Nightcrawlers]] all debuted earlier in 1995 but the band members all featured as individuals instead of the group's line-up. A.S.A.P., [[Backstreet Boys]], [[E.Y.C. (band)|E.Y.C.]], Gemini and The Flood were the groups whose line-up's first credit was on this record. [[Peter Andre]] and [[Sean Maguire]] were the two solo artists for which "The Gift of Christmas" was their first top 10 single of any description.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[Here Comes the Hotstepper]]" (from ''[[Pret-a-Porter]]''), "[[Have You Ever Really Loved a Woman?]]" (''[[Don Juan DeMarco]]''), "[[Hold Me, Thrill Me, Kiss Me, Kill Me]]" and "[[Kiss from a Rose]]" (''[[Batman Forever]]''), "[[Shy Guy]]" (''[[Bad Boys (1995 film)|Bad Boys]]''), "[[Gangsta's Paradise]]" (''[[Dangerous Minds]]'') and "[[GoldenEye (song)|GoldenEye]]" (''[[GoldenEye]]'').

Additionally, the K-Klass remix "[[The Best Things in Life Are Free (Luther Vandross and Janet Jackson song)|The Best Things in Life Are Free]]" from ''[[Mo' Money]]'' charted in the top ten.  Although the single itself did not promote the film, the music video was assembled from footage of the film.

===Charity singles===
Several singles raising money for different charitable causes reached the top ten this year. The [[Comic Relief]] single was an all-star collaboration between [[Cher]], [[Chrissie Hynde]], [[Neneh Cherry]] and [[Eric Clapton]], a cover version of "[[Love Can Build a Bridge#Cher, Chrissie Hynde and Neneh Cherry with Eric Clapton version|Love Can Build a Bridge]]". It topped the chart for one week on 25 March 1995 (week ending).

The charity collective Childliners, which included members of bands including [[Boyzone]], [[Backstreet Boys]] and [[Let Loose]], produced a single in aid of [[Childline]] entitled "[[The Gift of Christmas (song)|The Gift of Christmas]]". Its highest position was number 9, which it reached on 16 December 1995 (week ending), staying there for two weeks.

===Best-selling singles===
[[Robson & Jerome]] had the biggest-selling single of 1995 with the double-A side "[[Unchained Melody#Robson & Jerome version|Unchained Melody]]"/"[[(There'll Be Bluebirds Over) The White Cliffs of Dover|White Cliffs of Dover]]", which spent ten weeks in the top 10 (including seven weeks at number-one), sold over 1.8 million copies and was certified 2× platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]]. "[[Gangsta's Paradise]]" by [[Coolio]] featuring [[L.V. (singer)|L.V.]] came in second place. Robson & Jerome's "[[I Believe (1953 song)#Selected list of recorded versions|I Believe]]"/"[[Up on the Roof (song)#Other recordings|Up on the Roof]]",  "[[Back for Good (song)|Back for Good]]" from [[Take That]] and "[[Think Twice (Celine Dion song)|Think Twice]]" by [[Celine Dion]] made up the top five. Singles by  [[Michael Jackson]] ("[[Earth Song]]"), [[Simply Red]], Michael Jackson ("[[You Are Not Alone]]"), [[Everything but the Girl]] and [[Oasis (band)|Oasis]] were also in the [[1995 in British music#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.

"Unchained Melody"/"White Cliffs of Dover" (2) also ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 1990s in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Entries by artist==
{{see also|List of UK Singles Chart number ones of the 1990s#1995}}
The following table shows artists who achieved two or more top 10 entries in 1995, including songs that reached their peak in 1994 or 1996. The figures include both main artists and featured artists, while appearances on ensemble charity records are also counted for each artist.
[[File:Capitals All Caps playoff Concert 2018 (28903289938).jpg|150px|thumb|right|alt=|[[Shaggy (musician)|Shaggy]] (pictured in 2018) achieved two entries in the UK top 10 this year, including "[[Boombastic (song)|Boombastic]]", which reached number-one for one week in September.]]
[[File:Blur (band).png|150px|thumb|right|alt=|[[Blur (band)|Blur]] scored two top 10 hits in 1995, including the number-one hit "[[Country House (Blur song)|Country House]]", which famously kept Oasis' "[[Roll with It (Oasis song)|Roll with It]]" off the top spot in what became known as "[[Britpop#"The Battle of Britpop"|The Battle of Britpop]]".]]
[[File:Connect Music Festival 2007 - Jarvis Cocker.jpg|150px|thumb|right|[[Jarvis Cocker]] (pictured in 2007) and his band [[Pulp (band)|Pulp]] had three top 10 entries this year, including "[[Common People (song)|Common People]]" and "[[Mis-Shapes]]"/"[[Sorted for E's & Wizz]]", which both peaked at number two, becoming the group's joint highest-charting singles.]]
[[File:2018 Smokie - Mike Craft - by 2eight - DSC2933.jpg|150px|thumb|right|[[Smokie (band)|Smokie]] (band member Mike Craft pictured in 2018) teamed up with comedian [[Roy Chubby Brown]] to record a cover version of the band's 1976 hit single "[[Living Next Door to Alice]]". This version, entitled "Living Next Door to Alice (Who the F**k is Alice?)", peaked at number three in the UK in October 1995, two places higher than the number five peak of the original version nineteen years earlier.]]
[[File:Edwyn Collins portrait.jpg|150px|thumb|right|Scottish singer-songwriter [[Edwyn Collins]] who first found success as the lead singer with the band [[Orange Juice (band)|Orange Juice]], achieved his biggest hit as a solo artist in 1995 with "[[A Girl Like You (Edwyn Collins song)|A Girl Like You]]", which lasted seven weeks top 10, with its highest chart position at number four.]]
[[File:Kenny Dope Gonzalez.jpg|150px|thumb|right|US disc jockey and record producer [[Kenny "Dope" Gonzalez]] reached number five in the UK in March of this year under the pseudonym "The Bucketheads" with the hit record "[[The Bomb! (These Sounds Fall into My Mind)]]".]]

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Another Day|A|a}} "Another Day" re-entered the top 10 at number 7 on 7 January 1995 (week ending).
*{{note label|Riverdance|B|b}} "Riverdance" was performed during the interval of the Eurovision Song Contest in 1994, with [[Michael Flatley]] and [[Jean Butler]] dancing. It was expanded into a stage musical beginning in Dublin in February 1995.
*{{note label|Oakenfold|C|c}} ''Perfecto Allstarz'' was a pseudonym used by the DJ and producer [[Paul Oakenfold]].
*{{note label|Bomb|D|d}} "The Bomb! (These Sounds Fall into My Mind)" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 15 April 1995 (week ending).
*{{note label|Bucketheads|E|e}} ''The Bucketheads'' was a pseudonym used by American DJ [[Kenny "Dope" Gonzalez]].
*{{note label|Freak Power|F|f}} "Turn On, Tune In, Cop Out" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 29 in 1993.
*{{note label|Comic Relief|G|g}} Released as the official single for [[Comic Relief]].
*{{note label|Manchester United|H|h}} "We're Gonna Do It Again" was [[1994–95 Manchester United F.C. season|released]] by [[Manchester United F.C.]] to celebrate reaching the [[FA Cup Final]] in [[1995 FA Cup Final|1995]].
*{{note label|Love City Groove|I|i}} "Love City Groove" was the [[United Kingdom in the Eurovision Song Contest 1995|United Kingdom]]'s entry at the [[Eurovision Song Contest]] in [[Eurovision Song Contest 1995|1995]].
*{{note label|Scream|J|j}} A remixed version of "Scream" on its own was released in the United Kingdom, while it was released elsewhere as a double A-side single with "[[Childhood (Michael Jackson song)|Childhood]]".
*{{note label|Don't You Want Me|K|k}} The original version of "Don't You Want Me" peaked at number 6 upon its release in 1992.
*{{note label|Gun|L|l}} "Son of a Gun" originally peaked outside the top ten at number 13 upon its initial release in March 1994.
