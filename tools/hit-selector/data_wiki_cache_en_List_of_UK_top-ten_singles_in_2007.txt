The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Since 2004 the chart has been based on the sales of both [[CD single|physical singles]] and [[music download|digital downloads]], with [[airplay]] figures excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref>  This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2007 in British music charts|2007]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2006|2006]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2008|2008]] but were in the top 10 in 2007. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and thirty-four singles were in the top ten in 2007. Eight singles from 2006 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year. "[[Crank That (Soulja Boy)]]" by [[Soulja Boy|Soulja Boy Tell'em]] was the only single from 2007 to reach its peak in 2008.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7173993.stm|title=Radiohead CD tops UK album chart|date=6 January 2008|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> Twenty-eight artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2007. [[Calvin Harris]], [[Mark Ronson]], [[Mika (singer)|Mika]], [[OneRepublic]] and [[Scouting for Girls]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 2007.

New rules were introduced this year to alter how single downloads are counted, with a physical copy no longer having to be currently available in the shops for a single to be eligible for the charts. [[Snow Patrol]] were the most high-profile act to benefit from the change, with their song "[[Chasing Cars]]" returning to the top 10, months after first release the previous year. [[Baby's Coming Back/Transylvania|"Baby's Coming Back"/"Transylvania"]] by [[McFly]] also became only the third single in chart history to fall straight from number-one out of the top ten the following week.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and thirty-four singles charted in the top 10 in 2007, with one-hundred and twenty-five singles reaching their peak this year (including the re-entries "[[All I Want for Christmas is You]]" and "[[Fairytale of New York]]" which charted in previous years but reached peaks on their latest chart run).

Twenty-eight artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2007. American rapper [[Akon]], fellow countryman producer [[Timbaland]] and [[Lebanon]]-born British singer [[Mika (singer)|Mika]] shared the position of most top 10 entries in 2007 with four each, although Akon's "[[Smack That]]" - a collaboration with [[Eminem]] - peaked in 2006.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6163632.stm|title=Take That return to singles chart|date=19 November 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> Mika's debut number-one single "[[Grace Kelly (song)|Grace Kelly]]" acted as a breakthrough for the singer,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6285175.stm |title=Rising star Mika hits number one |work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=21 January 2007 |access-date=14 March 2010}}</ref> who won the [[BBC]]'s [[Sound of...|Sound of 2007]] poll,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6230415.stm|title=Singer Mika tops BBC talent list|date=4 January 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> as he went on to record three further top 10 entries during the year ("[[Love Today (song)|Love Today]]", "[[Big Girl (You Are Beautiful)]]" and "[[Happy Ending (Mika song)|Happy Ending]]").<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6605991.stm|title=Arctics' album storms to the top|date=29 April 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref>

Akon's other hit solo singles in 2007 included the number three peak "[[I Wanna Fuck You|I Wanna Love You]]" in February<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6373695.stm|title=Mika holds off Brit Award stars|date=18 February 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Don't Matter]]" which reached the same spot in May.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/6652057.stm|title=McFly go straight to top of chart|date=13 May 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> He also featured on the number two single "[[The Sweet Escape]]" by [[Gwen Stefani]], just missing out on number-one in March.<ref name = "Sweet">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6440127.stm|title=Take That keep UK chart top spot|date=11 March 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> Timbaland had two number-one singles in 2007: "[[Give It to Me (Timbaland song)|Give It to Me]]" featuring [[Nelly Furtado]] and [[Justin Timberlake]] in April,<ref name = "Give It" /> followed by "[[The Way I Are]] with [[Keri Hilson]] in July.<ref name = "The Way I Are" /> His feature on [[50 Cent]]'s "[[Ayo Technology]]" (also featuring Justin Timberlake)<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6997668.stm|title=Kanye West wins UK chart battle|date=16 September 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> reached number two, and "[[Apologize (OneRepublic song)|Apologize]]" with [[OneRepublic]] peaked one spot lower.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7089845.stm|title=Lewis hit song is 2007 top seller|date=12 November 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref>

Three British acts in the form of [[Girls Aloud]], [[Mark Ronson]] and [[Take That]] all achieved three top 10 entries in 2007, along with German dance group [[Cascada]] and American singer Justin Timberlake. Take That, who only returned unexpectedly as a four-piece in late 2006 following a ten-year hiatus, had a very successful year, as "[[Shine (Take That song)|Shine]]" was a number-one single<ref name = "Shine">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6417543.stm|title=Take That claim 10th number one|date=4 March 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> and "[[Rule the World (Take That single)|Rule the World]]" reached number two behind [[Leona Lewis]]' "[[Bleeding Love]]" in October 2007.<ref name = "Bleeding"/> "[[Patience (Take That song)|Patience]]" had also reached number-one in 2006 and remained in the top 10 at the beginning of 2007.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6221533.stm |title=Leona celebrates New Year at top |work=BBC News |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=31 December 2006 |access-date=30 March 2010}}</ref>

Girls Aloud added another number-one to their collection: "[[Walk This Way#Sugababes vs. Girls Aloud version|Walk This Way]]", a cover of the [[Run DMC]] and [[Aerosmith]] song, for [[Comic Relief]] with [[Sugababes]].<ref name = "Proclaimers">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6493667.stm|title=Proclaimers stroll to number one|date=25 March 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> "[[Sexy! No No No...]]" peaked at number five in September,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/6986277.stm|title=Pavarotti back in singles chart|date=9 September 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> while "[[Call the Shots]]" ended their year in December, reaching number three.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/7123946.stm|title=Leona remains top of both charts|date=2 December 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> Mark Ronson's "[[Stop Me If You Think You've Heard This One Before|Stop Me]]" was a number two entry in April.<ref name = "Give It" /> He produced [[Lily Allen]]'s cover of "[[Oh My God (Kaiser Chiefs song)|Oh My God]]" originally by [[Kaiser Chiefs]], which reached number eight in July, as well as [[Amy Winehouse]]'s interpretation of [[The Zutons]]' "[[Valerie (The Zutons song)|Valerie]]", peaking at number two in October.<ref name = "Valerie">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7055981.stm|title=Sugababes beat Britney in chart|date=22 October 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref>

Dance group Cascada's total included "[[Truly Madly Deeply (song)|Truly Madly Deeply]]" from the end of 2006, when it had peaked at number four.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6188245.stm|title=Take That's domination continues|date=17 December 2006|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref> "[[Miracle (Cascada song)|Miracle]]" landed at number eight in March<ref name = "Shine" /> and "[[What Hurts the Most]]" sneaked into the top 10 in the week before Christmas. Justin Timberlake had one more entry in addition to "Give It to Me" and "Ayo Technology". "[[What Goes Around.../...Comes Around]]" made it to number four in March.<ref name = "Sweet" />

Barbadian singer [[Rihanna]] was one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including number-one single "[[Umbrella (song)|Umbrella]]", which stayed at the top of the chart for ten weeks.<ref name = "Umbrella">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/6910836.stm|title=Rihanna achieves chart landmark|date=23 July 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> [[Avril Lavigne]], [[Calvin Harris]], [[Fedde Le Grand]], [[Gym Class Heroes]], [[Leona Lewis]], [[Mutya Buena]], [[Nelly Furtado]] and [[Snow Patrol]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 2007.

===Change to chart rules===
The new chart rules regarding downloads, introduced at the start of the year, enabled Scottish/Irish band [[Snow Patrol]] to re-enter the top 10 with "[[Chasing Cars]], a song that originally charted in summer 2006, without a [[CD single|physical copy]] of the record being in the shops at that time.<ref name = "Snow" >{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6239415.stm |title=Top 40 change helps Snow Patrol |work=[[BBC News]] |publisher=[[BBC|British Broadcasting Corporation]] |date=8 January 2007 |access-date=14 March 2010}}</ref>

===McFly number-one's rapid drop===
[[Baby's Coming Back/Transylvania|"Baby's Coming Back"/"Transylvania"]] by [[McFly]] joined a unique club of singles whose only one week in the top 10 was at number-one. It repeated the feat of "[[One Night (Elvis Presley song)|One Night]]"/"[[I Got Stung]]" and "[[It's Now or Never (song)|It's Now or Never]]", both by [[Elvis Presley]], from 2005. It held the record for the sharpest fall from number-one until "[[A Bridge over You]]" by [[Lewisham and Greenwich NHS Trust|Lewisham and Greenwich NHS Choir]] (2015) took this over in 2015 - its drop to number 29 was beaten by the charity single, which fell to number 29 after one week at the top of the chart.

===Chart debuts===
Forty-seven artists achieved their first top 10 single in 2007, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, five went on to record another hit single that year: [[Calvin Harris]], [[The Enemy (UK rock band)|The Enemy]], [[Groove Armada]], [[Gym Class Heroes]] and [[The Hoosiers]]. [[Mark Ronson]] scored two more top 10 singles in 2007. [[Mika (singer)|Mika]] had three other entries in his breakthrough year.

Sharam from the American electronic music duo [[Deep Dish (band)|Deep Dish]] had his first charting top 10 in his own right when his cover of "[[Party All the Time]]" (known as "PATT (Party All the Time)") reached number eight in January 2007.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.laweekly.com/music/top-10-dance-albums-club-land-crossover-2163071|title=Top 10 Dance Albums: Club-land Crossover|date=23 December 2009|work=LA Weekly|accessdate=25 June 2017}}</ref> [[Peter Kay]] featured on [[The Proclaimers]] new version of "[[I'm Gonna Be (500 Miles)]]" as his ''[[Phoenix Nights]]'' character [[Brian Potter (character)|Brian Potter]]. The [[Comic Relief (charity)|Comic Relief]] single went to number-one and also featured [[Matt Lucas]] playing [[Lou and Andy|Andy Pipkin]] from ''[[Little Britain (sketch show)|Little Britain]]''. Kay had previously been credited on "[[Is This the Way to Amarillo]]" in 2005, but this was his first chart hit as Potter.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-scotland-40261550|title=This is the story of 30 years of The Proclaimers|date=18 June 2017|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=25 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Patrick Stump]] of [[Fall Out Boy]] featured on the [[Gym Class Heroes]] top three single "[[Cupid's Chokehold]]", his first chart success outside the band.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.altpress.com/news/entry/what_happened_to_gym_class_heroes|title=What happened to Gym Class Heroes?|last=Sharp|first=Tyler|date=19 August 2016|work=Alternative Press|accessdate=25 June 2017}}</ref> [[Red Rat]] appeared on [[Groove Armada]]'s "[[Get Down (Groove Armada song)|Get Down]]", alongside Stush, although he was not officially credit on the single release.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/11300-soundboy-rock/|title=Groove Armada: Soundboy Rock Album Review|last=Ewing|first=Tom|date=14 April 2008|work=Pitchfork|accessdate=25 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Mutya Buena]], formerly of [[Sugababes]], had her first solo top 10 singles in 2007 with "[[Real Girl (song)|Real Girl]]" and her featured credit on Groove Armada's "[[Song 4 Mutya (Out of Control)]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.digitalspy.com/music/feature/a787351/sugababes-then-and-now-what-happened-to-all-six-members/|title=Sugababes then and now: What happened to all six members?|last=Corner|first=Lewis|date=24 December 2016|work=Digital Spy|accessdate=25 June 2017}}</ref> Russell Small from [[Freemasons (band)|Freemasons]] had previously been a part of the production duo [[Phats & Small]], peaking at numbers 2 and 7 with the singles "[[Turn Around (Phats & Small song)|Turn Around]]" (1999) and "Feel Good" (2000) respectively.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/charts/singles-chart/19990418/7501/|title=Official Singles Chart Top 100 - 18 April 1999-24 April 1999|publisher=Official Charts Company|accessdate=24 April 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://mn2s.com/news/dj/russell-small-releases-she/|title=Russell Small releases 'She'|date=15 July 2016|publisher=MN2S|accessdate=24 April 2017}}</ref>

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[Signal Fire (song)|Signal Fire]]" (from ''[[Spider-Man 3]]'')<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1555854/spider-man-3-soundtrack-features-killers-snow-patrol/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140813210218/http://www.mtv.com/news/1555854/spider-man-3-soundtrack-features-killers-snow-patrol/|url-status=dead|archive-date=August 13, 2014|title=Spider-Man 3 Soundtrack Features Killers, Snow Patrol|last=Kaufman|first=Gil|date=29 March 2007|work=MTV.com|publisher=MTV|accessdate=24 April 2017}}</ref> and "[[Rule the World (Take That song)|Rule the World]]" (''[[Stardust (2007 film)|Stardust]]'').<ref name ="Take That" /> "[[When You Believe]]" from ''[[The Prince of Egypt]]'' was also covered by [[Leon Jackson]] as his winning song on ''[[The X Factor (UK series 4)|The X Factor]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7146469.stm|title=Leon Jackson takes X Factor crown|date=16 December 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref>

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top 10 in the charts in 2007. The Comic Relief single was a new version of [[The Proclaimers]] hit "[[I'm Gonna Be (500 Miles)#Comic Relief version|(I'm Gonna Be) 500 Miles]]", featuring [[Peter Kay]] and [[Matt Lucas]] as their characters Brian Potter (from [[Phoenix Nights]] and Andy Pipkin ([[Little Britain (sketch show)|Little Britain]]) respectively, peaking at number one on 31 March 2007.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/pressoffice/pressreleases/stories/2007/03_march/17/comic.shtml|title=The Big One raises record Red Nose Day cash|date=17 March 2007|work=BBC Press Office|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref>

[[Runrig]] and the [[Tartan Army]], made up of [[Scotland national football team|Scotland]] football fans, recorded an unofficial Children in Need single for 2007, "[[The Bonnie Banks o' Loch Lomond|Loch Lomond]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/scotland/7043798.stm|title=Scotland fans record charity song|date=14 October 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> It reached number nine on 24 November 2007, two places higher than the official Children in Need single, "[[Headlines (Friendship Never Ends)]]" by [[Spice Girls]], which missed the top 10.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/scotland/7099634.stm|title=Scots back Children in Need night|date=17 November 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> Proceeds from the sales of "[[Any Dream Will Do (song)#Any Dream Will Do and Lee Mead version|Any Dream Will Do]]" by [[Lee Mead]] also went towards Children in Need. The song peaked at number two on 30 June 2007.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/6235522.stm|title=White Stripes take chart by storm|date=24 June 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=25 June 2017}}</ref>

===Best-selling singles===
[[Leona Lewis]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Bleeding Love]]". The single spent eleven weeks in the top 10 (including seven weeks at number one), sold over 787,000 copies and was certified platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref name = "Bleeding" /> "[[Umbrella (song)|Umbrella]]" by [[Rihanna]] featuring [[Jay Z]] came in second place, selling about 511,000 copies and losing out by around 276,000 sales.<ref name = "Umbrella" /> [[Mika (singer)|Mika]]'s "[[Grace Kelly (song)|Grace Kelly]]",<ref name ="Mika"/> "[[When You Believe#Leon Jackson version|When You Believe]]" from [[Leon Jackson]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/7158407.stm|title=Festive double for X Factor stars|date=23 December 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> and "[[Rule the World (Take That single)|Rule the World]]" by [[Take That]]<ref name="Take That"/> made up the top five. Singles by [[Sugababes]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/7021222.stm|title=Sugababes claim number one spot|date=30 September 2007|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> [[Timbaland]] featuring [[Keri Hilson]] and D.O.E.,<ref name = "Give It" /> [[The Proclaimers]] featuring [[Phoenix Nights#The Phoenix Club|Brian Potter]] and [[Lou and Andy|Andy Pipkin]],<ref name = "Proclaimers" /> [[Mark Ronson]] featuring [[Amy Winehouse]],<ref name = "Valerie" /> and [[Kaiser Chiefs]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/6395371.stm|title=Kaiser Chiefs take over UK charts|date=25 February 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref> were also in the [[2007 in British music charts#Best-selling singles of 2007|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/7165210.stm|title=Winehouse and Lewis head charts|date=31 December 2007|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=24 April 2017}}</ref>

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Soulja Boy|A|a}} "Crank That" reached its peak of number two on 12 January 2008 (week ending).
*{{note label|Cars|B|b}} "Chasing Cars" re-entered the top 10 at number 9 on 13 January 2007 (week ending), under new chart rules where a single only needed to be available to download to be eligible for the chart.
*{{note label|Comic|C|c}} Released as the official single for [[Comic Relief (charity)|Comic Relief]].
*{{note label|Flying the Flag|D|d}} "Flying the Flag (For You)" was the [[United Kingdom in the Eurovision Song Contest 2007|United Kingdom]]'s entry in the [[Eurovision Song Contest 2007]].
*{{note label|Heavyweight|E|e}} "Heavyweight Champion of the World" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 7 July 2007 (week ending).
*{{note label|Official CiN|F|f}} Released as the official single for [[Children in Need]].
*{{note label|Unofficial|G|g}} Released as an unofficial single for Children in Need. The song charted higher than one of the official Children in Need singles, "Headlines (Friendship Never Ends)" by Spice Girls, which peaked at number 11.
*{{note label|Mariah|H|h}} "All I Want for Christmas" first charted at its original peak of number 2 for three weeks in 1994. It re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 15 December 2007 (week ending).
*{{note label|Fairytale|I|i}} "Fairytale of New York" first charted at its original peak of number 2 for two weeks in 1987. It re-entered the Top 10 on 22 December 2007 (week ending) at number 8.
*{{note label|Akon|J|j}} Figure includes appearance on Gwen Stefani's "The Sweet Escape".
*{{note label|2006|K|k}} Figure includes song that peaked in 2006.
*{{note label|Ayo|L|l}} Figure includes appearance on 50 Cent's "Ayo Technology".
*{{note label|Give|M|m}} Figure includes appearance on Timbaland's "Give It to Me".
*{{note label|Mutya|N|n}} Figure includes appearance on Groove Armada's "Song 4 Mutya (Out of Control)".
