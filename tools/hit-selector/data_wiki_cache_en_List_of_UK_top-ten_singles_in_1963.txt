{{1960s in music (UK)}}
[[File:The Beatles i Hötorgscity 1963.jpg|thumb|right|200px|[[The Beatles]] had the biggest-selling single of 1963 with "[[She Loves You]]", which topped the chart for six non-consecutive weeks and was one of four top 10 singles in their breakthrough year, which included two other number-ones: "[[From Me to You]]" and "[[I Want to Hold Your Hand]]". "She Loves You" would also rank as the [[List of best-selling singles of the 1960s in the United Kingdom|best-selling single of the decade]] and remains the [[List of best-selling singles in the United Kingdom|8th highest selling song of all time]].]]
[[File:Gerry and the Pacemakers group photo 1964.JPG|thumb|right|200px|[[Gerry and the Pacemakers]] became the very first act to reach number-one in the UK charts with their first three singles: "[[How Do You Do It?]]", "[[I Like It (Gerry and the Pacemakers song)|I Like It]]" and "[[You'll Never Walk Alone#Gerry and the Pacemakers version|You'll Never Walk Alone]]".]]
[[File:The Searchers (1965).jpg|thumb|right|200px|[[The Searchers (band)|The Searchers]] entered the top 10 for the first time in 1963 with two singles making the countdown, including their debut release, "[[Sweets for My Sweet#The Searchers version|Sweets for My Sweet]]", which reached number-one for two weeks in August.]]
The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Before 2004, the chart was only based on the sales of physical singles.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[1963 in British music|1963]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1962|1962]] and [[List of UK top-ten singles in 1964|1964]] but were in the top 10 in 1963. The entry date is when the single appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

One-hundred and three singles were in the top ten in 1963. Nine singles from 1962 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year, while "[[Glad All Over]]" by [[The Dave Clark Five]], "[[I Only Want to Be with You]]" by [[Dusty Springfield]] and "[[Twenty Four Hours From Tulsa]]" by [[Gene Pitney]] were all released in 1963 but did not reach their peak until 1964. "[[The Next Time]]"/"[[Bachelor Boy]]" by [[Cliff Richard]] and [[The Shadows]] and "[[Dance On!]]" by [[The Shadows]] were the singles from 1962 to reach their peak in 1963. Twenty-nine artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1963. [[The Beatles]], [[Chuck Berry]], [[The Dave Clark Five]], [[Dusty Springfield]], [[Gerry and the Pacemakers]] and [[The Searchers (band)|The Searchers]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 1963.

The first new number-one single of the year was "[[The Next Time]]"/"[[Bachelor Boy]]" by [[Cliff Richard]] and [[The Shadows]]. Overall, seventeen different singles peaked at number-one in 1963, with [[The Beatles]] and [[Gerry and the Pacemakers]] (3) having the joint most singles hit that position.

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
One-hundred and three singles charted in the top 10 in 1963, with ninety-three singles reaching their peak this year. "[[Sukiyaki (song)|Sukiyaki]]" was recorded by [[Kenny Ball|Kenny Ball & His Jazzmen]] and [[Kyu Sakamoto]] (version titled "Sukiyaki (Ue o Muite Arukō)") and both versions reached the top 10.

Twenty-seven artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 1963. [[The Shadows]] secured the record for most top 10 hits in 1963 with nine hit singles, four of which were with [[Cliff Richard]].

[[The Searchers (band)|The Searchers]] were one of a number of artists with two top-ten entries, including the number-one single "[[Sweets for My Sweet#The Searchers version|Sweets for My Sweet]]". [[Del Shannon]], [[Joe Brown (singer)|Joe Brown]], [[Kenny Lynch]] and [[Tommy Roe]] were among the other artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 1963.

===Chart debuts===
Thirty-nine artists achieved their first top 10 single in 1963, either as a lead or featured artist. Of these, eight went on to record another hit single that year: [[Brian Poole]], [[The Crystals]], [[Gerry and the Pacemakers]], [[Kenny Lynch]], [[Paul & Paula]], [[The Searchers (band)|The Searchers]], [[The Springfields]] and [[The Tremeloes]]. [[Billy J. Kramer]] & [[The Dakotas (band)|The Dakotas]], [[Dusty Springfield]], [[Freddie and the Dreamers]] all had two more top 10 singles in 1963. [[The Beatles]] had three other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Jet Harris]] and [[Tony Meehan]] scored three chart hits as a duo in 1963, including number-one single "[[Diamonds (instrumental)|Diamonds]]" in February. They had both been members of [[The Shadows]] (formerly The Drifters) since its inception, as well as joining [[The Vipers Skiffle Group]] before going solo. "[[Just Like Eddie]]" was [[Heinz Burt|Heinz]]'s first foray into the chart under his own name, but he had been a bassist in [[The Tornados]], the group debuting with the number-one hit "[[Telstar (song)|Telstar]]" at the end of 1962.

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[Summer Holiday (song)|Summer Holiday]]" (from ''[[Summer Holiday (1963 film)|Summer Holiday]]''), "[[Commando (1962 film)|Theme from ''The Legion's Last Patrol'']]" (''[[Commando (1962 film)|The Legion's Last Patrol]]''), "[[She Loves You]]" (''[[A Hard Day's Night (film)|A Hard Day's Night]]''), "[[You'll Never Walk Alone]]" (''[[Carousel (film)|Carousel]]'') and "[[Dominique (song)|Dominique]]" (''[[The Singing Nun (film)|The Singing Nun]]'').

Additionally, a melody used in "[[Never on Sunday]]" was turned into a hit single recorded by [[Brenda Lee]] as "[[All Alone Am I]]". "[[Do You Want to Know a Secret?]]", written by [[Lennon-McCartney]] and performed by [[The Beatles]] and taken into the top 10 by [[Billy J. Kramer]] and [[The Dakotas (band)|The Dakotas]], was inspired by the tune "I'm Wishing" from ''[[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937 film)|Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs]]''. "[[Secret Love (Doris Day song)|Secret Love]]" had first been heard in the 1953 film ''[[Calamity Jane (1953 film)|Calamity Jane]]'', sang by [[Doris Day]]. [[Kathy Kirby]] took her cover version to number four. "[[María Elena (song)|María Elena]]" started as an instrumental featured in the film ''[[Bordertown (1935 film)|Bordertown]]''.

===Best-selling singles===
Until 1970 there was no universally recognised year-end best-sellers list. However, in 2011 the Official Charts Company released a list of the best-selling single of each year in chart history from 1952 to date. According to the list, "[[She Loves You]]" by [[The Beatles]] is officially recorded as the [[List of best-selling singles by year in the United Kingdom|biggest-selling single]] of 1963. "She Loves You" (1) was also ranked as the [[List of best-selling singles of the 1960s in the United Kingdom|best-selling single of the decade]], while "[[I Want to Hold Your Hand]]" (2) also ranked in the top 10 best-selling singles of the 1960s. "She Loves You" also stands as the 8th [[List of best-selling singles in the United Kingdom|biggest-selling single of all time]] in the UK (as of December 2017).

==Notes==
{{refbegin|2}}
*{{note label|Only Want|A|a}} "I Only Want to Be with You" reached its peak of number four on 15 January 1964 (week ending).
*{{note label|Glad All Over|B|b}} "Glad All Over" reached its peak of number one on 22 January 1964 (week ending).
*{{note label|Dominique|C|c}} "Dominique" reached its peak of number seven on 1 January 1964 (week ending).
*{{note label|Twenty Four Hours|D|d}} "Twenty Four Hours from Tulsa" reached its peak of number five on 8 January 1964 (week ending).
*{{note label|Telstar|E|e}} "Telstar" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 23 January 1963 (week ending).
*{{note label|Let's Dance|F|f}} "Let's Dance" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 2 January 1963 (week ending).
*{{note label|Rebelettes|G|g}} The Rebelettes were actually [[The Blossoms]], a girl group consisting of [[Darlene Love]], Fanita James, and [[Jean King (singer)|Jean King]].
*{{note label|Island of Dreams|H|h}} "Island of Dreams" re-entered the top 10 at number 8 on 27 February 1963 (week ending) for 6 weeks.
*{{note label|Say Wonderful Things|I|i}} "Say Wonderful Things" was the [[United Kingdom in the Eurovision Song Contest 1963|United Kingdom]]'s entry at the [[Eurovision Song Contest]] in [[Eurovision Song Contest 1963|1963]].
*{{note label|In Dreams|J|j}} "In Dreams" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 19 June 1963 (week ending).
*{{note label|Losing You|K|k}}"Losing You" re-entered the top 10 at number 10 on 29 May 1963 (week ending).
