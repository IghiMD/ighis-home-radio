The [[UK Singles Chart]] is one of many music charts compiled by the [[Official Charts Company]] that calculates the best-selling singles of the week in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theofficialcharts.com/ |title=The Official UK Charts Company |publisher=[[Official Charts Company]] |accessdate=1 January 2012}}</ref> Since 2004 the chart has been based on the sales of both [[CD single|physical singles]] and [[music download|digital downloads]], with [[airplay]] figures excluded from the official chart.<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Guinness Book of British Hit Singles & Albums|Guinness World Records: British Hit Singles and Albums]] (18th edition)|first=David|last=Roberts|year=2005|isbn=1-904994-00-8|pages=14|publisher=[[HiT Entertainment|Guinness World Records Limited]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3196892.stm |title=New singles formats to save the charts
|work=[[BBC News]]|date=16 October 2003 |access-date=21 February 2010}}</ref> This list shows singles that peaked in the Top 10 of the [[UK Singles Chart]] during [[2004 in British music charts|2004]], as well as singles which peaked in [[List of UK top-ten singles in 2003|2003]] but were in the top 10 in 2004. The entry date is when the song appeared in the top 10 for the first time (week ending, as published by the Official Charts Company, which is six days after the chart is announced).

Two hundred and ten singles were in the top ten in 2004. Ten singles from 2003 remained in the top 10 for several weeks at the beginning of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/charts/singles-chart/20031228/7501/|title=Official Singles Chart Top 100: 28 December 2003 - 03 January 2004|work=Official Chart|publisher=The Official Charts Company|accessdate=14 May 2017}}</ref> [[Hey Ya!]] by [[Outkast]] was the only single from 2003 to reach its peak in 2004.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/cbbcnews/hi/music/newsid_3470000/3470833.stm |title=LMC Vs U2 still top singles chart |work=[[Newsround]] |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=8 February 2004 |access-date=29 November 2016}}</ref> Sixty-five artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2004.  [[Dizzee Rascal]], [[Kanye West]], [[Kasabian (band)|Kasabian]], [[Maroon 5]] and [[Snow Patrol]] were among the many artists who achieved their first UK charting top 10 single in 2004.

The 2003 [[List of Christmas number one singles (UK)|Christmas number-one]], "[[Mad World]]" by [[Michael Andrews (musician)|Michael Andrews]] featuring [[Gary Jules]] (which was recorded for the 2001 film ''[[Donnie Darko]]''), remained at number-one for the first week of 2004.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3352667.stm|date=28 December 2004 |title=Gary Jules remains at number one|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> The first new number-one single of the year was "[[All This Time (Michelle McManus song)|All This Time]]" by ''[[Pop Idol (series 2)|2003 Pop Idol]]'' winner [[Michelle McManus]]. The release sent McManus into the [[Guinness Book of Records]] as the first [[Scotland|Scottish]] female to reach the top of the charts with their debut single.<ref name = "McManus">{{cite news |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3387601.stm |title=Pop Idol's Michelle tops charts |work=[[BBC News]] |publisher=[[BBC|British Broadcasting Corporation]] |date=11 January 2004 |access-date=20 January 2010}}</ref> Overall, thirty different singles peaked at number-one in 2004, with [[Busted (band)|Busted]] (3) having the most singles (including their appearance on [[Band Aid 20]]'s "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]") hit that position.<ref name = "David">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3511687.stm|title=Brit stars Busted grab number one|date=22 February 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref><ref name ="Thunderbirds">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3526424.stm|title=Busted rocket Thunderbirds to top|date=1 August 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4095227.stm|title=Band Aid takes festive top spot|date=19 December 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref>

==Background==
===Multiple entries===
Two hundred and ten singles charted in the top 10 in 2004, with 200 singles reaching their peak this year (including the re-entry "[[Mysterious Girl]]" which charted in previous years but reached a peak on its latest chart run).

Sixty-six artists scored multiple entries in the top 10 in 2004. [[Pete Doherty]] had the most top 10 entries in 2004 with five. This included one solo hit ("[[For Lovers]]),<ref name ="Swear">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3637485.stm|title=Swear word song hits number one|date=18 April 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> one entry with [[Babyshambles]] ("[[Killamangiro]]"),<ref name = "Doherty">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/programmes/newsnight/4106249.stm|title=Pete Doherty: Cool icon, hard drugs|last=Wark|first=Kirsty|date=21 December 2004|access-date=14 August 2017}}</ref> and three entries with [[The Libertines]] ("[[Can't Stand Me Now]],<ref name = "Stand">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3567900.stm|title=3 Of A Kind top the singles chart|date=15 August 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=16 May 2017}}</ref> "[[What Became of the Likely Lads]]"<ref name = "Likely">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3970097.stm|title=Rapper knocks DJ off top spot|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> and "[[Born in England]]" as part of [[Radio X (United Kingdom)|Twisted X]], supporting [[England national football team|England]] ahead of [[UEFA Euro 2004]]).<ref name = "Twisted X">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3803339.stm|title=Trio of football songs in top 10|date=13 June 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref>

Thirteen artists recorded four or more top 10 singles across the course of 2004. Of these, seven artists totals included an appearance on the Band Aid 20 charity single, which reached number-one. Two of [[Busted (band)|Busted]]'s other three singles in 2004 also peaked at the top spot, with "[[Who's David]]"<ref name = "David" /> and "[[Thunderbirds / 3AM|"Thunderbirds"/"3AM"]]"<ref name = "Thunderbirds" /> both landing the coveted position. "[[Air Hostess (song)|Air Hostess]]" just missed out, reaching number-two in May.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3678761.stm|title=Eamon fends off Busted challenge|date=2 May 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=16 May 2017}}</ref> [[Jamelia]]'s biggest solo hit of the year was "[[Thank You (Jamelia song)|Thank You]]" which lost out on number-one to [[Peter Andre]]'s "[[Mysterious Girl]]" in March.<ref name = "Mysterious">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3520569.stm|title=Andre comeback single tops chart|date=29 February 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> She scored two further top tens with "[[See It In A Boy's Eyes]]" (5) in July and double-A side "[[DJ (Jamelia song)|DJ]]"/"[[Stop (Sam Brown song)#Jamelia version|Stop]]" (9) in November.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3705714.stm|title=Jamelia's return to the top|date=11 February 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref>

[[Natasha Bedingfield]] began to catch up with her brother [[Daniel Bedingfield|Daniel's]] chart success with three top ten entries on top of the Band Aid hit. Her debut song "[[Single (Natasha Bedingfield song)|Single]]" peaked at number three in May,<ref name = "Eamon Four">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3705714.stm|title=Eamon spends fourth week on top|date=9 May 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> "[[These Words]]" topped the charts in August,<ref name = "Words">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3589000.stm|title=Bedingfield takes number one slot|date=22 August 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> and she finished off her year with number 6 single "[[Unwritten (song)|Unwritten]]" in December.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/number-1-today-in-2004-natasha-bedingfield-these-words__7721/?Day=&Month=01&Year=1976|title=Number 1 today in 2004: Natasha Bedingfield - These Words|last=Myers|first=Justin|date=29 August 2014|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=21 May 2017}}</ref> [[Tom Chaplin]] and [[Tim Rice-Oxley]] had a breakout year with their band [[Keane (band)|Keane]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4066303.stm|title=Keane's sound is 2004 hit|date=30 December 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> Their iconic song "[[Somewhere Only We Know]]" started things off, reaching a high of number-three in February.<ref name = "David" /> Three months later, "[[Everybody's Changing]] landed one spot lower at number-four in the chart.<ref name = "Eamon Four" /> In August, "[[Bedshaped]]" capped off their year entering at number-ten before they both featured on "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]".<ref name = "Words" />

The winner of the first series of [[Pop Idol]], [[Will Young]]'s "[[Leave Right Now]]" carried on its chart run from 2003, having peaked at number-one in December.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3316687.stm|date=14 December 2003|title=Osbourne's duet is new number one|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> His first new entry of the year, "[[Your Game]]", reached number-three in March.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3555629.stm|title=George Michael tops album charts|date=21 March 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Friday's Child (Will Young song)|Friday's Child]]" took fourth position in the chart in July.<ref name = "Stevens">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3905145.stm|title=Rachel Stevens beaten to top spot|date=18 July 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> Girl group [[Sugababes]] had three additional top 10 singles in 2004, including "[[Too Lost in You]]" from the end of the previous year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3335473.stm|title=Gary Jules tops Christmas charts|date=21 December 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Caught in a Moment]]" and "[[In the Middle (Sugababes song)|In the Middle]]" both reached number eight.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4548326.stm|title=Colourful past of pop's Sugababes|date=21 December 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref>

Six further acts reached the top 10 with four entries this year. [[Andre 3000]] of the American group [[OutKast]] had three hits with his band and one guest spot. "[[Hey Ya!]]", first released in 2003, finally peaked at number three in February.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/cbbcnews/hi/music/newsid_3470000/3470833.stm |title=LMC Vs U2 still top singles chart |work=[[Newsround]] |publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation |date=8 February 2004 |access-date=18 February 2011}}</ref> "[[The Way You Move]]" reached number 7 in April<ref name = "Usher">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3577243.stm|title=Chart double for R&B star Usher|date=28 March 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> and "[[Roses (Outkast song)|Roses]]" got to number four in July.<ref name = "Roses">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3844847.stm|title=McFly make singles chart summit|date=27 June 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> He also collaborated with [[Kelis]] on the number three hit "[[Millionaire (Kelis song)|Millionaire]]" in October. Kelis herself had two solo hits in 2004, as well as featuring on [[Enrique Iglesias]] single "[[Not in Love (Enrique Iglesias song)|Not In Love]]", which peaked at number five.<ref name = "DJ Casper">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3511022.stm|title=DJ Casper tops UK singles chart|date=14 March 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Milkshake (Kelis song)|Milkshake]]" took second spot behind "[[All This Time (Michelle McManus song)|All This Time]]" by [[Michelle McManus]] in January<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3428577.stm|title=Michelle holds out at number one|date=25 January 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> and "[[Trick Me]]" reached the same position in June.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3766823.stm|title=Ex-lovers still dominate chart|date=1 June 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref>

[[Duncan James]] from the band [[Blue (English band)|Blue]] reached number two with his debut solo single "[[I Believe My Heart]]" (featuring [[Keedie]])<ref name = "Likely" /> and had three top 10 hits with the band: "[[Breathe Easy]]",<ref name = "Usher" /> "[[Bubblin' (Blue song)|Bubblin']] and "[[Curtain Falls]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4011693.stm|title=U2 shoot to number one in charts|date=14 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> [[McFly]]'s first single, "[[5 Colours in Her Hair]]" reached number-one,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3599255.stm|title=Teen band McFly top singles chart|date=4 April 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> while follow-ups "[[Obviously (song)|Obviously]]",<ref name = "Roses" /> "[[That Girl (McFly song)|That Girl]]",<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3650272.stm|title=Ex-Westlife star storms to No 1|date=12 September 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Room on the 3rd Floor (song)|Room on the 3rd Floor]]" all made the top 10.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4030705.stm|title=Girls Aloud seize number one slot|date=21 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref>

[[Morrissey]] scored four hit singles in 2004: "[[Irish Blood, English Heart]],<ref name = "Frankee">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3719673.stm|title='Response song' is UK number one|date=16 May 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> "[[First of the Gang to Die]]", "[[Let Me Kiss You]]", "[[I Have Forgiven Jesus]]". [[The Libertines]] had two hit singles in their own right, "Can't Stand Me Now"<ref name = "Stand" /> and "What Became of the Likely Lads",<ref name = "Likely" /> as well as the Twisted X song "Born in England".<ref name = "Twisted X" />

[[Ronan Keating]] narrowly missed out on a number-one in 2004, with three singles reaching number two: "[[She Believes in Me#Ronan Keating version|She Believes in Me]]",<ref name = "Sam and Mark">{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3490865.stm|title=Pop Idol stars in chart triumph|date=15 February 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> "[[I Hope You Dance#Ronan Keating version|I Hope You Dance]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3844847.stm|title=Joss Stone tops UK album charts|date=3 October 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> and "[[Father and Son (song)#Ronan Keating and Yusuf Islam|Father and Son]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4141883.stm|title=X Factor star ends Band Aid reign|date=2 January 2005|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> A fourth single, "[[Last Thing on My Mind (Ronan Keating song)|Last Thing on My Mind]]" peaked at number five.<ref name = "Eamon Four" />

[[Usher (entertainer)|Usher]] was one of a number of artists with three top-ten entries, including the number one singles "[[Yeah! (Usher song)|Yeah!]]"<ref name="Usher"/> and "[[Burn (Usher song)|Burn]]".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/3865449.stm|title=Usher's Burn fires its way to the top|date=4 July 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> [[Bono]], [[Justin Hawkins]], [[Lemar]], [[Rachel Stevens]] and [[Robbie Williams]] all featured on the Band Aid single.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/newsbeat-30052182|title=Band Aid 20: What exactly are they doing now?|date=14 November 2014|work=Radio 1 Newsbeat|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=21 May 2017}}</ref> [[Christina Aguilera]], [[Britney Spears]], [[Eminem]], [[Girls Aloud]], [[Kanye West]], [[Kylie Minogue]] and [[R. Kelly]] were among the artists who had multiple top 10 entries in 2004.

===Chart debuts===
Eighty-two artists achieved their first top 10 single in 2004, either as a lead or featured artist. This includes the charity group Band Aid 20 (made up of chart acts but charting together for the first time). Of these, eleven went on to record another hit single that year: [[2Play]], [[The 411]], [[Boogie Pimps]], [[Deepest Blue]], [[Dizzee Rascal]], [[Jay Sean]], [[Joss Stone]], [[Maroon 5]], [[Naila Boss]], [[Twista]] and [[V (band)|V]]. Four artists scored two more top 10 singles in 2004: [[Franz Ferdinand (band)|Franz Ferdinand]], [[Keane (band)|Keane]], [[Raghav]] and [[The Streets]]. [[McFly]] and [[Natasha Bedingfield]] both had three other entries in their breakthrough year.

;Notes
[[Michelle McManus|Michelle]] debuted at number-one with "[[All This Time (Michelle McManus song)|All This Time]]" in January, but her first chart credit was with her fellow ''[[Pop Idol]]'' [[Pop Idol (series 2)|series 2]] finalists on "[[Happy Xmas (War is Over)]]" at the end of 2003.<ref name = "McManus" /><ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/music/3211901.stm|title=Pop Idols release Lennon war song|date=24 October 2003|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> This also included [[Sam and Mark]], whose single "[[With a Little Help from My Friends]]"/"[[Measure of a Man (Sam and Mark song)|Measure of a Man]]" marked their debut as a duo.<ref name = "Sam and Mark" />

Twisted X was a supergroup created by the radio station [[Radio X (United Kingdom)|XFM]]. The song "Born in England" featured several artists who had previously appeared in the UK top 10 ([[Supergrass]] and [[The Libertines]]) as well as several debutants ([[James Nesbitt]], [[Bernard Butler]] and [[The Delays]]).<ref name = "Twisted X" /> [[Duncan James]] from [[Blue (English band)|Blue]] debuted with a solo single in 2004, "[[I Believe My Heart]]", which was recorded as a duet with [[Keedie]]. Another former boyband member - [[Brian McFadden]] of [[Westlife]] - reached number-one with his first solo single, "[[Real to Me (Brian McFadden song)|Real to Me]]", while a second song "[[Irish Son (song)|Irish Son]]" entered at number 6.

[[Band Aid 20]] was a new iteration of the original [[Band Aid (band)|Band Aid]] and [[Band Aid II]] supergroups, recording a new version of "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]" in aid of crisis in [[Darfur]], [[Sudan]]. [[Shaznay Lewis]], formerly of [[All Saints (band)|All Saints]], peaked at number 8 with her debut solo single "[[Never Felt Like This Before]]". She also performed on the Band Aid 20 single.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/programmes/breakfast/3873673.stm|title=Saintly Shaznay goes solo|date=8 July 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> [[Róisín Murphy]] had appeared in the chart before with Moloko but she featured on "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]" in her own right.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/8154515.stm|title=Roisin Murphy voices star cover|date=16 July 2009|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> [[Tom Hannon]] (of [[The Divine Comedy]]),<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-37115552|title=The Divine Comedy's Neil Hannon: I can't resist an earworm|date=30 August 2016|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|accessdate=14 May 2017}}</ref> [[Coldplay]]'s [[Chris Martin]], [[Tim Wheeler]] ([[Ash (band)|Ash]]) and [[Tim Rice-Oxley]] and [[Tom Chaplin]] (both from the band [[Keane (band)|Keane]]) had similarly charted with their groups before Band Aid 20.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4015337.stm|title=Band Aid 20's low key style|date=18 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref>

===Songs from films===
Original songs from various films entered the top 10 throughout the year. These included "[[Thunderbirds merchandise#Music|Thunderbirds]]" (from ''[[Thunderbirds (2004 film)|Thunderbirds]]''),<ref name = "Thunderbirds" /> "[[We Are (Ana Johnsson song)|We Are]]" (''[[Spider-Man 2]]''),<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.soundtrack.net/album/spider-man-2/|title=Spider-Man 2 Soundtrack|work=Soundtrack.net|accessdate=14 May 2017}}</ref> "[[Car Wash (song)|Car Wash]]" (''[[Shark Tale]]'')<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0307453/soundtrack|title=Shark Tale (2004)|publisher=IMDB|accessdate=14 May 2017}}</ref> and "[[Misunderstood (Robbie Williams song)|Misunderstood]]" (''[[Bridget Jones: The Edge of Reason (film)|Bridget Jones: The Edge of Reason]]'').<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4090401.stm|title=Band Aid retains number one spot|date=12 December 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref>

===Charity singles===
A number of singles recorded for charity reached the top 10 in the charts in 2004. The [[Sport Relief]] single was "[[Some Girls (Rachel Stevens song)|Some Girls]]" by former [[S Club 7]] member [[Rachel Stevens]], peaking at number two on 24 July 2004.<ref name = "Stevens" />

[[Girls Aloud]] recorded the [[Children in Need]] single for 2004, a cover of [[The Pretenders]] song "[[I'll Stand by You#Girls Aloud version|I'll Stand by You]]". It was their seventh successive top 10 single and reached number-one on 27 November 2004.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4030705.stm|title=Girls Aloud seize number one slot|date=21 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref>

A group of artists came together under the banner of [[Band Aid 20]] and released the single "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]" in aid of the [[Darfur]] region of [[Sudan]]. It was the third version of the song to hit the charts after [[Band Aid (band)|Band Aid]] and [[Band Aid II]] in the 1980s. The song featured artists including [[Bono]], [[Busted (band)|Busted]], [[Daniel Bedingfield]], [[Jamelia]] and [[Paul McCartney]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/pressoffice/pressreleases/stories/2004/11_november/11/band_aid.shtml|title=Initial and BBC ONE join forces for Band Aid 20|date=11 November 2004|work=BBC Press Office|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> It was the Christmas number-one single for 2004, topping the chart for four weeks from 11 December 2004.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4052621.stm|title=How Band Aid 20 came together|date=30 November 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4070073.stm|title=Band Aid 20 single storms to No 1|date=5 December 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref>

===Best-selling singles===
[[Band Aid 20]] had the best-selling single of the year with "[[Do They Know It's Christmas?]]". The song spent six weeks in the top 10 (including four weeks at number one), sold over 1.06 million copies and was certified platinum by the [[British Phonographic Institute|BPI]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4126843.stm|title=Band Aid 20 holds on to top slot|date=26 December 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> "[[F**k It (I Don't Want You Back)]]" by [[Eamon (singer)|Eamon]] came in second place, selling more than 552,000 copies and losing out by around 454,000 sales.<ref name = "Swear" /> [[DJ Casper]]'s "[[Cha Cha Slide]]",<ref name = "DJ Casper" /> "[[Call on Me (Eric Prydz song)|Call on Me]]"<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3671146.stm|title=Call on Me hits number one spot|date=19 September 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> from [[Eric Prydz]] and "[[Yeah! (Usher song)|Yeah!]]"<ref name = "Usher"/> by [[Usher (entertainer)|Usher]] featuring [[Lil Jon]] and [[Ludacris]] made up the top five. Singles by [[Michelle McManus]],<ref name ="McManus" /> [[Anastacia]],<ref name = "Usher" /> [[Peter Andre]] featuring [[Bubbler Ranx]],<ref name="Mysterious"/> [[Britney Spears]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3541221.stm|title=Britney beats Kylie to number one|date=8 March 2004|work=BBC News|publisher=British Broadcasting Corporation|access-date=14 May 2017}}</ref> and [[Frankee]] (a response song to Eamon called "[[F.U.R.B. (Fuck You Right Back)]]")<ref name = "Frankee" /> were also in the [[2004 in British music charts#Best-selling singles|top ten best-selling singles]] of the year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ukchartsplus.co.uk/ChartsPlusYE2004.pdf|title=The Official UK Singles Chart 2004: Top 200|work=Charts Plus|accessdate=14 May 2017}}</ref>

"Do They Know It's Christmas?" (6) was also ranked in the top 10 [[List of best-selling singles of the 2000s (decade) in the United Kingdom|best-selling singles of the decade]].

==Entries by artist==
[[File:Usher Cannes 2016 retusche.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Usher (musician)|Usher]] achieved three top 10 singles in 2004, including the number-one hits "[[Yeah! (Usher song)|Yeah!]]" and "[[Burn (Usher song)|Burn]]".]]
[[File:Britney HIAM Cleveland.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Britney Spears]] had three more hit singles, two of which went to number-one - "[[Toxic (song)|Toxic]]" and "[[Everytime]]". The third, a cover of [[Bobby Brown]]'s "[[My Prerogative]]", reached number three.]]
[[File:CUN2008 Oscar party Christina Milian.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Christina Milian]] (pictured in 2008) had one solo entry in 2004, the number two-peaking "[[Dip It Low]]", as well as "[[Whatever U Want]]" featuring "[[Joe Budden]]", a number 9 hit.]]
[[File:PeteDoherty-Madrid2008.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Pete Doherty]] had five top 10 singles this year. One of these was as a featured artist on [[Peter Wolfe (musician)|Wolfman]]'s "[[For Lovers]]". He was also a member of two groups, [[Babyshambles]] and [[The Libertines]], with whom he had two top 10 hits each.]]
[[File:MorrisseySXSW2006.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Morrissey]] took four singles into the top region of the chart: "[[First of the Gang to Die]]", "[[I Have Forgiven Jesus]]", "[[Irish Blood, English Heart]]" and "[[Let Me Kiss You]]".]]
[[File:Anastacia1.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Anastacia]] scored two more hit singles in the UK in 2004, with "[[Left Outside Alone]]" peaked at number 3 and "[[Sick and Tired (Anastacia song)|Sick and Tired]]" one place lower.]]
[[File:AnaJohnssonatNRJPark.jpg|thumb|right|150px|[[Ana Johnsson]] recorded "[[We Are (Ana Johnsson song)|We Are]]" for the soundtrack to the film ''[[Spider-Man 2]]'' and the single went to number 8.]]
{{see also|List of UK Singles Chart number ones of the 2000s#2004}}

The figures include both main artists and featured artists, while appearances on ensemble charity records are also counted for each artist.
